NO SUS — PRIVACY POLICY

Last updated: 19 October 2026

1. What we collect

  • Account data: your email address, display name, and chosen avatar (a preset character or a photo you upload). New accounts sign in with Google only, so we receive the email address and name of the Google account you choose. Accounts created earlier with an email and password, GitHub or a phone number still work.
  • Content you upload: documents and notes you add to groups, stored in private storage protected by row-level security. Optional profile photos are stored in a public avatars bucket.
  • Activity records: to protect shared documents, we record events such as file uploads, views, membership changes, and screenshot attempts in a tamper-evident audit log visible to members of the same group.
  • Share-link views: when someone opens a document you shared by link, we record the email they enter and view timestamps, visible to you as the sender.
  • Feedback: messages you submit through Help & Feedback, with your app version.
  • Website visits: our website, nosus.foo, uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits. It sets no cookies and, according to Cloudflare, does not collect visitors' personal data; we see aggregate figures such as pages viewed, referring sites, browser and device types, countries, and page-load speed. It never loads on a link that carries a share key or sign-in data, and it is not part of the NO SUS app.
  • Website funnel events: nosus.foo also sends a few events to PostHog (hosted in the US) so we can see which pages lead people to the beta: a page was viewed, a burn note or file was created on the site (only whether it was a note or a file), and a link to the beta, Google Play, the APK download or the web app was clicked. Each event carries the page path without its query or fragment, the referring site's domain, any utm campaign tags in the address, and a random ID kept for that browser tab only, so one visit's steps can be linked but separate visits cannot. PostHog uses your IP address only to estimate an approximate location (such as country and city) and then discards it; the IP address is not stored. It sets no cookies, does not record the screen or keystrokes, and never includes note text, file names, links or keys. It does not run on /go, /to, personal address pages, or any link that carries a share key or sign-in data, and it is not part of the NO SUS app.
  • Abuse limits: the tools that need no account (burn notes and files, 6-digit codes, borrowed-screen sessions) count requests so a script can’t flood them. The counts hold only a keyed hash of your IP address and of a random number that stands for your copy of the tool: the Android app makes it once when it is installed and keeps it on the phone, and the website and the web app make a fresh one on each page load and keep it only in that page’s memory. That number is tied to no account and says nothing about your device; we never store it, only the keyed hash. A count is deleted a day after the last request it covers. A borrowed-screen session keeps the same kind of hash of the network that opened it until its record is deleted, a day after the session ends.
  • Device security signals: on sign-in and periodically during use, the app checks the device for indicators of tampering (root access, known instrumentation/hooking frameworks, active screen mirroring). Findings are written to a tamper-evident security ledger tied to your account — never sold or shared with third parties — and used only to flag your own account as higher-risk (e.g. requiring re-authentication) if serious tampering is detected.
  • App usage events: the app records a small, fixed set of events so we can see where people get stuck — for example: the app was opened; the welcome screen was viewed; a no-account tool was opened; someone reached a feature that needs an account; sign-up was started or completed, or sign-in completed; onboarding was started, skipped, or completed; creating or joining a group was started or completed; a first document was uploaded or viewed; or a notification permission prompt was shown, accepted, or declined. Each event carries the app version, the platform (such as web or Android), your account ID if you are signed in, and at most a short fixed label such as which tool or action it concerns. Events are recorded whether or not you are signed in — including when someone opens a shared link in the app — and carry no account ID when no one is signed in. They never include document or file names, group names, file contents, invite codes, or share-link keys. They are stored in our own Supabase database, readable only by NO SUS administrators, and are not sold or shared. There is currently no setting to turn them off.

2. Burn notes & burn files

Burn notes and burn files are encrypted on your device before upload, and we store the encrypted content. The key goes in the link itself, in the part after the #, which browsers do not send to any server. Our servers get a copy of a key only when a code is made:

  • In the app, the link you share carries the key and nothing else is sent: no copy of the key reaches us unless you tap “Use a code instead”.
  • On our website, a single note or file also gets a two-digit code. A copy of its key is sent to our servers so the recipient can open it with a link and those two digits, and the link the website gives you then does not carry the key (“Copy direct link (no code)” gives you the link with the key instead). The copy is kept until the code is used, stops working or expires (20 minutes by default, never longer than the note or file itself). Several files shared together on the website get no two-digit code.
  • “Use a code instead”, in the app or on the website, makes a 6-digit code that opens a note, a file or several files without a link. A copy of each key is kept until the code is used, stops working or expires (10 minutes at most, never longer than the note or files).

A copy of a key is deleted from our servers within ten minutes of its code being used or ending. A burn note is permanently deleted the moment it is read. A burn file can’t be opened a second time once it has been opened; its encrypted copy is kept for five more minutes, so the person who opened it can finish a download that broke, and is then deleted within a few minutes. Notes and files nobody opens are deleted after they expire. Burn notes and burn files require no NO SUS account on either end. We never know who received or opened one. If you send one while signed in to the app, we know which account sent it until it is opened or expires (see below); a guest's link is tied to no one.

Did they open it? So you can see whether a link you sent was opened, the app keeps a short list of the one-open links it sent on that device (never the link or its key), each with a random receipt code, and our server keeps a fingerprint of that code against the note or file. When the link is opened we record only that it was opened and when, never who opened it, their IP address or their device, and never what is inside. If you are signed in, our server knows which account sent which link, so it can tell you when it is opened: that tie is deleted at the first open or when the link expires, whichever comes first. Turn off “Link opened” in Settings → Notifications and links you send after that are not tied to your account. Guests are not tied to their links: a guest's receipt has no account in it, and the app asks whether a link was opened without sending any account. The receipt itself (with no account in it) is deleted a week after the link expires, when you clear the list, or, while it is still tied to you, with your account. Signing out clears the list on that device.

3. What we don't do

  • We do not sell your data.
  • We do not use third-party advertising or tracking SDKs. The website visit counter described above sets no cookies.
  • We do not read your documents for any purpose other than serving them back to you and your group.

4. Where data lives

Data is stored on Supabase (managed Postgres, storage, and authentication). Transport is encrypted with TLS. Access is enforced with row-level security so users can only reach rows their membership allows.

5. Data deletion

You can delete your account at any time from Profile → Danger Zone → Delete User Account. This permanently removes your profile, group memberships, uploaded files, and auth record. App usage events are not deleted: your account ID is removed from them, detaching them from your account, and the rest of each event is kept. See account deletion for details.

7. NO SUS Chat and Google Drive

Notes and files you keep in NO SUS Chat are written to a NO SUS folder in your own Google Drive. NO SUS asks Google only for the drive.file permission: it lets NO SUS see and change the files NO SUS itself created, and nothing else in your Drive. We do not store those files on our servers. Google can read them the same way it can read anything else in your Drive.

Your handle and your domain (your handle followed by .nosus.foo) are public. Opening your domain shows a QR code; nothing opens on that screen unless you scan it and approve on your phone. That screen does not receive your Google password or token; it gets only what you approve, for the session you approve, relayed through our server sealed between your phone and that screen. Anything you download or print on that screen may remain there. Ending the session stops further access. It does not delete copies already made.

8. Traceable copies and leak checks

When you send a note with traceable copies, our server stores each reader's copy encrypted, and the name each reader types (not verified) with the time they opened it. Reader names are deleted 7 days after the link expires. Each seal and each open is recorded on Solana devnet, a public test network, as a short signed record containing only hashes, never your text or reader names. When you check a leak, the comparison runs on your own device. If you pick a screenshot, the Android app reads its text on your device and does not upload the image.

9. PDFs you open in NO SUS

When you open a PDF in NO SUS from another app, it is read on your device. It is not uploaded unless you choose to send it, and then only through the way you pick in Send.

10. Receiving files from people without an account (ended)

Since version 1.7.0, NO SUS no longer takes files from people without an account. Files that were still waiting to be accepted are deleted within 24 hours of arrival, as before.
