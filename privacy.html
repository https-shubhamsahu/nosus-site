NO SUS — PRIVACY POLICY

Last updated: 8 November 2026

1. What we collect

  • Account data: your email address, display name, and chosen avatar (a preset character or a photo you upload). New accounts sign in with Google only, so we receive the email address and name of the Google account you choose. Accounts created earlier with an email and password, GitHub or a phone number still work.
  • Content you upload: documents and notes you add to groups, stored in private storage protected by row-level security. Optional profile photos are stored in a public avatars bucket.
  • Activity records: to protect shared documents, we record events such as file uploads, views, membership changes, and screenshot attempts in a tamper-evident audit log visible to members of the same group.
  • Share-link views: when someone opens a document you shared by link, we record the email they enter and view timestamps, visible to you as the sender.
  • Feedback: messages you submit through Help & Feedback, with your app version. If “Include phone details” is on (it is unless you turn it off), the message also carries your phone’s maker and model, Android version, memory size, and why NO SUS last stopped (for example a crash or low memory).
  • Crash reports: when the app or the web app hits an error, it sends our own Supabase database the error’s type name (such as “StateError”) and up to five places in NO SUS’s own code where it happened, with the app version, whether it was Android or the web, and the Android version number. On Android it also reports why NO SUS last stopped when that was a crash, a freeze (“not responding”) or the phone closing it for memory. A report never contains the error’s message, links, keys, notes, file names or contents, your account, or any device or install ID. Your IP address is used only for the keyed-hash counter described under “Abuse limits” and is not stored with the report. Identical reports are counted together per day and deleted after 30 days. Turn them off in Settings → Privacy & security → Crash reports.
  • Website visits: our website, nosus.foo, uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits. It sets no cookies and, according to Cloudflare, does not collect visitors' personal data; we see aggregate figures such as pages viewed, referring sites, browser and device types, countries, and page-load speed. It never loads on a link that carries a share key or sign-in data, and it is not part of the NO SUS app.
  • Website funnel events: nosus.foo also sends a few events to PostHog (hosted in the US) so we can see which pages lead people to the beta: a page was viewed; a burn note or file was created on the site (only whether it was a note or a file, and whether a starter such as Aadhaar for printing, Wi-Fi password or Login was used, never which one); how a link was shared (the phone’s share sheet, WhatsApp or copy); that the site first saw a link you sent was opened, with roughly how long after it was made (under 10 minutes, 10 to 60 minutes, or over an hour); that the list of links you sent was shown (it shows only when this browser already holds links sent from it on an earlier visit); on the Scam ya Nahi? game (nosus.foo/play), that a game was started, finished (with the score out of 5, never which answers) or shared (the score or one practice card, by share sheet or copy); and a link to the beta, Google Play, the APK download or the web app was clicked. Each event carries the page path without its query or fragment, whether the screen is a touch screen (phone) or not (computer), the referring site's domain, any utm campaign tags in the address, and a random ID kept for that browser tab only, so one visit's steps can be linked but no ID links separate visits. Two events do say something about an earlier visit: the sent list being shown means links were sent from this browser before, and the rough time-to-open comes from when a link was made, which may be an earlier visit. Neither carries the link, a count or any ID from that visit. PostHog uses your IP address only to estimate an approximate location (such as country and city) and then discards it; the IP address is not stored. It sets no cookies, does not record the screen or keystrokes, and never includes note text, file names, links or keys. The game keeps your streak and today’s answers in your browser’s storage only; they are not sent. It does not run on /go, /to, personal address pages, or any link that carries a share key or sign-in data, and it is not part of the NO SUS app. The same events, with the same details, also go to Google Analytics (below).
  • Google Analytics: nosus.foo, these legal pages and the web app at app.nosus.foo use Google Analytics 4, a Google service, to count visits. It sets first-party cookies (_ga and _ga_ followed by an ID) holding a random ID for your browser, kept for up to two years, so it can tell new visitors from returning ones and link visits from the same browser. On nosus.foo it receives the address and title of each page you view (with any utm campaign tags), the referring site, your browser, operating system, device type, screen size and language, and an approximate location (country, region and city) that Google works out from your IP address; according to Google, Google Analytics does not log or store IP addresses. It can also record scrolling to the end of a page, clicks on links to other sites and file downloads, and it receives the website funnel events above. In the web app it receives one page view each time the app opens, with only the kind of page (for example a one-open note, an invite or a pairing link), never the link itself, its key, an ID or a code, plus any utm tags and only the domain of the referring site. Google Analytics never loads on /go, /to, personal address pages, or any link that carries a share key or sign-in data; automated browsers do not load it; and it is not in the Android app. Google processes this data on its servers, including in the United States; see How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. To stop it, block cookies and scripts for nosus.foo or use Google’s Analytics opt-out browser add-on; every NO SUS tool keeps working.
  • Ads on the blog: pages under nosus.foo/blog/ show ads from Google AdSense. The rest of nosus.foo, the web app and the Android app show no ads. Google and its partners use cookies and similar technologies, on nosus.foo and on their own domains (such as doubleclick.net), to choose, show and measure those ads, limit how often you see them and detect fraud. See section 12. A blog page never holds a note, a file or a share key.
  • Abuse limits: the tools that need no account (burn notes and files, 6-digit codes, borrowed-screen sessions) count requests so a script can’t flood them. The counts hold only a keyed hash of your IP address and of a random number that stands for your copy of the tool: the Android app makes it once when it is installed and keeps it on the phone, and the website and the web app make a fresh one on each page load and keep it only in that page’s memory. That number is tied to no account and says nothing about your device; we never store it, only the keyed hash. A count is deleted a day after the last request it covers. A borrowed-screen session keeps the same kind of hash of the network that opened it until its record is deleted, a day after the session ends.
  • Remembered screens: if you choose “Remember this screen”, that browser keeps a signing key that web pages can’t read out (someone with access to that computer’s files could copy it, so only use this on your own computer), and your phone keeps only a fingerprint of the key, the screen’s name (like “Chrome on Windows”) and when it was added and last used. Once a browser has a key, it shows the key to any phone that scans its code. They travel only inside the sealed session, so our servers can’t read them. On your own domain (yourname.nosus.foo), a remembered browser also asks your Android phone to open your chat: it sends the key’s fingerprint and its code to our server, which passes them on as a push through Google’s Firebase Cloud Messaging and keeps nothing. Your phone then opens your chat without a tap, without asking for your screen lock, and only for a browser it remembers; turn this off with “Open on linked computers without a tap”. A browser not used for 14 days is forgotten. Sign it out on your phone (Linked computers, on Open on any screen) or forget it on that page.
  • Device security signals: on sign-in and periodically during use, the app checks the device for indicators of tampering (root access, known instrumentation/hooking frameworks, active screen mirroring). Findings are written to a tamper-evident security ledger tied to your account — never sold or shared with third parties — and used only to flag your own account as higher-risk (e.g. requiring re-authentication) if serious tampering is detected.
  • App usage events: the app records a small, fixed set of events so we can see where people get stuck — for example: the app was opened; the welcome screen was viewed; a no-account tool was opened; someone reached a feature that needs an account; sign-up was started or completed, or sign-in completed; onboarding was started, skipped, or completed; creating or joining a group was started or completed; a first document was uploaded or viewed; or a notification permission prompt was shown, accepted, or declined. Each event carries the app version, the platform (such as web or Android), your account ID if you are signed in, and at most a short fixed label such as which tool or action it concerns. Events are recorded whether or not you are signed in — including when someone opens a shared link in the app — and carry no account ID when no one is signed in. They never include document or file names, group names, file contents, invite codes, or share-link keys. They are stored in our own Supabase database, readable only by NO SUS administrators, and are not sold or shared. There is currently no setting to turn them off.

2. Burn notes & burn files

Burn notes and burn files are encrypted on your device before upload, and we store the encrypted content. The key goes in the link itself, in the part after the #, which browsers do not send to any server. Our servers get a copy of a key only when a code is made:

  • In the app, the link you share carries the key and nothing else is sent: no copy of the key reaches us unless you tap “Use a code instead”.
  • On our website, the link works the same way: the key is in the part after the #, which browsers do not send to our server. A copy of the key goes to our server only when you ask for a 6-digit code (“Use a code instead”, under More), and it is kept until up to 10 minutes after the code is used or expires.
  • “Use a code instead”, in the app or on the website, makes a 6-digit code that opens a note, a file or several files without a link. A code is valid for 10 minutes at most, never longer than the note or files, and a copy of each key is kept until up to 10 minutes after the code is used, stops working or expires.

A copy of a key is deleted from our servers within ten minutes of its code being used or ending. A burn note is permanently deleted the moment it is read. A burn file can’t be opened a second time once it has been opened; its encrypted copy is kept for five more minutes, so the person who opened it can finish a download that broke, and is then deleted within a few minutes. Notes and files nobody opens are deleted after they expire. Burn notes and burn files require no NO SUS account on either end. We never know who received or opened one. If you send one while signed in to the app, we know which account sent it until it is opened or expires (see below); a guest's link is tied to no one.

Did they open it? So you can see whether a link you sent was opened, the app, and the website’s tool, keep a short list of the one-open links sent from that device or browser (never the link or its key), each with a random receipt code, and our server keeps a fingerprint of that code against the note or file. When the link is opened we record only that it was opened and when, never who opened it, their IP address or their device, and never what is inside. If you are signed in, our server knows which account sent which link, so it can tell you when it is opened: that tie is deleted at the first open or when the link expires, whichever comes first. Turn off “Link opened” in Settings → Notifications and links you send after that are not tied to your account. Guests are not tied to their links: a guest's receipt has no account in it, and the app asks whether a link was opened without sending any account. The receipt itself (with no account in it) is deleted a week after the link expires, when you clear the list, or, while it is still tied to you, with your account. Signing out clears the list on that device. On our website, the list lives only in that browser (its local storage) and never reaches our server; “Clear” removes it and its receipts. The website’s receipts never carry an account, and the website asks whether a link was opened without sending any account.

3. What we don't do

  • We do not sell your data.
  • The NO SUS app, on Android and on the web, shows no ads and has no advertising SDK, and neither do the note, file and other tool pages on nosus.foo; we do not build advertising profiles ourselves. Google ads appear only on the nosus.foo blog (section 12). Google ML Kit sends SDK usage and performance metrics to Google as described below. Cloudflare Web Analytics sets no cookies; Google Analytics and Google’s ads do.
  • We do not read your documents for any purpose other than serving them back to you and your group.

4. Where data lives

Data is stored on Supabase (managed Postgres, storage, and authentication). Transport is encrypted with TLS. Access is enforced with row-level security so users can only reach rows their membership allows.

5. Data deletion

You can delete your account at any time by tapping your avatar, then Settings → Danger zone → Delete account. This permanently removes your profile, group memberships, files you uploaded to NO SUS storage, and auth record. Your domain (your handle followed by .nosus.foo) stops working, and the name is held for 30 days, linked to no account, before anyone can claim it; the hold keeps only the name and the time it was released. NO SUS disconnects Google Drive on this device; notes and files in your own Drive are not deleted. You can delete those in Google Drive. App usage events are not deleted: your account ID is removed from them, detaching them from your account, and the rest of each event is kept. See account deletion for details.

7. NO SUS Chat and Google Drive

Notes and files you keep in NO SUS Chat stay on your phone, encrypted in the app’s own storage with a key kept in the phone’s secure storage, and we do not store them. Kept on the phone, the chat is its only copy: removing the app removes it. If you connect Google Drive (on a computer the chat always uses it), they are written to a NO SUS folder in your own Google Drive instead. NO SUS asks Google only for the drive.file permission: it lets NO SUS see and change the files NO SUS itself created, and nothing else in your Drive. We do not store those files on our servers. Google can read them the same way it can read anything else in your Drive.

Your handle and your domain (your handle followed by .nosus.foo) are public. Opening your domain shows a QR code; nothing opens on that screen unless you scan it and approve on your phone. That screen does not receive your Google password or token; it gets only what you approve, for the session you approve, relayed through our server sealed between your phone and that screen. If you allow it, that screen can also add notes and files to your chat: they reach your phone the same sealed way, and your phone saves them to your chat, on the phone or in your Drive. A session lasts no longer than the time you pick (at most 8 hours) and ends early when nobody uses it. Anything you download or print on that screen may remain there. Ending the session stops further access. It does not delete copies already made.

Your phone and that screen may also try to connect directly to send files. To find a path, each side asks public STUN servers run by Cloudflare (stun.cloudflare.com) and Google (stun.l.google.com) for the address its network shows the internet. Those servers see the public IP address of each device. They see no session, note or file. When a direct path is blocked, which is common on mobile data in India, the connection may go through a Cloudflare TURN relay. A relay sees both devices’ public IP addresses and ports, when the connection is open and how much data passes through it. It can’t read files, file names or notes, because the connection is encrypted between your phone and that screen with keys checked inside the sealed session. If neither a direct path nor a relay works, files go through our server, sealed as above.

8. Traceable copies and leak checks

When you send a note with traceable copies, our server stores each reader's copy encrypted, and the name each reader types (not verified) with the time they opened it. Reader names are deleted 7 days after the link expires. Each seal and each open is recorded on Solana devnet, a public test network, as a short signed record containing only hashes, never your text or reader names. When you check a leak, the comparison runs on your own device. If you pick a screenshot, the Android app uses Google ML Kit to read its text on your device and does not upload the image or recognized text to Google. ML Kit also supports QR scanning. Its Android SDKs send device and app information, per-installation identifiers, feature usage, performance metrics and error codes to Google over HTTPS for diagnostics and usage analytics. Google uses those metrics to operate and improve ML Kit. See ML Kit privacy information and data disclosures.

9. PDFs you open in NO SUS

When you open a PDF in NO SUS from another app, it is read on your device. It is not uploaded unless you choose to send it, and then only through the way you pick in Send.

10. Receiving files from people without an account (ended)

Since version 1.7.0, NO SUS no longer takes files from people without an account. Files that were still waiting to be accepted are deleted within 24 hours of arrival, as before.
