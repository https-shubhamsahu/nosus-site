Send a self-destructing note that opens once

NO SUS (nosus.foo) makes a self-destructing note in your browser: type up to 50,000 characters and share the link. The note is encrypted with AES-256 before upload, opens once, and is then deleted from the server. Unread notes expire after 7 days. Neither you nor the reader needs an account.

Try it · Encrypted in your browser

0/50,000

How it works in four steps

  1. 1

    Step 1: Write the note

    Open the Note tab above and type or paste up to 50,000 characters.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Create the link

    Tap Create a share link; the note is encrypted in your browser before anything is uploaded.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Share the link

    Tap Share and pick the chat, or Copy link. The key stays in the part of the link after the #.

  4. 4

    Step 4: They read it once

    The reader taps Open note, and after that the link stops working for everyone.

Self-destructing note online, with no app

It works in any browser at nosus.foo, on a phone or a computer. The reader needs only the link and a browser: no app, no account, no sign-up.

Is there a self-destructing note app?

The NO SUS Android app has the same switch: in the Send tab, turn on One open. It is in closed testing on Google Play, and there is no iPhone app, so on an iPhone use the website.

Before you trust a self-destructing notepad site

Any one-time note site, this one included, should pass these checks:

  • The note is encrypted in your browser before upload, and the key travels in the link after the #, which browsers do not send to a server.
  • It tells you when the server can hold a key. Here that is only while a 6-digit code you asked for is valid, and up to 10 minutes after.
  • You typed the address yourself. Lookalike copies of Privnote were caught swapping the crypto wallet addresses inside notes.
  • It says plainly that the reader can still copy or photograph the note.

Honest limits

  • The reader can still copy, screenshot or photograph the note; a browser can't block that.
  • If you ask for a 6-digit code, its key is held on our server too, until up to 10 minutes after the code is used or expires.
  • Whoever opens the link first uses it up, so send it only where your reader will see it.
  • Notes made on the website have no custom timer: an unread note expires after 7 days.

Questions

Can a self-destructing note be screenshotted?

Yes. Once the note is on the reader's screen they can copy, screenshot or photograph it. Self-destructing means the link works once and the stored copy is deleted, not that the reader can't keep what they saw.

How long does a self-destructing note last?

Until it is opened once. An unread NO SUS note expires after 7 days. A 6-digit code opens once and lasts up to 10 minutes.

Can NO SUS read my note?

The note is encrypted in your browser before upload, and the key stays in the link after the #, which browsers do not send. Only if you ask for a 6-digit code does our server hold the key, until up to 10 minutes after the code is used or expires, so a break-in during that window could expose it.

Can I send a self-destructing email?

Not as an email. Make the note, then paste its link into any email or chat. The note opens once and is deleted, but the email itself stays in both inboxes. A link preview or mail scanner won't use it up: the note is fetched only when the reader taps Open note.

Do I need an app or an account to send a self-destructing message?

No. It works in any browser on nosus.foo. Neither the sender nor the reader needs an account or an app, and it is free to use.

Is there a Privnote alternative that also sends files?

Yes. Privnote has run since 2008 and sends text. NO SUS sends text and up to 10 files, 50 MB combined. Privnote also has features NO SUS lacks, like custom passwords.
