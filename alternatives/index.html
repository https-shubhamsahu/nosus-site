Secure sharing alternatives

Pick by what you are sending. A note that opens once, a password for a developer, a big file, a tracked deck for investors and a leak to trace each suit a different tool. NO SUS covers one-time notes and files, watermarked links and traced copies. Each page below compares NO SUS with one tool and lists its limits.

Tool by tool

  • Onetime Secret alternativeOnetime Secret and NO SUS (nosus.foo) both make a link that shows a note once, with no account needed. Onetime Secret is open source, self-hostable, has an API and passphrases, and encrypts on its servers. NO SUS encrypts in your browser and sends files up to 50 MB, but is closed source and briefly holds the key too.
  • NO SUS vs PrivnotePrivnote and NO SUS (nosus.foo) both encrypt a note in your browser, need no account, and delete it after one read. Privnote has run since 2008 and adds timed notes, custom passwords and read alerts by email. NO SUS adds files (up to 10, 50 MB) and shows no ads, but briefly holds each note's key on its server.
  • Yopass alternativeYopass and NO SUS (nosus.foo) both encrypt in your browser and need no account. Yopass is open source, built to self-host, and has optional passphrases and a command-line client; its public demo is for testing. NO SUS is hosted with nothing to install and sends files up to 50 MB, but is closed source and briefly holds each key.
  • Password Pusher alternativePassword Pusher (pwpush.com) is the better pick if you need view limits, a passphrase, an audit log, an API or self-hosting. NO SUS (nosus.foo) is simpler: no account, a one-time note or up to 10 files (50 MB combined), encrypted in your browser. Password Pusher encrypts on its server, not in your browser.
  • Bitwarden Send alternativeBitwarden Send needs a Bitwarden account to create a link, though recipients need none. Free accounts send text up to 1,000 encrypted characters; files need Premium, at $19.80 a year. NO SUS (nosus.foo) needs no account on either side and sends a note up to 50,000 characters or up to 10 files, free.
  • PrivateBin alternativePrivateBin is free, open source pastebin software with burn-after-reading and optional passwords, which you run yourself or use on a server someone else runs. NO SUS (nosus.foo) is hosted for you: nothing to install, no account, a one-time note or up to 10 files. PrivateBin keeps the key in the link only.
  • NO SUS vs WeTransferWeTransfer is the better pick for big transfers: Free allows 3 GB and 10 transfers per 30 days, and Ultimate goes up to 1 TB. NO SUS (nosus.foo) Burn Files is for small sensitive files: up to 10 files, 50 MB combined, one link that opens once, encrypted in your browser, no accounts.
  • NO SUS vs DocSendDocSend (by Dropbox) is built for business deals and data rooms: paid plans start at $30 per user a month billed yearly, and watermarks need the Advanced plan or above. NO SUS (nosus.foo) watermarked links cover the basics for one person: viewer-email watermark, view log, 30-day links and revoke, free to use today. It isn't end-to-end encrypted.
  • NO SUS vs DigifyDigify is a document-security platform with DRM, data rooms and page-by-page analytics; its Pro plan is listed at $140 a month, and dynamic watermark is a $50 a month add-on. NO SUS (nosus.foo) watermarked links give one person a viewer-email watermark, view log and revoke, free to use today. They aren't end-to-end encrypted.
  • NO SUS vs PapermarkPapermark is an open-source document-sharing tool you can self-host, with a free plan of 50 documents and 50 links; its watermark needs the Data Rooms plan. NO SUS (nosus.foo) watermarked links give one person a viewer-email watermark, view log and revoke, free to use today. They aren't end-to-end encrypted.
