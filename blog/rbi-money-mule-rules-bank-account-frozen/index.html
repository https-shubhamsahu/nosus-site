The short answer

The RBI's 11 September 2026 draft rules would let banks put a temporary debit hold on flagged transactions of Rs 1,000 or more, but they must tell you why, give you 20 days to explain, and lift the hold within 60 days unless police or another authority directs otherwise. It is still a draft, proposed to apply from 1 April 2027, so these deadlines do not yet bind banks. The surest protection is never letting anyone else use your account, UPI ID or SIM.

On 11 September 2026 the RBI released the draft Reserve Bank of India (Know Your Customer) Amendment Directions, 2026 for public comment. It follows a Supreme Court direction of 4 August 2026 asking for a standard procedure on temporary debit holds linked to mule accounts and cyber fraud.

What did the RBI propose on 11 September 2026?

A single procedure for what banks do when they suspect an account is being used as a money mule. The draft text defines a mule account as one used, "knowingly or unknowingly", to receive, layer or pass on cyber-fraud money for someone else. That second word matters: your account can be flagged even if you never meant to help anyone.

The key points:

  • What gets flagged: a transaction of Rs 1,000 or more that the bank's own monitoring systems, which may include AI/ML tools, mark as unusual for your declared profile or linked to an account already reported as mule or fraudulent.
  • Hold the amount, not the account: for a flagged transaction, the hold covers that amount. Banks' policies must treat holding an entire account as a last resort, for exceptional cases only.
  • Who it covers: commercial banks including small finance, payments, regional rural and local area banks, plus urban co-operative banks. Nodal, pool, escrow and similar special-purpose accounts are excluded.
  • Accountability: banks must name nodal officers and show their contact details on the website and in every branch, resolve complaints within 30 days, and keep hold records for at least 5 years (10 years after an account is closed).

Comments closed on 2 October 2026, and the RBI says it will issue final directions separately for each type of bank. The draft proposes they take effect on 1 April 2027, with banks free to adopt them earlier (FCC Times, Free Press Journal). Until final directions are in force, none of these deadlines bind your bank.

How long can a bank hold my money under the draft rules?

At most 60 days from the day the hold starts, unless police or another competent authority tells the bank to continue. The draft sets this timeline:

Why do student bank accounts end up frozen?

Because mule networks recruit them. This year's police cases show the pattern:

  • Jaipur, July 2026: two men aged 19 and 20 were arrested for allegedly approaching students at tea stalls, cafes, colleges and coaching centres with commission offers to rent out their accounts. Fraud money was pushed through those accounts and withdrawn at ATMs. Police warned that knowingly lending an account, even passively, can lead to prosecution at any age (The420.in).
  • Maharashtra, reported August 2026: Operation 1930 froze 2,717 mule accounts linked to more than Rs 159 crore. Syndicates recruited unemployed youth and students on social media, paid monthly or one-off commissions, then took the net banking logins, debit cards and linked SIMs (The420.in).
  • Hyderabad, October 2026: two men arrested on 8 October allegedly collected 60 to 70 accounts, with passbooks, cards, logins and SIMs, from students, delivery workers and locals for commission. Telangana's Cyber Security Bureau separately arrested 101 people in October, 83 of them alleged account holders (The420.in).

In every case the hook was easy money: a commission or monthly payout for opening or lending an account. Once your account sits in that chain, it is your name the bank's system flags, not the recruiter's.

What critics say the draft misses

Dvara Research, in comments published on 5 October, backs the push against arbitrary freezes but warns that a fixed Rs 1,000 line may teach mule networks to keep transfers just below it. It also asks for compensation when a bank breaks the procedure and harms a customer, noting that High Courts have already given relief where freezes were found disproportionate.

How to avoid a frozen account, and what to do if it happens

  1. Never rent out or lend your account, UPI ID or SIM to anyone, including friends and online "employers". No genuine job needs your net banking login, debit card or a SIM in your name.
  2. Don't pass on money you don't recognise. If a stranger's credit lands in your account and someone calls asking you to refund or forward it, don't. SBI's October 2026 advisory says to verify unexpected deposits through official bank channels instead (The420.in). Tell your bank in writing. You can check the sender's number or account in the Suspect Repository on cybercrime.gov.in.
  3. If a hold or freeze hits, reply in writing. Ask the bank for the reason and any complaint or NCRP reference number. Send invoices, chats or salary slips showing where the money came from, within 20 days if your bank follows the new procedure. Keep copies.
  4. Escalate in order. Bank grievance desk, then the nodal officer listed on its website, then the RBI's complaint portal at cms.rbi.org.in, which the RBI's complaints page points to for complaints against banks. The RBI contact centre is 14448. If you have lost money to fraud, the government's Chakshu page says to report it on 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in.
  5. Check what's registered in your name. Use "Know Mobile Connections in Your Name" on Sanchar Saathi and mark any number that isn't yours. One person can hold at most 9 connections across operators (6 in J&K, Assam and the North East). New SIM rules notified on 21 August 2026 also require biometric identification to get, change or disconnect a connection, with operators given three months, extendable, to set it up (MediaNama). Lock your biometrics in the Aadhaar app, unlock them only when you need a biometric check yourself, and review your authentication history there.
  6. Share KYC copies narrowly. Before sending an Aadhaar or PAN copy, write the recipient and purpose across it, for example "For XYZ internship KYC only, 12 Oct 2026". Our guides on sharing an Aadhaar copy safely and watermarking a PDF show how.

Questions people ask

Can my bank freeze my account without telling me why?

Under the RBI's draft rules, no. A bank placing a temporary debit hold must give you the reason, the process to get it removed and the contact of the officer handling it, immediately by SMS or email if those are on record, or in writing by the end of the next day. The draft is proposed to take effect on 1 April 2027, though banks may adopt it earlier.

How long can a bank keep a debit hold on a suspected mule account?

The draft caps it at 60 days from the date the hold was placed. After that the hold must be removed unless police, a court or another competent authority directs the bank to keep it.

Is it a crime to let someone use my bank account for a commission?

Police treat it as taking part in the fraud. In 2026 cases in Jaipur and Hyderabad, recruiters and people who handed over their accounts were booked under the IT Act and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, and Jaipur police warned that knowingly letting your account be used, even passively, can lead to prosecution.

A stranger sent money to my UPI and wants it back. What should I do?

Do not send it back or forward it yourself. Tell your bank in writing that you received a credit you do not recognise and let the bank handle the reversal. Report a pushy or threatening caller on Chakshu in the Sanchar Saathi portal, and call 1930 or use cybercrime.gov.in if you have lost money.

Where do I complain if my bank will not lift a hold?

Write to the bank's grievance channel and its nodal officer first; the draft requires banks to resolve such complaints within 30 days. If that fails, file against the bank on the RBI's complaint portal at cms.rbi.org.in or call the RBI contact centre on 14448.
