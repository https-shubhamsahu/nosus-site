2b:T217c,<p>Your SSN never expires, so don&#39;t send it anywhere it can sit for years. That rules out the two ways most people send it: a normal email and a text message. Here is what to do instead, and what to do if it&#39;s already out.</p>
<h2 id="is-it-safe-to-send-your-ssn-over-email">Is it safe to send your SSN over email?</h2>
<p>No, not in a normal email. The IRS says plainly that <a href="https://www.irs.gov/help/sending-and-receiving-emails-securely" rel="noopener" target="_blank">&quot;standard email is not encrypted&quot;</a>, and the FTC tells businesses that regular email <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business" rel="noopener" target="_blank">&quot;is not a secure method for sending sensitive data&quot;</a> and to caution staff against emailing Social Security numbers. Gmail encrypts mail while it travels when the other provider supports it (<a href="https://support.google.com/transparencyreport/answer/7381230" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TLS</a>), but Google notes that TLS does not protect email while it is stored on a server. Your number then sits in your Sent folder and in their inbox, in every backup, for as long as nobody deletes it.</p>
<p>If you must use email, keep the number out of the subject line (the IRS points out that even encrypted email doesn&#39;t encrypt the subject) and out of the body. Put a one-time link in the email instead.</p>
<h2 id="is-it-safe-to-text-your-ssn">Is it safe to text your SSN?</h2>
<p>Not by SMS. Apple and Google both say SMS and MMS messages are <a href="https://support.apple.com/en-us/104972" rel="noopener" target="_blank">not end-to-end encrypted</a>. iMessage is, and so are <a href="https://support.google.com/messages/answer/10262381" rel="noopener" target="_blank">RCS chats in Google Messages</a> when both people use Google Messages with RCS chats on and a lock shows. Even then, the number stays on both phones and in their backups, so ask the other person to delete it once they&#39;ve copied it, and delete your own copy.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-the-best-way-to-send-a-social-security-number">What is the best way to send a Social Security number?</h2>
<p>From best to acceptable:</p>
<div class="table-scroll" role="region" aria-label="Table" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Way to send it</th><th scope="col">Where your SSN ends up</th><th scope="col">Use it when</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th scope="row">The requester&#39;s secure portal</th><td>Only in the system that needs it</td><td>An employer, bank, lender or tax preparer has one</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">A phone call you place</th><td>Not in any message</td><td>You looked the number up yourself, not from the message asking</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">In person</th><td>Not in any message</td><td>It&#39;s a local office, bank branch or HR desk</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">A link that opens once</th><td>On the recipient&#39;s screen once, then the link stops working</td><td>Nothing above is possible and they need it in writing</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">End-to-end encrypted chat (iMessage, RCS with a lock)</th><td>Both phones, until deleted</td><td>You know the person and will both delete it</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Plain email or SMS</th><td>Both inboxes or phones, plus backups</td><td>Never</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>For the IRS itself, use your <a href="https://www.irs.gov/privacy-disclosure/irs-privacy-guidance-about-email-contact" rel="noopener" target="_blank">IRS online account</a>, or the <a href="https://www.irs.gov/help/irs-document-upload-tool" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Document Upload Tool</a> when a notice or letter asks for documents, not plain email.</p>
<h2 id="who-actually-needs-your-ssn">Who actually needs your SSN?</h2>
<p>Before you send it anywhere, do what SSA suggests: ask <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/advocates/2021-11-17.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">why the number is needed, how it will be used, and what happens if you don&#39;t provide it</a>. A real employer, bank or tax preparer will have an answer. The SSA&#39;s inspector general adds one simple rule: <a href="https://oig.ssa.gov/scam-alerts/2026-08-31-protect-your-social-security-number/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">don&#39;t share your SSN with anyone who contacts you first</a>, however routine the request sounds.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-spot-an-ssn-scam">How to spot an SSN scam</h2>
<ul>
<li>Social Security <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/scam/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">will never ask for sensitive information</a> through social media, email or text message.</li>
<li>Real SSA staff never threaten to suspend your SSN, and never demand gift cards, crypto, wire transfers or cash (<a href="https://oig.ssa.gov/scam-alerts/2026-08-31-protect-your-social-security-number/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SSA OIG</a>).</li>
<li>The IRS <a href="https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/ways-to-tell-if-the-irs-is-reaching-out-or-if-its-a-scammer" rel="noopener" target="_blank">doesn&#39;t make first contact by email or social media</a>. A letter or notice comes first.</li>
<li>The IRS will never ask for your <a href="https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Identity Protection PIN</a>. Calls, emails or texts asking for it are scams.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="already-sent-your-ssn-by-email-or-text-what-to-do-now">Already sent your SSN by email or text? What to do now</h2>
<ol data-checklist>
<li>Ask the recipient to delete the message, then delete yours from Sent and Trash.</li>
<li>Freeze your credit at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. The FTC says a freeze <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/credit-freezes-and-fraud-alerts" rel="noopener" target="_blank">costs nothing to place or lift, doesn&#39;t affect your score</a>, and lasts until you lift it. You have to contact all three.</li>
<li>Or add a fraud alert instead: it lasts one year and can be renewed, and the bureau you contact tells the other two. An extended alert lasts seven years but needs an FTC identity theft report or a police report.</li>
<li>Get an IRS Identity Protection PIN. Anyone with an SSN or ITIN who can verify their identity is eligible, and a new PIN is generated each year (<a href="https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin" rel="noopener" target="_blank">IRS</a>).</li>
<li>With a myE-Verify account, turn on <a href="https://www.e-verify.gov/employees/employee-self-services/mye-verify/self-lock" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Self Lock</a>, which helps stop anyone else using your SSN for an E-Verify job check. Unlock it before you start a job with an employer that uses E-Verify.</li>
<li>Check your credit reports. All three bureaus now let you check each report <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/free-credit-reports" rel="noopener" target="_blank">once a week for free</a>, permanently, through AnnualCreditReport.com.</li>
<li>If you see a loan, card or job you don&#39;t recognise, report it at IdentityTheft.gov for a recovery plan (<a href="https://www.identitytheft.gov/Info-Lost-or-Stolen" rel="noopener" target="_blank">FTC</a>).</li>
</ol>
<p>Don&#39;t count on a new number. The FTC says you can&#39;t get a new Social Security number without evidence that someone is already misusing yours.</p>
