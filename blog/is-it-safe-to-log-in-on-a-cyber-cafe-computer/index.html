The short answer

Sometimes, with care. A quick Gmail, DigiLocker or college-portal login on a cyber cafe or lab computer is manageable if you use a Guest or private window, refuse every offer to save a password or stay signed in, and sign out remotely from your phone afterwards. Net banking is the exception: banks such as PNB tell customers not to use cyber cafe computers for it, so do it on your phone instead.

Which logins are okay on a cyber cafe computer

Not every login carries the same risk. Here is a rough guide for a normal cafe, college lab or print-shop PC you know nothing about:

What can go wrong when you log in on a public computer

Someone records what you type. A keylogger is software or a small device that records your keystrokes. Software keyloggers often arrive bundled with other malware. Hardware ones usually need someone with physical access to plug in a gadget, such as a USB device or a fake connector between the keyboard cable and the PC, according to Kaspersky. More capable versions also grab periodic screenshots and anything you copy and paste.

This is not theoretical. In 2011, staff at two public libraries on the southern edge of Greater Manchester found USB keyloggers plugged into keyboard sockets on the back of their PCs, most likely to capture banking logins. Those PCs already had filtering software and accounts that stopped users installing programs. A plug-in keylogger needs no installing, so neither helped.

The browser remembers you for the next person. When you type a new password, Chrome offers to save it. Google's 2-Step Verification screen has a "Don't ask again on this computer" box that Google says to tick only on devices you regularly use and don't share. Some sites add their own "Remember me" or "Stay signed in" checkbox. Accept any of these on a cafe PC and the next customer may walk straight into your account.

You sign in to the browser instead of the website. Signing in to Gmail is different from signing in to Chrome itself. Signing in to Chrome is how you get your bookmarks, passwords and more on all your devices. On a machine strangers use, that is the opposite of what you want.

Files stay behind. Private browsing does not clean up downloads. Google's own help page says downloaded files stay on your device until you delete them, even in Incognito. The marksheet, Aadhaar copy or offer letter you downloaded to print is still sitting in the Downloads folder after you leave.

Someone else can see or control the machine. Remote-access tools like AnyDesk exist to let someone reach and control a computer from somewhere else. Cafe management software is sold with features to manage computers remotely and track every session in detailed logs. None of this is sinister by default, but the person running the cafe may see more than you assume.

The cafe keeps records by law. India's Cyber Cafe Rules, 2011 require a cafe to verify your ID and keep a copy for at least a year, log your login and logout times, and back up the history of websites accessed on its computers for at least a year. The same rules say screens outside cubicles must face the open area of the cafe, so the person standing behind you can often read your screen.

Private windows only tidy up the browser. Guest and Incognito windows mainly stop the browser keeping your history, cookies and site data after you close them. Google says Guest mode doesn't hide your activity from the device owner, the network operator or your school. Neither mode does anything about a keylogger that is already on the machine.

How to judge a cyber cafe or lab computer before you log in

Do these quick checks before you type anything:

  • Look at the keyboard plug. Follow the keyboard cable to the computer. A small extra adapter between the cable and the port is a red flag. Ask for another machine.
  • Check the taskbar. Icons for remote-access tools such as AnyDesk or TeamViewer in the corner of the screen suggest the machine is set up to be reached from elsewhere.
  • See what the last person left. If the browser opens on someone else's inbox or a half-filled form, the cafe does not reset machines between customers. Hong Kong's government InfoSec site suggests picking places that wipe and reinstall the system for each user.
  • Try opening a Guest window. If Guest mode and private windows are blocked, Google advises clearing cache, cookies and history both before you sign in and after you sign out.
  • Pick your seat. Choose a screen nobody can stand behind.

None of these checks will find well-hidden malware. They only rule out the obvious problems.

Before, during and after: a checklist for logging in safely

Before you sit down

  1. Ask whether it has to happen on this computer. Much of it can be done on your phone (see the next section).
  2. Turn on 2-Step Verification for your Google account if it is off. It exists to protect you in case your password is stolen, which is exactly the cyber cafe risk. Our Google account security checkup guide walks through it.

While you're logged in

  1. Open a clean window. In Chrome, click the profile icon at the top right and choose Open Guest profile. Google recommends Guest mode for public computers such as a library or cafe. In Edge or Firefox, open a private window.
  2. Type the website address yourself instead of clicking links or bookmarks already on the machine.
  3. Sign in to the website only. If the browser offers to turn on sync or sign you in to Chrome, decline.
  4. Refuse every "save password", "remember me" or "stay signed in" prompt, and leave "Don't ask again on this computer" unticked.
  5. If a bank login truly cannot wait, use the bank's on-screen keyboard. PNB recommends it for entering passwords, and Kaspersky notes it stops malware reading what you type on the physical keyboard. It does not help against tools that capture the screen.
  6. Never walk away with a session open, even to collect a printout from the counter.

After you're done

  1. Sign out of every site. Delete whatever you downloaded or scanned, and check the Recycle Bin too.
  2. Close every Guest or private window. Closing the Guest window deletes browsing history, cookies and site data.
  3. On your phone, open your Google Account and go to Security & sign-in, then Your devices, then Manage all devices (labels as of October 2026; Gmail Help also links straight to this device list). Select the cafe computer and tap Sign out. One computer can show up as several sessions, so Google says to sign out of every session with that device's name. If you used WhatsApp Web, unlink that too: see how to log out of WhatsApp Web from another computer.
  4. If you typed a password you also use anywhere else, change it from your own device. Hong Kong's InfoSec site gives the same advice.

Safer ways to get it done without typing your password

Use your phone. DigiLocker has Android and iPhone apps, and so do most banks. If the only reason you are at the computer is to print, the shop has its own privacy risks: see how to keep documents private at a print shop, and use a masked Aadhaar where it is accepted (how to share an Aadhaar copy safely).

Sign in with a passkey from your phone. On the Google sign-in page, choose Try another way, then Use your passkey, and scan the QR code with your phone (Google's steps). Google says neither the passkey nor your screen lock information is sent to the computer, so there is no password for a keylogger to catch. Three caveats:

  • The computer checks that your phone is nearby over Bluetooth, so this only works on a machine with Bluetooth turned on.
  • The login still creates a new session on that computer, so do the remote sign-out step anyway.
  • If the computer offers to create a passkey on itself, say no. Google warns not to create one on a shared device, because anyone who can get past that device's screen lock could then sign in as you.

Questions people ask

Can the cyber cafe owner see which websites I visited?

Often, yes. India's Cyber Cafe Rules, 2011 require cafes to keep a backup of the websites accessed on their computers for at least a year, along with your ID details and login times. Cafe management software can also log every session.

Is it safe to do net banking at a cyber cafe?

Indian banks advise against it. PNB, for example, tells customers to avoid internet banking from cyber cafes and shared computers because you cannot know what software is installed. Use your bank's app on your own phone instead.

How do I sign out of Gmail on a cyber cafe computer after I have left?

On your phone, open your Google Account, go to Security & sign-in, then Your devices, then Manage all devices. Select the cafe computer's session and tap Sign out, and repeat for every session listed under that device.

Should I change my password after using a public computer?

If you typed a password on a machine you don't control, changing it from your own device is the safe move, especially if you use the same password anywhere else. Turning on 2-Step Verification also means a stolen password alone is not enough to get in.

Should I save a passkey on a cyber cafe or lab computer?

No. Google says to create passkeys only on devices you personally own, because anyone who can get past the screen lock of a device holding your passkey can sign in to your account. Use the passkey on your phone by scanning the QR code instead.
