<h2 id="what-does-masked-aadhaar-mean">What does masked Aadhaar mean?</h2>
<p>&quot;Masked&quot; means the first eight digits of your 12-digit Aadhaar number are hidden and only the last four show (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-online-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). It isn&#39;t a separate card: UIDAI says the masked facility &quot;is available only in e-Aadhaar&quot;, the PDF you download. UIDAI also says e-Aadhaar is <a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-mythbusters" rel="noopener" target="_blank">as legally valid as the original</a> it issues.</p>
<p>Everything else stays: your name, photo, gender, date of birth, address and the QR code.</p>
<h2 id="masked-aadhaar-vs-photocopy-what-each-one-shows">Masked Aadhaar vs photocopy: what each one shows</h2>
<p class="table-hint" aria-hidden="true"></p><div class="table-scroll" role="region" aria-label="Table" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col"></th><th scope="col">Plain photocopy (xerox)</th><th scope="col">Full e-Aadhaar PDF</th><th scope="col">Masked e-Aadhaar</th><th scope="col">Aadhaar app QR share</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th scope="row">Aadhaar digits visible</th><td>All 12</td><td>All 12</td><td>Last 4</td><td>None, or the last 4 if the request asks</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Name, photo, date of birth, address</th><td>Yes</td><td>Yes</td><td>Yes</td><td>Only the fields the request asks for</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Signed by UIDAI</th><td>Only its QR code</td><td>Yes, digitally</td><td>Yes, digitally</td><td>Yes</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Best for</th><td>Avoid if you can</td><td>Your own records</td><td>Landlord, HR, college, print shop</td><td>Hotels, hospitals and other places with a UIDAI QR request</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>The last column is the newest option. With UIDAI&#39;s Aadhaar app, launched in January 2026, the organisation shows a QR, you scan it, and it gets a digitally signed credential with only the fields that use needs (<a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2219790" rel="noopener" target="_blank">PIB</a>). Where that works, it beats any copy.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-download-a-masked-aadhaar-card">How to download a masked Aadhaar card</h2>
<p>The download is free, but it needs an OTP on the mobile number linked to your Aadhaar (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/my-aadhaar" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI My Aadhaar</a>).</p>
<ol>
<li>Open UIDAI&#39;s <a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/my-aadhaar" rel="noopener" target="_blank">My Aadhaar page</a> and, under <strong>Keep Aadhaar Secure</strong>, choose <strong>Download Masked Aadhaar</strong>. It opens the <a href="https://myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in/genricDownloadAadhaar/en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">myAadhaar download page</a>; labels there may differ slightly.</li>
<li>Enter your Aadhaar number, Virtual ID or enrolment ID, and the security code if one is shown.</li>
<li>Enter the OTP sent to your registered mobile.</li>
<li>Make sure the masked option is selected, then download the PDF.</li>
<li>Open it and check that only the last four digits are visible before you send it anywhere.</li>
</ol>
<p>No mobile linked to your Aadhaar? Register one at an Aadhaar Seva Kendra or enrolment centre first. Our guide to <a href="/blog/how-to-share-aadhaar-copy-safely/">sharing an Aadhaar copy safely</a> covers the other official tools: the Aadhaar app, offline e-KYC and DigiLocker.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-the-masked-aadhaar-password">What is the masked Aadhaar password?</h2>
<p>The PDF is locked with the first four letters of your name, in capitals, followed by your year of birth (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-online-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). UIDAI&#39;s own examples, all born in 1990:</p>
<div class="table-scroll" role="region" aria-label="Table" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Name on Aadhaar</th><th scope="col">Password</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th scope="row">SURESH KUMAR</th><td>SURE1990</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">SAI KUMAR</th><td>SAIK1990</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">P. KUMAR</th><td>P.KU1990</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">RIA</th><td>RIA1990</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>Spaces are skipped, a dot counts as a character, and a name shorter than four letters is used as it is.</p>
<p>That password is weak: anyone who has seen your Aadhaar knows both halves. So a PDF left in a print shop&#39;s Downloads folder is effectively open. Delete it before you leave, or skip downloading it there at all (see <a href="/print-without-login/">print at a xerox shop without logging in</a>).</p>
<h2 id="is-masked-aadhaar-valid-everywhere">Is masked Aadhaar valid everywhere?</h2>
<p>UIDAI says e-Aadhaar is as valid as the physical card, and a masked Aadhaar is an e-Aadhaar. It doesn&#39;t say anything separate about the masked version, so a few places may still insist on the full number. A masked copy also can&#39;t help where a form or system needs your full number typed in, because there you are quoting the number, not handing over a copy.</p>
<p>The rules lean towards masking anyway. RBI&#39;s KYC directions tell banks to make sure a customer <a href="https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13141" rel="noopener" target="_blank">redacts or blacks out the Aadhaar number</a> where authentication isn&#39;t required. UIDAI&#39;s regulations say agencies that authenticate Aadhaar must mask the first 8 digits <a href="https://uidai.gov.in/images/The_Aadhaar_Authentication_and_Offline_Verifications_Regulations_2021-_Clean_copy-30122025.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">before storing a photocopy</a>. So when a bank asks for a full-number xerox, a masked one is what RBI&#39;s own rule points to, and anywhere else it follows the spirit of those rules. UIDAI also advises against putting your Aadhaar on public platforms such as social media (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/your-aadhaar" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>).</p>
