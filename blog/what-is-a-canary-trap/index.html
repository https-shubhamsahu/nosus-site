What Is a Canary Trap? How Reworded Copies Catch a Leaker

By Shubham Sahu, founder of NO SUS16 October 2026, 4 min read

The short answer

A canary trap, also called a barium meal test, gives each person a slightly different version of the same document. If it leaks, the version that appears points to whose copy it was. It is a strong hint, not proof, and heavy rewording or mixing copies can defeat it. NO SUS Canary automates it for up to 100 readers, for text.

A canary trap is a way to catch a leaker by giving each person a slightly different copy of the same document. If a copy leaks, its exact wording shows whose it was. The trick is also called a barium meal test. It gives a strong hint about the source, not proof of who shared it.

Where the name comes from

Tom Clancy coined the term in his 1987 novel Patriot Games. According to Wikipedia's canary trap article, he did not invent the technique. In the novel, the analyst Jack Ryan prepares numbered copies of a document, each with different versions of its summary paragraphs, and makes them dramatic enough that a reporter is likely to quote them. Wikipedia dates the novel to 1987.

The older name is the barium meal test. Wikipedia says intelligence agencies have used it for many years under that name, and that former MI5 officer Peter Wright wrote about it in his memoir. A barium meal is the contrast drink given before a medical X-ray, so the doctor can see where it travels. Code and Dagger makes the same comparison: a marker in the information makes its path visible.

How reworded copies identify a leaker

The method has three steps.

  1. Make every copy different. Change something small in each version: a word, a comma, a figure, an extra detail. Former FBI agent Joe Navarro, quoted by Code and Dagger, describes a comma instead of a semicolon or a deliberately misspelled word.
  2. Keep a record. Write down who got which version.
  3. Match the leak. When text appears where it should not, compare it with your record. The version that matches names the copy.

Here is a tiny example. Three people each get one version of this sentence:

  • Asha: "Kindly do not share these five figures until Friday."
  • Ben: "Kindly don't share these 5 figures until Friday."
  • Dev: "Please do not share these 5 figures until Friday."

If the leaked line reads "please do not share these 5 figures until friday", it matches Dev's copy. Each choice between two wordings doubles the number of possible copies. Make a few such choices across a long text and there are enough combinations to give dozens of people their own copy.

The idea also works outside documents. In 2019, Coleen Rooney posted fake Instagram stories that only one account could see. When they appeared in a tabloid, she named that account publicly, as Wikipedia's article describes. The dispute became the libel trial known as the Wagatha Christie case. A match is the start of a conversation, not the end of one.

Honest limits

A canary trap is good at one thing and weak at several others.

  • It identifies a copy, not a person. A phone gets lost, a screen gets shown to a friend, a login gets shared. The copy that leaked may not be the reader who leaked it.
  • Heavy rewording defeats it. If the leaker retypes the text in their own words, the markers are gone.
  • Mixing copies confuses it. If two readers compare notes and merge their versions, the result can point at the wrong person or at nobody.
  • Obvious differences give it away. If readers spot that their copies differ, they know there is a trap. Subtle changes work better.
  • It stops nothing. It does not prevent copying. It only helps you work out where a copy came from afterwards.

Code and Dagger also notes that a former White House official could not say whether one reported use of the trick had ever worked. Treat it as a deterrent and a clue.

Questions people ask

What is the canary trap meaning?

A canary trap is a way to find a leaker by giving each person a slightly different version of the same document. When a version leaks, its exact wording shows whose copy it came from. It is also called a barium meal test.

Did Tom Clancy invent the canary trap?

No. Wikipedia says Clancy coined the term in his 1987 novel Patriot Games but did not invent the technique. Intelligence agencies used it before that, where it is sometimes called a barium meal test.

What is a barium meal test?

It is the spy world's name for the same idea. The name comes from the barium meal used in medical X-rays, which makes the path through the body visible. Planting a marker makes the path of leaked information visible.

Does a canary trap prove who leaked a document?

No. It shows whose copy was used, which is a strong hint. Someone else could have seen that reader's screen, copied the text from a shared device, or mixed two readers' copies, so confirm by other means before you accuse anyone.

How many people can I use a canary trap on?

By hand, a handful at most, because you edit every copy yourself. NO SUS Canary makes up to 100 reworded copies from one link, for text only for now, and readers need no account.

Can a canary trap stop screenshots?

No. It does nothing to stop copying. It only helps you identify which copy was copied. NO SUS Canary also cannot stop a photo of the screen.
