AI Chatbot Privacy Study: Your Chats May Be Reaching Trackers

By the NO SUS team · 12 October 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

A preprint from IMDEA Networks in Madrid, reported from 5 October 2026, found that all nine big AI chatbots it tested contact ad or tracking companies, and that 6 of 9 chatbot websites sent chat links, chat titles, prompts or screenshots to outside firms, often alongside ad cookies or hashed emails. Grok was the worst case: its chat links were public by default, and test links planted in Grok chats were later opened from 14 countries. The study does not show any ad company reading chats, but you should keep ID numbers and documents out of prompts and stop using share links for anything personal.

What did the IMDEA chatbot study find?

Researchers at IMDEA Networks Institute in Madrid tested the websites of nine consumer AI chatbots (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, Mistral's Le Chat and Meta AI) and eight of their Android apps. According to the full preprint, tests ran from Spain in May 2026 with health-related prompts, across guest, free and paid accounts and different cookie choices.

The main numbers:

  • Every service contacted at least one ad or tracking company: 124 third-party domains belonging to 44 organisations, 34 of them advertising or tracking firms.
  • 6 of 9 websites and 3 of 8 Android apps passed chat details to outside services: chat links, chat IDs, auto-generated titles, prompts or screenshots. These often travelled with identifiers that can point to a person, such as Meta's _fbp and TikTok's _ttp cookies, account IDs, or plain or hashed emails.
  • Privacy settings helped less than you'd hope. After non-essential cookies were rejected, 80.8% of third-party trackers stayed active. Free and paid accounts talked to largely the same third parties.

The paper, "Prompt like a Butterfly, Sting like a Tracker", is listed for the 2027 Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies in its artifact repository. PPC Land reported it on 5 October and BASIC thinking on 8 October. It expands the team's four-service LeakyLM release of 4 May 2026, which Decrypt covered, so this is the full study, not a new leak.

Which chatbots sent what, and to whom?

Grok was the worst case. In the study, ordinary Grok chat links for free and paid users were readable by anyone by default unless you opted out, and LeakyLM found guest chats were always public. Once cookies were accepted, one Grok conversation reached seven ad and analytics services, each getting the chat's ID and title, and Meta's pixel tied it to the user's Meta identity through the _fbp cookie. Grok also relayed links and titles to Meta and TikTok through a server-side setup that ad blockers can't touch. When someone opened a shared Grok chat, TikTok received a screenshot of it, and Meta and TikTok both got the latest prompt.

The others:

  • Gemini sent conversation titles to Google Analytics (Google's own product, which the paper still counts as a tracker).
  • ChatGPT and Claude websites sent each chat's unique ID to Datadog, a monitoring service. The ID is enough to rebuild the chat's link, though chats on both stay owner-only unless shared.
  • Claude, if you accept non-essential cookies, forwards events server-to-server to eleven ad and tracking services, including Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok and Reddit. Ad blockers can't see this, but rejecting cookies stopped it in the tests.
  • Perplexity sent hashed emails to the marketing firm Singular. It stopped sending chat links to Meta on 3 April 2026.
  • Mistral's Le Chat sent chat titles, links and the account email to Intercom, a customer-support tool.

Titles are revealing because the service writes them to summarise what you asked. In the paper's examples, a question about early Parkinson's symptoms got a title naming the condition, and a salary question got one naming the salary, the city and a mortgage range. Gemini, Grok and Mistral sent titles like these to outside firms.

Did anyone actually read the chats?

The team planted unique trap links (canary tokens) in chats and uploaded files. Only Grok showed outside access: 70 activations from 70 IP addresses in 14 countries, hours to days later, about two-thirds from the US although the tests ran in the EU. Perplexity's own crawler also fetched the links repeatedly, even when told not to. The Grok result shows links inside Grok chats were picked up by outside systems, but not by whom or why.

The caveats:

  • It is a preprint, not yet formally published.
  • It doesn't show any ad company reading a conversation; the authors argue the exposure itself is the problem.
  • It was measured from Spain under EU cookie rules; the authors say other countries may differ. Nothing was tested from India.
  • Meta AI was tested on its own website and app, not inside WhatsApp or Instagram.

The team alerted EU and UK data protection authorities and xAI in April 2026. The paper says that as of 10 September 2026, Grok links were still public and xAI had not replied.

Why this matters if you use AI for SOPs, resumes and loan papers

Polishing a resume or SOP, drafting a visa or loan letter, or asking about health and money can mean pasting an Aadhaar or PAN number, phone number, address, marksheet, offer letter or salary slip into a chatbot.

LeakyLM warns that leaking a chat's link "can be equivalent to leaking the conversation itself". OpenAI's help page says personal-account share links open for anyone with the link, can't be limited to specific people and have no expiry setting. It also asks users to keep health information, financial details, passwords and account numbers out of shared chats.

What to do now

  1. Don't paste ID numbers, phone numbers, addresses, bank details or whole documents. Swap them for placeholders like [NAME], [PAN] and [PHONE]. If someone genuinely needs your Aadhaar, see how to share an Aadhaar copy safely.
  2. Keep your opening message generic. In the paper's examples, titles were built from the question itself. Start with "Help me edit a letter", not the diagnosis, bank or visa type.
  3. Stop using share links for anything personal, and clear old ones. In ChatGPT: Settings > Data controls > Shared links > Manage, then delete. Deleting a conversation also removes its link, but anyone who already continued a shared chat keeps their own copy. LeakyLM advises restricting conversation access in Grok's settings, revoking Grok shared links chat by chat, and setting Perplexity threads to Private.
  4. Reject non-essential cookies and use a tracker-blocking browser. Firefox's Strict mode blocks tracking content in every window, and the paper notes Brave blocks trackers by default. Neither stops server-to-server forwarding.
  5. Use temporary chats and delete old ones, but don't treat them as hidden from trackers. ChatGPT's temporary chats stay out of history and training but may be kept for up to 30 days, and the study saw tracker disclosures in incognito modes too.
  6. Log out of AI accounts on lab and cyber cafe computers. An open session shows your chat history to the next user. See is it safe to log in on a cyber cafe computer.
  7. Consider a separate email for AI sign-ups. Hashed emails were among the identifiers sent, and the US FTC says hashing doesn't make data anonymous.

Questions people ask

Is ChatGPT sharing my chats with advertisers?

The full IMDEA study found ChatGPT's website sending conversation IDs to Datadog, a monitoring service, and the team's earlier LeakyLM release in May 2026 reported chat links and titles reaching Google Analytics for free logged-in users. The study does not show anyone reading those chats, and ChatGPT chats stay owner-only unless you share them.

Does rejecting cookies stop AI chatbots from tracking me?

Only partly. In the study, 80.8% of third-party trackers stayed active after non-essential cookies were rejected. On Claude, rejecting cookies stopped the Meta Pixel, Datadog and server-side ad forwarding, but not its Intercom support widget. Most Android apps offered no cookie choice at all.

Are AI chat share links public?

Usually, yes. ChatGPT says anyone with a personal-account share link can view it, and that links cannot be limited to specific people or given an expiry date. In the study, Grok's ordinary chat links were also public by default unless you opted out.

Does paying for ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro protect my privacy?

Not much. The researchers found free and paid accounts contacted largely the same third parties. One difference: on the web, only ChatGPT's paid tier remembered your cookie choices.

Was Meta AI inside WhatsApp tested?

No. The study tested Meta AI's own website and its standalone Android app, not the assistant built into WhatsApp or Instagram, so it says nothing direct about those.
