TRAI New Spam Call Rules 2026: BLOCK PROMO, DND and 1600 Numbers

By the NO SUS team · 12 October 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

TRAI's amended spam regulations, notified on 18 September 2026, let telecom operators use their AI spam detection to start action against callers, bring robocalls and AI-voice calls under the rules, and replace the old FULLY BLOCK DND choice with BLOCK PROMO. BLOCK PROMO stops promotional calls and SMS but still lets OTPs, service alerts and government messages through. Bank and government service calls are meant to come from 1600xx numbers, and caller-ID apps may no longer mark those numbers as spam.

What did TRAI change on 18 September 2026?

On 18 September 2026 the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India notified the Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026 (05 of 2026) and issued a press release the same day. It amends the 2018 rules behind the DND registry, and press coverage followed around 29 September (Moneylife, Onmanorama, Upstox).

TRAI's explanatory memo explains why complaints alone weren't enough. In April 2026 operators' AI systems flagged about 771 crore calls and 48 crore SMS as suspected spam, yet customers filed only 2,66,105 complaints, and just 17,401 met the threshold for action. About 89% of complaints from January to April 2026 were against unregistered telemarketers, whose calls typically bypass the DND registry.

How will telcos act on spam they detect themselves?

A new regulation 21A lets action start from the operators' own AI flags, not only from complaints:

  1. The operator receiving a suspicious call flags the number and passes it to the caller's operator within two hours.
  2. Within one business day, the caller's operator finds the sender's KYC details and shares them with all operators, who identify all numbers issued to that sender.
  3. Operators check whether the sender's other numbers were flagged in the previous ten days. Five or more triggers action.

Several headlines called this a 15-day ban for a first offence. The regulation text is more graded. A first case means a KYC re-check, with action under the KYC rules if it fails. A second means physical verification, and if the KYC doesn't match or misuse is confirmed, all outgoing services on all the sender's SIMs and trunks are barred for 15 days across operators. Later cases can mean one-year disconnection, blacklisting and blocked devices. TRAI's memo says no sender is penalised on AI flags alone.

Complaints will count for more too. From the 90-day stage, three complaints from different people within ten days, plus an AI flag on any of the sender's numbers, trigger suspension and an investigation of an unregistered telemarketer. A confirmed first violation brings a 15-day outgoing bar, and a repeat brings one-year disconnection.

Do the rules cover robocalls and AI-voice calls?

The rules now cover application-to-person (A2P) calls: autodialled, robo, pre-recorded and AI-voice calls placed by software. Businesses must declare these calls and their calling numbers to their operator in advance, or the calls count as spam. The receiving operator may charge the caller's operator up to 5 paise a minute for them, except for calls from the designated 140xx, 1600xx and 1601xx series.

What happened to your DND "FULLY BLOCK" setting?

FULLY BLOCK is being retired. Every FULLY BLOCK preference moves to BLOCK PROMO within 15 days of the rules coming into force. BLOCK PROMO blocks all promotional categories at all times, but not transactional messages, service messages (OTPs, balance alerts, delivery updates) or government messages. If FULLY BLOCK kept some of those away, expect them back.

When do the new spam rules start?

Most provisions apply 30 days after the regulation appears in the Official Gazette. A2P declarations, the 5 paise charge and tighter rules on misused SMS headers follow at 60 days. The consumer appeal route and the matching of complaints with AI data come at 90 days. Neither TRAI's notification nor the press coverage gives the Gazette publication date. If it was 18 September, the main rules start around 18 October and the last parts around mid-December 2026.

How do you tell a real bank call from a fake one?

TRAI has reserved number series for regulated callers:

  • 140xx: registered promotional telemarketing.
  • 1600xx: service and transaction calls from banks, insurers and other financial regulated entities, plus government-to-citizen calls. TRAI says only regulated entities get these numbers.
  • 1601xx: service calls from other sectors, starting with utilities, logistics and courier companies under an August 2026 direction.

Once the rules apply, caller-ID apps such as Truecaller may not tag, block or filter these series wholesale. You can still block any number on your own phone.

A "bank" or "courier" call from an ordinary 10-digit number deserves suspicion. It isn't automatically fake, since TRAI's memo leaves room to exempt small financial firms from the 1600 mandate and some service calls still use normal numbers. So hang up and call back on the number on your card or in the official app. Whatever number shows, the RBI says never to share an OTP, PIN or UPI PIN, even with bank staff, and to ignore calls offering to update your KYC.

What should you do now?

  1. Set your preference. Text BLOCK PROMO to 1909 or dial *1909*50# and keep the confirmation SMS. If it isn't accepted yet, retry after the rules take effect. FULLY BLOCK users should check their setting after the migration.
  2. Check the number. A "bank" on an ordinary mobile number gets a call back on the official number, never your OTP.
  3. Complain within seven days, in the right place. Use the DND app, 1909 or your operator's app, with the full number or SMS header. After seven days it is logged only as a report. A "report spam" tap in a caller-ID app reaches no operator unless the app forwards it to the operators' DLT platform, which the new rules require. TRAI's memo, citing a Truecaller report, notes one spammer was reported over 3.25 lakh times in the app during 2025 and still made about 1.25 crore calls.
  4. Report fraud on Chakshu. Fake KYC, bank or government calls, SMS and WhatsApp messages go to Chakshu on Sanchar Saathi within 30 days. If you've lost money, call 1930 or file at cybercrime.gov.in immediately.
  5. Appeal a wrongly closed complaint. From the 90-day stage, you can appeal within 15 days of the operator's reply, or, if it doesn't reply within 15 days, during the following 15 days. TRAI's release says appeals use the same channels as complaints: the DND app, your operator's app or portal, or 1909.
  6. Hand out your number less. Under the amended rules, a written or online enquiry lets a business contact you for seven days, and an application for three months. Every form you fill and every "send it on WhatsApp" request gives your number to one more business. See print shop document privacy and receiving files without sharing your number.

Questions people ask

Does BLOCK PROMO stop OTPs and bank alerts?

No. Under the amended TRAI rules, BLOCK PROMO blocks only promotional calls and SMS. Transactional messages, service messages such as OTPs, balance alerts and delivery updates, and government messages still reach you.

What happens to my FULLY BLOCK DND preference?

TRAI's amendment says every FULLY BLOCK preference moves to BLOCK PROMO within 15 days of the rules coming into force. You don't need to do anything, but expect service and government messages to start arriving if FULLY BLOCK had stopped them.

Is a call from a 1600 number really from my bank?

1600xx numbers are reserved for banks and other financial regulated entities and for government-to-citizen calls, so they are a far better sign than an ordinary mobile number. Still, never share an OTP, PIN or UPI PIN on any call, whatever number appears.

Can Truecaller still block 140 or 1600 numbers?

Once the amended rules take effect, caller-ID and call-management apps may not tag, block or filter calls from designated series such as 140xx, 1600xx and 1601xx. You can still block any individual number on your own phone.

Will a spammer be banned the first time the AI flags them?

Not straight away. If five or more of a sender's numbers are flagged within ten days, the first step is a KYC re-check. A second case brings physical verification and a 15-day outgoing bar if misuse is found, and later cases can mean one-year disconnection and blacklisting.
