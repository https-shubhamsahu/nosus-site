The short answer

Yes, you can log out of Google from another device. On your phone, open your Google Account, go to Security & sign-in, then Your devices, then Manage all devices, pick the public computer and tap Sign out on every session with its name. Then change your password if you typed it there, check that nothing in Gmail was changed, and unlink WhatsApp Web or any other account you opened.

Walked out of a cyber cafe, college lab or print shop and realised your Gmail is still open on their computer? You can fix the login from your phone in a minute or two. The rest takes a few more minutes, because signing out from your phone ends the session; it does not clean the computer.

How to log out of your Google account from other devices remotely

  1. On your phone, open myaccount.google.com, or tap your profile picture in any Google app and choose Manage your Google Account.
  2. Go to Security & sign-in, then Your devices, then Manage all devices (labels as of October 2026). Google's device list opens straight to it.
  3. Find the public computer. Look for a Windows computer and a browser you used around that time and place.
  4. Select it and tap Sign out. One computer can show up as several sessions, so Google says to sign out of every session with that device's name.
  5. Back on Security & sign-in, open Recent security events and check that nothing happened after you left.

So yes, you can log out of Google from another device, and it covers the whole account on that computer, not just Gmail. Gmail Help points to the same device list for signing out of a computer you no longer have in front of you.

What if the public computer isn't in the list?

Change your Google password from your own phone. Google says a password change signs you out everywhere except devices you use to verify it's you, and some home devices and third-party apps you gave account access. A new password also matters if you typed the old one on that keyboard: a shared computer can record keystrokes, as our cyber cafe guide explains. Then open the Details link at the bottom right of your Gmail inbox on a computer, which lists the last 10 IP addresses that accessed your mail.

Did you sign in to Chrome itself, not just Gmail?

If the cafe's Chrome showed your name or photo in the top right, or you clicked Turn on sync, you signed in to the browser. That is different from signing in to a website: Chrome sync puts your bookmarks, passwords and payment info on every device signed in to your account. Sign the computer out as above, then, from your own device, change your Google password and every saved password that guards money, mail or ID documents.

Check what someone could have changed

A stranger with a few minutes in your inbox doesn't need your password to keep reading your mail. Forwarding addresses and filters are saved settings, not sessions, so they survive a sign-out. On a computer, open Gmail, click the gear icon, then See all settings, and check:

  • Forwarding and POP/IMAP: no forwarding to an address you don't know.
  • Filters and Blocked Addresses: no filter that forwards or deletes mail you didn't set up.
  • Accounts and Import: every "Send mail as" address is yours.

Our Google account security checkup walks through each screen, plus recovery phone and email.

Signed in to WhatsApp Web or another account too?

Sign those out from your phone as well:

  • WhatsApp Web: on your phone, open Linked devices, tap the computer and choose Log out. Step by step: log out of WhatsApp Web from another computer.
  • Microsoft (Outlook, Hotmail): sign in to the advanced security options of your Microsoft account, scroll to Sign out everywhere and select Sign out. Microsoft says this signs you out of browsers and apps within 24 hours, Xbox consoles aside.
  • Banks, DigiLocker and college portals: change the password from your own device if you typed it on that computer.

How to access Gmail on a public computer next time

  1. Open a Guest window (in Chrome: profile icon, then Open Guest profile) or a private window. Google recommends Guest mode for public computers.
  2. Type gmail.com yourself and sign in to the website only. Decline any offer to sign in to the browser or turn on sync.
  3. Refuse "save password", and leave "Don't ask again on this computer" unticked.
  4. Sign out, close the window, then check Your devices on your phone before you walk away.

Closing a Guest window deletes its history, cookies and site data, but downloads stay on the computer until someone deletes them, even from Incognito. Delete them yourself, and empty the Recycle Bin. The full before-and-after list is in is it safe to log in on a cyber cafe computer.

What remote sign-out does not do

  • It doesn't delete files you downloaded, scanned or printed on that computer.
  • It doesn't undo anything a keylogger already captured: only a new password makes a captured one useless.
  • It doesn't remove Gmail forwarding or filters someone added while you were away.

Questions people ask

Can you log out of Google from another device?

Yes. On your phone, open your Google Account, go to Security & sign-in, then Your devices, then Manage all devices. Select the public computer and tap Sign out. One computer can appear as several sessions, so sign out of every session with its name.

How do I log out of my Google account from other devices remotely if the computer isn't listed?

Change your Google password from your own phone. Google says a password change signs you out everywhere except devices you use to verify it's you and some devices or apps you gave account access.

Does signing out remotely delete files I downloaded on that computer?

No. It ends the session only. Anything you downloaded, scanned or printed stays on that computer until someone deletes it, so ask the cafe or shop to delete it, or go back and do it yourself.

How do I access Gmail on a public computer safely?

Open a Guest or private window, type gmail.com yourself, sign in to the website only and refuse every offer to save your password or remember the device. When you finish, sign out, close the window, and check Your devices on your phone.

Should I change my password after using a public computer?

If you typed it there, yes, from your own device. A shared computer can record keystrokes, and a new password plus 2-Step Verification means a captured one is useless.
