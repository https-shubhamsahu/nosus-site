3f:T272e,<p>A xerox can&#39;t receive an OTP or press a fingerprint, so it can&#39;t prove the person holding it is you. That&#39;s the honest answer to &quot;can someone misuse my aadhar card xerox&quot;: the risk is real, but it is smaller and more specific than the WhatsApp forwards suggest.</p>
<h2 id="what-cant-someone-do-with-your-aadhaar-xerox">What can&#39;t someone do with your Aadhaar xerox?</h2>
<p>A copy can&#39;t pass the checks that prove a person is you. OTP authentication needs the code sent to your Aadhaar-linked mobile, and biometric authentication needs your fingerprint, iris or face (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/authentication" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). A photocopy has neither. It can pass checks that only look at the paper: the QR code on every form of Aadhaar is <a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-online-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">digitally signed by UIDAI</a>, so scanning it proves the details are genuine, but not that the person holding the copy is you. UIDAI&#39;s FAQ says it plainly: <a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/your-aadhaar" rel="noopener" target="_blank">&quot;Just, by knowing your Aadhaar number, no one can harm you&quot;</a>, and no one can withdraw money just by knowing your Aadhaar number or Aadhaar-linked bank account. Its mythbusters page adds that the number alone <a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-mythbusters" rel="noopener" target="_blank">can&#39;t be used for banking or any other service</a>.</p>
<p>The two places people worry about most have mostly closed the paper route:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>SIM cards.</strong> The Department of Telecommunications <a href="https://www.dot.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/cafe32ff32948adf26001a48b2093280.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ended paper-based KYC</a> for new connections from 1 January 2024, keeping it only for exceptions such as persons with disability (<a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20240712084917/https://dot.gov.in/sites/default/files/Use%20of%20Paper%20based%20KYC%20process.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">DoT, March 2024</a>). Shops now use Aadhaar e-KYC or digital KYC, where the app takes a live photo of the customer and of the <a href="https://www.dot.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/94c5c5c3b29c65d994a1c0e6dce211fc.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">original document</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Bank accounts.</strong> RBI&#39;s KYC directions tell banks to verify Aadhaar offline or through digital KYC, and to use a certified copy (checked against the original by a bank officer) only as a stop-gap (<a href="https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13141" rel="noopener" target="_blank">RBI</a>).</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-can-someone-do-with-an-aadhaar-photocopy">What can someone do with an Aadhaar photocopy?</h2>
<p>They can use it wherever someone accepts paper without verifying it. UIDAI itself says that agencies which simply accept a physical copy, without biometric or OTP authentication, are <a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/your-aadhaar" rel="noopener" target="_blank">not following good practice</a>, and in November 2025 it said it wants to <a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2191764" rel="noopener" target="_blank">discourage reliance on photocopies</a> because they are a possible reason for fraud. In practice that means:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Paper-only &quot;proof&quot;.</strong> A private rent agreement, a job or admission form, a society register or any counter that glances at a copy and files it. Your name, photo, date of birth and address are all on the page.</li>
<li><strong>Pretending to be you.</strong> With your details in hand, a caller or a fake &quot;KYC update&quot; message sounds far more convincing.</li>
<li><strong>Building a fuller profile.</strong> An Aadhaar xerox plus a PAN copy plus a cheque leaf is much more useful to a fraudster than any one of them.</li>
</ul>
<p>The fix is the same for all three: give less, and make each copy useless for anything but its purpose. Our guide to <a href="/blog/how-to-share-aadhaar-copy-safely/">sharing an Aadhaar copy safely</a> goes recipient by recipient: hotel, landlord, recruiter and print shop.</p>
<h2 id="fingerprint-fraud-is-a-separate-risk">Fingerprint fraud is a separate risk</h2>
<p>The Aadhaar fraud that does empty bank accounts uses a cloned fingerprint together with your Aadhaar number, which a full-number xerox hands over. That&#39;s one more reason to give masked copies. The Ministry of Home Affairs told Parliament in 2024 that <a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2039647" rel="noopener" target="_blank">around 29,000 Aadhaar Enabled Payment System (AePS) frauds</a> had been reported on the cybercrime portal. AePS lets you withdraw cash with your Aadhaar number and a fingerprint, so a cloned fingerprint is what a fraudster needs. A biometric lock stops it: with your biometrics locked, any fingerprint, iris or face authentication fails until you unlock it (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/authentication" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>).</p>
<h2 id="is-keeping-an-aadhaar-photocopy-banned-in-2026">Is keeping an Aadhaar photocopy banned in 2026?</h2>
<p>No outright ban has been notified. Here is what actually happened:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>May 2022.</strong> UIDAI&#39;s Bengaluru regional office told people <a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1828797" rel="noopener" target="_blank">not to share photocopies</a> of their Aadhaar with any organisation. Two days later the government <a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1829162" rel="noopener" target="_blank">withdrew that release</a> over &quot;the possibility of the misinterpretation&quot;, and advised people only to &quot;exercise normal prudence&quot;.</li>
<li><strong>December 2025.</strong> News reports said UIDAI would ban hotels and others from keeping photocopies (<a href="https://www.biometricupdate.com/202512/india-to-ban-aadhaar-photocopying-as-uidai-moves-to-regulate-private-sector-verification" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Biometric Update</a>). The <a href="https://backend.uidai.gov.in/get/files/media/document/2026-06/Gazette_dated_9_dec_2025.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">amendment UIDAI actually notified on 9 December 2025</a> makes entities register to verify Aadhaar through the Aadhaar app or offline e-KYC. It says nothing about photocopies. As of 23 October 2026, UIDAI&#39;s <a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/gazetted-notifications" rel="noopener" target="_blank">list of notifications</a> shows no later change to those rules.</li>
</ul>
<p>What the rules already say is useful at the counter. An entity that verifies you offline must not <a href="https://uidai.gov.in/images/The_Aadhaar_Authentication_and_Offline_Verifications_Regulations_2021-_Clean_copy-30122025.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">collect, use or store your Aadhaar number</a>. Agencies that authenticate Aadhaar must mask the first 8 digits before storing a photocopy, and banks must have you <a href="https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13141" rel="noopener" target="_blank">black out the number</a>. Offering a masked copy anywhere else follows the spirit of those rules. See <a href="/blog/masked-aadhaar-vs-photocopy/">masked Aadhaar vs photocopy</a> for how to get one.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-check-if-your-aadhaar-is-being-misused">How to check if your Aadhaar is being misused</h2>
<p>Your authentication history shows every time your Aadhaar was used to authenticate, by any agency or by you, in the last 6 months, up to 50 records at a time (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/authentication" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). Each record shows the method (OTP, fingerprint and so on), the date and time, the agency&#39;s name and whether it succeeded. Open it on myAadhaar or in the Aadhaar app; you need your registered mobile.</p>
<p>Look for authentications you don&#39;t remember, especially fingerprint ones from places you&#39;ve never been. If one wasn&#39;t you, UIDAI says to contact that agency for details.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-do-if-your-aadhaar-xerox-was-misused">What to do if your Aadhaar xerox was misused</h2>
<ol data-checklist>
<li>Lock your biometrics in the Aadhaar app or on UIDAI&#39;s website. You can unlock for about 15 minutes, or for a single authentication, when you need your fingerprint (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/authentication" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>).</li>
<li>For serious misuse, lock your whole Aadhaar. You need a 16-digit Virtual ID first, and once locked, no authentication works until you unlock it.</li>
<li>Call UIDAI on 1947 or email <a href="mailto:help@uidai.gov.in">help@uidai.gov.in</a>, and file a grievance on myAadhaar.</li>
<li>If money moved or a fake account was opened, report it at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930 (<a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2039647" rel="noopener" target="_blank">PIB</a>), and tell your bank.</li>
<li>From now on, give masked copies only, with the purpose and date written across them.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>What a masked copy and a biometric lock don&#39;t do</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>A masked copy still shows your name, photo, date of birth and address.</li>
<li>A biometric lock doesn&#39;t stop OTP authentication; only the full Aadhaar lock does. Never share an Aadhaar OTP.</li>
<li>Neither one takes back copies you have already handed out.</li>
</ul>
</blockquote>
