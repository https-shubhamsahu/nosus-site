3f:T4cdb,<p>Your Aadhaar doesn&#39;t need to be hacked to leak. A stack of photocopies in a hostel office, a PDF forwarded around a WhatsApp group, or a scan left on a print-shop computer is enough. UIDAI says your number alone can&#39;t hurt you, but it still advises people not to post it on public platforms (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/your-aadhaar" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). The Aadhaar Act goes further: an Aadhaar number, demographic details or photo collected under the Act must not be published, displayed or posted publicly except where regulations allow (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/images/Aadhaar_Act_2016_as_amended.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">section 29(4)</a>).</p>
<p>Here&#39;s how to share as little as possible with the official tools available in October 2026.</p>
<h2 id="which-way-of-sharing-aadhaar-should-i-use">Which way of sharing Aadhaar should I use?</h2>
<div class="table-scroll" role="region" aria-label="Table" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Situation</th><th scope="col">Best option</th><th scope="col">What the other side gets</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th scope="row">Hotel, hospital, cinema or gig platform shows you a QR code</th><td>Aadhaar app, selective sharing</td><td>Only the fields that QR request asks for, as a digitally signed credential</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Landlord, HR or college office wants a soft copy</th><td>Masked e-Aadhaar PDF</td><td>Last 4 digits, plus your name, photo, date of birth and address</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">An agency wants verifiable KYC data offline</th><td>Offline e-KYC ZIP and share code</td><td>A reference ID instead of your number, plus the fields you pick</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Office that requests documents through DigiLocker</th><td>DigiLocker consent</td><td>Your digital Aadhaar, with the access logged</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Paper is unavoidable</th><td>Masked printout, marked with purpose and date</td><td>A copy tied to one stated use</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<h2 id="is-it-safe-to-give-aadhaar-to-a-hotel-landlord-recruiter-or-print-shop">Is it safe to give Aadhaar to a hotel, landlord, recruiter or print shop?</h2>
<p>Usually, if you give each one the least it needs and write down why. People often type this as &quot;is it safe to give aadhar card to hotel&quot;, or ask the same about a recruiter. Here is what to hand over to the four who ask most.</p>
<h3 id="hotel-check-in">Hotel check-in</h3>
<p>Hotel check-in is one of the uses the government named for the new Aadhaar app. The desk shows a QR, you scan it, and the hotel gets only the fields it asked for as a signed credential instead of your number (<a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2219790" rel="noopener" target="_blank">PIB</a>). A hotel that verifies you offline must take your consent, tell you the purpose and the alternatives, and must not collect, use or store your Aadhaar number (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/images/Aadhaar_Act_2016_as_amended.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">section 8A</a>). If the desk only has a photocopier, offer a masked copy marked &quot;For check-in at [hotel], [date]&quot;, or ask whether another government photo ID, such as a driving licence, will do.</p>
<h3 id="landlord-or-pg-owner">Landlord or PG owner</h3>
<p>A landlord wants to know who you are for the rent agreement, and sometimes for a police tenant-verification form. A masked e-Aadhaar marked &quot;For rent agreement with [owner&#39;s name], [flat], [date]&quot; covers that. If they insist on the full 12 digits, ask where the number will be written and who keeps the copy. Never leave your original card with them.</p>
<h3 id="recruiter-or-hr">Recruiter or HR</h3>
<p>A recruiter doesn&#39;t need your Aadhaar to set up an interview. Identity checks usually come after a written offer. Send a masked copy through the company&#39;s own portal or to an email address on its official domain, not to a personal WhatsApp number. Treat anyone who wants Aadhaar, PAN and a &quot;registration fee&quot; before an interview as a scam.</p>
<h3 id="print-or-xerox-shop">Print or xerox shop</h3>
<p>The shop needs your file, not a copy for itself. Print the masked PDF, count your copies, take spoiled prints with you, and watch the file get deleted from their computer and their WhatsApp. To skip the shop&#39;s WhatsApp altogether, see <a href="/print-without-login/">how to print at a xerox shop without logging in</a>, and read our <a href="/blog/print-shop-document-privacy/">print shop privacy guide</a>.</p>
<p>Already handed out plain copies? Read <a href="/blog/can-someone-misuse-aadhaar-xerox/">what someone can and can&#39;t do with your Aadhaar xerox</a>. For what a masked copy hides and what it doesn&#39;t, see <a href="/blog/masked-aadhaar-vs-photocopy/">masked Aadhaar vs photocopy</a>.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-i-download-a-masked-aadhaar-in-2026">How do I download a masked Aadhaar in 2026?</h2>
<p>A masked Aadhaar replaces the first eight digits with &quot;xxxx-xxxx&quot;, so only the last four show (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-online-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). It is still a digitally signed e-Aadhaar, and UIDAI says e-Aadhaar is as valid as the physical letter for all purposes. The download is free but needs an OTP on your Aadhaar-linked mobile (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/my-aadhaar" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI My Aadhaar</a>).</p>
<ol>
<li>Open UIDAI&#39;s <a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/my-aadhaar" rel="noopener" target="_blank">My Aadhaar page</a>. Its links open a newer beta version of the myAadhaar portal, so labels may differ slightly.</li>
<li>Under <strong>Keep Aadhaar Secure</strong>, choose <strong>Download Masked Aadhaar</strong>.</li>
<li>Enter your Aadhaar number, Virtual ID or Enrolment ID, plus the security code if one is shown.</li>
<li>Enter the OTP sent to your registered mobile and download the PDF.</li>
<li>Open it with the password: the first four letters of your name in capitals plus your birth year. UIDAI&#39;s example is SURE1990 for SURESH KUMAR, born 1990.</li>
<li>Check that only the last four digits are visible before you send it anywhere.</li>
</ol>
<p>Masking hides digits, nothing else. Your name, photo, date of birth and address stay on the page, and the secure QR code carries the same details plus the last four digits (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-online-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). A masked copy is safer, not harmless.</p>
<p>No linked mobile? You&#39;ll have to register one at an Aadhaar Seva Kendra or enrolment centre first. The app can change an existing number but can&#39;t add one for the first time (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-app-faq" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI Aadhaar App FAQ</a>).</p>

<h2 id="how-do-i-share-aadhaar-with-the-new-aadhaar-app">How do I share Aadhaar with the new Aadhaar app?</h2>
<p>UIDAI launched its new Aadhaar app on 28 January 2026. The organisation generates a QR code, you scan it in the app, and you share only the identity fields that use case needs. UIDAI&#39;s CEO said this means verifiers get a digitally signed credential instead of storing your Aadhaar number (<a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2219790" rel="noopener" target="_blank">PIB</a>). The release lists hotel check-ins, age checks for cinema tickets, hospital visitors and gig-worker verification as uses.</p>
<p>To set it up:</p>
<ol>
<li>Install it from the links on UIDAI&#39;s FAQ page (Google Play package <code>in.gov.uidai.pehchaan</code>, or the App Store). It needs Android 9 or iOS 16 and above.</li>
<li>Choose a language, enter your Aadhaar number, pick the SIM, complete face authentication and set a 6-digit PIN (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-app-faq" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI Aadhaar App FAQ</a>).</li>
<li>At the counter, scan the organisation&#39;s QR code and read which fields it asks for. If the request goes beyond the purpose, decline and ask why.</li>
</ol>
<p>Some limits: the app doesn&#39;t work outside India or while Developer options are switched on, and one phone can hold up to five family profiles (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-app-faq" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). UIDAI has said the older mAadhaar app will be discontinued (<a href="https://www.biometricupdate.com/202605/uidai-shifts-aadhaar-users-to-new-app-with-selective-data-sharing" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Biometric Update</a>).</p>
<p>App-based sharing only works with a registered Offline Verification Seeking Entity, and UIDAI publishes the list (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/ovse" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI OVSE</a>). Under a December 2025 amendment, places that only check a physical or electronic copy or scan the printed QR don&#39;t have to register (<a href="https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/658/aadhaar-authentication-and-offline-verification-amendment-rules" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Ikigai Law</a>), so photocopy requests won&#39;t vanish overnight.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-aadhaar-offline-e-kyc-and-when-should-i-use-it">What is Aadhaar offline e-KYC, and when should I use it?</h2>
<p>Offline e-KYC is a password-protected ZIP holding an XML file signed by UIDAI. Instead of your Aadhaar number, it carries a reference ID made of the last four digits and a timestamp, along with your name, address, photo, gender, date of birth, and hashed mobile and email (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-paperless-offline-e-kyc" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). The receiver can check the signature to confirm nothing was edited, without calling UIDAI.</p>
<p>UIDAI&#39;s steps (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-online-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Aadhaar Online Services</a>):</p>
<ol>
<li>Go to <code>myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in/offline-ekyc</code>, or download it from the Aadhaar app.</li>
<li>Enter your Aadhaar number or VID and the security code, then request an OTP.</li>
<li>Enter the OTP, choose the details to include if asked, set a share code (the ZIP&#39;s password), and download.</li>
<li>Send the ZIP and the share code separately: the file by email, the code by phone call, for example.</li>
</ol>
<p>On the same page, UIDAI says service providers must not share, publish or display the XML, the share code or its contents, and can face action under the Aadhaar Act if they do.</p>
<h2 id="can-i-share-aadhaar-through-digilocker-instead">Can I share Aadhaar through DigiLocker instead?</h2>
<p>Yes, when the organisation requests documents through DigiLocker. Once you link Aadhaar to DigiLocker, the digital Aadhaar in your Issued Documents is the same as UIDAI&#39;s e-Aadhaar (<a href="https://www.digilocker.gov.in/web/about/faq" rel="noopener" target="_blank">DigiLocker FAQ</a>). Organisations must register with DigiLocker and ask for your explicit consent, and DigiLocker says all sharing and access is logged and reported to you, with a record in your Activity section. That log is the real benefit over a photocopy, which leaves no trail at all.</p>
<h2 id="if-you-have-to-give-a-photocopy-mark-it">If you have to give a photocopy, mark it</h2>
<p>Some offices still want paper. Print the masked version, then write on it in pen:</p>
<ul>
<li>who it is for and why (&quot;For PG rental agreement with R. Sharma only&quot;)</li>
<li>the date</li>
<li>your signature</li>
</ul>
<p>Let the writing run into the printed area so it can&#39;t be cropped out, without covering details the office needs. Hand over one copy and take back extras. If you printed at a shop, read our guide on <a href="/blog/print-shop-document-privacy/">print shop document privacy</a>. For a soft copy, add the same line as a watermark using <a href="/blog/how-to-watermark-a-pdf-before-sharing/">these PDF watermark steps</a>.</p>
<p>The note also records what you agreed to. The Aadhaar Act bars an organisation that verifies you from using your identity information beyond the purposes it told you in writing (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/images/Aadhaar_Act_2016_as_amended.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">section 29(3)</a>), so asking &quot;what is this for?&quot; is fair.</p>
<h2 id="what-about-pan-card-copies">What about PAN card copies?</h2>
<p>Use the same habits. Mark every PAN copy with purpose and date, never post it in a group, and don&#39;t attach PAN and Aadhaar together unless both are genuinely required. Section 139AA of the Income-tax Act, 1961 made you quote Aadhaar when applying for PAN and filing returns, and the Supreme Court upheld it in 2018 (<a href="https://indiankanoon.org/doc/127517806/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Puttaswamy judgment</a>). That Act was <a href="https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/objective-and-scope-new-act" rel="noopener" target="_blank">repealed on 1 April 2026</a>, when the Income-tax Act, 2025 took over, so check the current rule on the e-filing portal. Either way, treat the two as one identity set. When PAN sharing is fine, and how to spot misuse, is in <a href="/blog/is-it-safe-to-share-pan-card/">is it safe to share your PAN card?</a></p>
<h2 id="lock-your-biometrics-and-check-who-used-your-aadhaar">Lock your biometrics and check who used your Aadhaar</h2>
<p><strong>Biometric lock.</strong> Locking blocks fingerprint, iris and face authentication; any attempt returns error code 330 (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-online-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). It does not cover OTP or demographic authentication. You can lift it temporarily, say before a fingerprint check at a ration shop, through UIDAI&#39;s website, the Aadhaar app, an enrolment centre or an Aadhaar Seva Kendra. A registered mobile is required.</p>
<p><strong>Full Aadhaar lock.</strong> This stops biometric, demographic and OTP authentication with your Aadhaar number. You need a 16-digit Virtual ID before you can lock, and your latest VID to unlock (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/aadhaar-online-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI</a>). Most people only need the biometric lock; the full lock is for suspected misuse.</p>
<p><strong>Authentication history.</strong> The Aadhaar app lets you view your authentication history (<a href="https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2219790" rel="noopener" target="_blank">PIB</a>). UIDAI also emails you every time a biometric or OTP authentication request arrives, if your email is registered (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/en/authentication" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI Authentication FAQ</a>). If you see one you didn&#39;t make, contact UIDAI on 1947 or <a href="mailto:help@uidai.gov.in">help@uidai.gov.in</a>, using the details in that email.</p>

<h2 id="who-can-legally-ask-for-your-aadhaar">Who can legally ask for your Aadhaar?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Government schemes.</strong> Aadhaar can be required for subsidies, benefits and services paid from the Consolidated Fund, but if you don&#39;t have one you must be offered another way to identify yourself (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/images/Aadhaar_Act_2016_as_amended.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">section 7</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Income tax.</strong> Section 139AA of the Income-tax Act, 1961 required Aadhaar in PAN applications and tax returns. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced that Act on 1 April 2026.</li>
<li><strong>Banks and telecom.</strong> They may verify you with Aadhaar, but you can choose a passport or other notified document, and no one can be denied service for not having Aadhaar (<a href="https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-aadhaar-and-other-laws-amendment-bill-2019" rel="noopener" target="_blank">PRS on the 2019 amendment</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Schools.</strong> The Supreme Court said Aadhaar can&#39;t be compulsory for school admission (<a href="https://indiankanoon.org/doc/127517806/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Puttaswamy</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Private companies.</strong> In 2018 the Court struck down the part of section 57 that let companies and individuals seek Aadhaar authentication (<a href="https://indiankanoon.org/doc/127517806/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Puttaswamy</a>). Since then, rule changes have let some public and private organisations, such as hotels, housing societies and workplaces, verify Aadhaar offline. Privacy groups such as Rethink Aadhaar argue this risks bringing back private-sector use the Court barred (<a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/09/india-makes-aadhaar-more-ubiquitous-but-critics-say-privacy-concerns-remain/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TechCrunch</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Hotels and other offline verifiers.</strong> They must take your consent and tell you what they collect, how they&#39;ll use it and what the alternatives are. They must not collect, use or store your Aadhaar number or biometrics (<a href="https://uidai.gov.in/images/Aadhaar_Act_2016_as_amended.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">section 8A</a>). Verifying without consent, or using your information beyond the verification, can mean up to three years in jail, a fine of up to ₹10,000 (₹1 lakh for a company), or both (section 40).</li>
</ul>
<p>At a counter, &quot;Will a masked copy or the app&#39;s QR do?&quot; usually works better than quoting section numbers.</p>
<h2 id="where-no-sus-fits">Where NO SUS fits</h2>
<p>When someone insists on a soft copy, a NO SUS <a href="/guides/#watermark">watermarked link</a> stamps a watermark with the email the viewer enters, shows access history, and lets you set expiry or view limits and revoke the link. Revoking stops new access through the link; it can&#39;t recall a downloaded file, screenshot or print. Watermarked links aren&#39;t end-to-end encrypted, and a watermark is an attribution clue, not proof of who leaked a file. For a quick send with no account on either side, a <a href="/guides/#burn">Burn File</a> link is encrypted in your browser and opens once. The recipient can still save what they opened, so send the masked version either way.</p>
