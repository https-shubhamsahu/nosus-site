39:T2a9d,<p>On 27 September 2026, the Central Bureau of Investigation <a href="https://cbi.gov.in/press-detail/ODAzMg==" rel="noopener" target="_blank">arrested two more men</a> in a Gujarat digital arrest scam. The victim was kept under a fake &quot;digital arrest&quot; for three months and made to transfer Rs 11.42 crore. With that, the CBI says it has searched 142 locations in 22 states and arrested 17 people across four digital-arrest cases in 30 days, under a nationwide drive called Operation Chakra-VI.</p>
<h2 id="what-did-the-cbi-find-in-operation-chakra-vi">What did the CBI find in Operation Chakra-VI?</h2>
<p>The drive escalated on 4 September 2026, when the CBI <a href="https://cbi.gov.in/press-detail/Nzk5Mg==" rel="noopener" target="_blank">searched 89 locations in 20 states</a> in three cases, acting on Supreme Court directions. The court had <a href="https://www.barandbench.com/news/supreme-court-orders-cbi-probe-into-digital-arrest-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ordered a CBI probe into digital arrest scams</a> in December 2025, and <a href="https://www.newsgram.com/india/2026/09/05/cbi-operation-chakra-vi-digital-arrest-scam-crackdown" rel="noopener" target="_blank">NewsGram reports</a> that Chakra-VI began on 25 June 2026.</p>
<p>The three cases, per the CBI:</p>

<p>That adds up to Rs 42.36 crore. In the first two, the callers posed as police officers.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.ssbcrack.com/2026/09/cbi-searches-89-locations-across-20-states-in-massive-crackdown-on-digital-arrest-syndicates-three-arrested.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SSBCrack describes</a> the three people arrested that day as part of the fraud&#39;s money-movement side, not necessarily the callers. According to the CBI, one man received Rs 1.95 crore in an account he had opened and sent it onward the same day, Rs 1.5 crore went into a Kolkata firm&#39;s account, and a third accused withdrew some in cash and moved the rest.</p>
<p>Two days later the CBI <a href="https://cbi.gov.in/press-detail/Nzk5Mw==" rel="noopener" target="_blank">arrested five more</a>, alleging that three of them installed a malicious APK on an account holder&#39;s phone to intercept bank SMS alerts and secretly operate the account.</p>
<p>The CBI&#39;s latest update, on 27 September, covers a separate Gujarat case: searches at 22 locations in 10 states and two arrests. The callers posed as police and TRAI officials. The CBI says Rs 2.5 crore passed through one company&#39;s bank account and that the two men used APK files to siphon off proceeds. <a href="https://the420.in/cbi-11-42-crore-digital-arrest-22-searches-two-arrested/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">The420 says</a> the victim is reported to be a 73-year-old retired teacher from Ahmedabad; <a href="https://www.etvbharat.com/amp/en/state/two-more-held-by-cbi-in-digital-arrest-case-in-gujarat-enn26092702924" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ETV Bharat</a> also reported the arrests. None of these allegations has been tested in court yet.</p>
<h2 id="what-happens-to-people-whose-accounts-receive-scam-money">What happens to people whose accounts receive scam money?</h2>
<p>On 10 October 2026, <a href="https://the420.in/india-cyber-tech-brief-10-october-2026-malware-fraud-ai-security/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">The420 reported</a> two cases that show where the risk lands for account holders. Both details below come from that single report.</p>
<ul>
<li>The Bombay High Court refused anticipatory bail to a man whose account received about Rs 4.03 crore of the Rs 7.17 crore a doctor lost in a digital arrest fraud. He said he had shared his bank details for a charity donation and his phone was hacked. Justice R. M. Joshi found too little evidence for the hacking claim and said custodial questioning was needed to trace the network.</li>
<li>In Katwa, West Bengal, police arrested a former private tutor whose account was linked to money from 195 cyber fraud cases, including digital arrests, fake job offers and investment scams. Investigators are checking whether he knew how it was being used.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="how-does-a-digital-arrest-scam-work">How does a digital arrest scam work?</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>The hook.</strong> A caller posing as police, CBI, customs, RBI or a telecom official says your Aadhaar, SIM or bank account is tied to money laundering or drugs (<a href="https://the420.in/cbi-11-42-crore-digital-arrest-22-searches-two-arrested/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">The420</a>). I4C&#39;s account of the scam, <a href="https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/pm-modi-warns-of-digital-arrest-scam-heres-what-it-is-and-how-to-stay-safe-451952-2024-10-29" rel="noopener" target="_blank">summarised by Business Today</a>, says scammers gather your personal details in advance to sound credible.</li>
<li><strong>The proof.</strong> Fake uniforms and staged backgrounds on video calls (<a href="https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/pm-modi-warns-of-digital-arrest-scam-heres-what-it-is-and-how-to-stay-safe-451952-2024-10-29" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Business Today</a>). Fake notices circulate too: the <a href="https://cybercrime.gov.in/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">national cybercrime portal</a> carries its own alert about fake emails posing as I4C, Intelligence Bureau and Delhi Police officials.</li>
<li><strong>The isolation.</strong> You are kept on long video calls and discouraged from contacting family (<a href="https://www.ssbcrack.com/2026/09/cbi-searches-89-locations-across-20-states-in-massive-crackdown-on-digital-arrest-syndicates-three-arrested.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SSBCrack</a>). In the CBI&#39;s cases this lasted one to three months.</li>
<li><strong>The payment.</strong> Money is demanded for &quot;verification&quot; of your bank accounts, bail or security deposits (<a href="https://www.ssbcrack.com/2026/09/cbi-searches-89-locations-across-20-states-in-massive-crackdown-on-digital-arrest-syndicates-three-arrested.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SSBCrack</a>).</li>
<li><strong>The app.</strong> Some gangs send APK files over WhatsApp or SMS disguised as traffic challans, bank updates or KYC documents, which can then read your SMS and notifications (<a href="https://the420.in/cbi-11-42-crore-digital-arrest-22-searches-two-arrested/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">The420</a>).</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="why-students-and-young-professionals-should-pay-attention">Why students and young professionals should pay attention</h2>
<p>The victim in the Rs 11.42 crore case is reported to be 73, but nothing in the script depends on age. It works on anyone whose ID details are already out there.</p>
<p>There is usually no public record of where a gang got a victim&#39;s details, but every Aadhaar photocopy left at a print shop, PAN photo sent on WhatsApp, or ID scan uploaded for a hostel or internship is a copy you no longer control. When a stranger reads out your real Aadhaar number, the fake case feels real.</p>
<p>The second risk is the money side. If anyone asks to use your bank account, for a commission, a favour or a &quot;donation&quot;, remember that every arrest the CBI announced on 4 September was of someone whose account moved the money. The account is in your name, so you are the first person police can trace, and &quot;my phone was hacked&quot; did not get the Bombay account holder bail.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-do-if-you-get-a-digital-arrest-call">What to do if you get a &quot;digital arrest&quot; call</h2>
<ol data-checklist>
<li><strong>Know the rule.</strong> India has <a href="https://the420.in/cbi-11-42-crore-digital-arrest-22-searches-two-arrested/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">no legal process called digital arrest</a>. The CBI says <a href="https://cbi.gov.in/press-detail/ODAzMg==" rel="noopener" target="_blank">no police, CBI or other government agency</a> places anyone under digital arrest or demands money over a phone or video call. PM Modi made a similar point in his <a href="https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/pm-modi-warns-of-digital-arrest-scam-heres-what-it-is-and-how-to-stay-safe-451952-2024-10-29" rel="noopener" target="_blank">October 2024 Mann Ki Baat address</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Hang up, then verify yourself.</strong> Call the agency or your bank on the number from its official website, never the number the caller gave or one printed on a document they sent.</li>
<li><strong>Tell someone right away.</strong> A parent, friend or flatmate. The scam depends on keeping you alone and quiet.</li>
<li><strong>Share nothing, install nothing.</strong> No Aadhaar, PAN or passport photos, no screen sharing, and no APK or &quot;case file&quot; app they send.</li>
<li><strong>If money has moved, call 1930 now.</strong> Or file at <a href="https://cybercrime.gov.in/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">cybercrime.gov.in</a>, the two channels the <a href="https://cbi.gov.in/press-detail/ODAzMg==" rel="noopener" target="_blank">CBI&#39;s advisory</a> points to, and call your bank&#39;s official helpline. Speed matters: in one CBI case, Rs 1.95 crore left the receiving account the day it arrived.</li>
<li><strong>Report the number on Chakshu.</strong> <a href="https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Chakshu on Sanchar Saathi</a> takes reports of suspected fraud calls, SMS and WhatsApp messages, including impersonation of government officials. It is for reporting, not getting money back.</li>
<li><strong>Never lend or rent your bank account.</strong> Routing others&#39; money makes you part of the laundering chain.</li>
</ol>

A watermark is a clue, not proof of who leaked it, and revoking cannot recall copies already downloaded, screenshotted or printed.</p>
