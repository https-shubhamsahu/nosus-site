The short answer

Privnote is reasonably safe for a one-off note if you are on the real site. Its privacy policy says the link, which holds the decryption key, is generated in your browser and never sent to Privnote, so it cannot read notes. The real risks sit around the service: fake lookalike sites, someone opening your link before the reader does, and the reader copying the note. Type privnote.com yourself.

How Privnote protects a note

According to Privnote's privacy policy, the link to your note is generated in your browser and never sent to Privnote as such. The decryption key is part of that link, and only the link ties the key to the note. Privnote says that means nobody at the company, including its administrators, can read a note, and that someone who got into its database would find only encrypted text.

By default a note is deleted the first time it is opened. In "Show options" you can set a timer instead, and then the note stays readable until the time is up, however many people open it. The FAQ says unread notes are deleted after 30 days. The policy also says Privnote does not log IP addresses.

Privnote has run since 2008, according to KrebsOnSecurity. That is a long record for a free tool.

What you have to take on trust

Three things in the sources are worth knowing before you paste something sensitive.

  • You cannot check the encryption. The policy says the link, with the key in it, is made in your browser, but the pages we read link to no source code. With any website that encrypts for you, you are trusting the code it serves you right now.
  • The policy is old. The privacy policy we read says it was last modified on 25 May 2018. That is not a flaw by itself, but it means the text has not been refreshed in years.
  • There are ads. The policy says third-party cookies are used for promotional purposes, so the page runs ads. Treat any ad on a privacy site with the same care as a search result.

The real danger: fake Privnote sites

Most of the reported harm has come from copies of Privnote, not from Privnote.

In June 2020, KrebsOnSecurity reported a clone called Privnotes.com. It had been running for about a year, ranked second in Google for "privnote", and was also promoted through a paid ad. A script swapped bitcoin addresses inside notes for the operator's own. The real Privnote's owners told Krebs the clone did not implement the encryption properly, so it could read and change notes. ITPro covered the same story.

In April 2024 Krebs reported more clones. One of them, privnote.co, was flagged as phishing, and its operator threatened a lawsuit. Four addresses swapped into notes on that site moved nearly $18,000 in cryptocurrency between 15 and 19 March 2024. Krebs named other lookalikes, including tornote.io (fifth in Google for "privnote" at the time), privatemessage.net and privnode.com.

The trick works because a self-destructing note hides its own evidence. The sender cannot go back and see what the reader received. A site only has to alter the one thing that matters, such as a wallet address or an account number.

A self-destructing note is only as safe as the address you typed and the channel you sent the link on.

How to check you are on the real one

  1. Type the address yourself. Enter privnote.com in the address bar, or use a bookmark. Do not click a search result or an ad.
  2. Read the spelling. One swapped letter is enough. Krebs's list includes names that differ from the real one by a single character.
  3. Treat the link like the secret. Anyone holding the full link can open the note first. If your reader says "already read" and it was not them, assume someone else saw it and change whatever the note contained.
  4. Do not put the whole secret in one place. Send the link in one app and say any extra detail aloud. For passwords, our guide to sending a password securely shows the split-channel method.
  5. Confirm money details another way. If a note contains a wallet address or bank account, check it with a call or a different app before sending anything.

Do not open the link to test it. The FAQ confirms that opening it destroys the note.

What Privnote cannot protect

Privnote's FAQ is honest here: a reader can copy the text, take a screenshot or remember it, and Privnote does not try to stop that. No browser tool can. If the reader is the problem, a note will not help.

When to use something else

Privnote does one job: short text, one reader, no account. It also does extras that some tools lack: timed notes, optional passwords and an email when the note is read.

Look elsewhere when you need to send files, when you want source code you can read or self-host, or when you want a different trade-off. Questions people ask

Is Privnote safe?

For a one-off note, mostly yes, if you are on the real site. Privnote's privacy policy says the link, which holds the decryption key, is generated in your browser and that Privnote never has it. The main danger is fake lookalike sites, so type privnote.com yourself and send the link over a channel you trust.

Is Privnote secure and encrypted?

Privnote says each note is encrypted and that the decryption key exists only in the link, which it never receives. You cannot verify that yourself, because the pages we read link to no source code. You are trusting the code its site serves you.

Is Privnote still safe in 2026?

The service still runs and its policy still says the same thing, but that policy was last modified in May 2018. Fake clones have been reported since 2020 and again in 2024. The real service has no reported breach in the sources we checked; the reported harm came from lookalike sites.

Can Privnote read my notes?

By its own account, no. It says the key sits only in the link, so even someone who copied its database could not read the notes. A fake site is different: a clone can read and change whatever you type into it.

Is privnote.co the real Privnote?

No. KrebsOnSecurity reported in 2024 that privnote.co swapped cryptocurrency addresses inside notes for the scammers' own. The real service is privnote.com. Spellings like privnotes.com, privnode.com and privnate.com were also reported as fakes.

What is a safer alternative to Privnote?

It depends on the job. For files, a source-available tool you can self-host or an app that sends files as one-time links does what Privnote cannot. Our alternatives page lists the main options and where each is better or worse.
