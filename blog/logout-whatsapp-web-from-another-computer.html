How to Log Out of WhatsApp Web on Another Computer From Your Phone

By the NO SUS team · 12 October 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer

Open WhatsApp on your phone and go to Linked devices (Android: the three-dot menu; iPhone: Settings or your profile picture). Every computer, browser and tablet using your account is listed there; tap one and choose Log out. If you see one you don't recognise, log it out, then turn on two-step verification and app lock.

If you have ever scanned a QR code on a college lab PC, a cyber cafe computer or the Xerox shop near campus, your WhatsApp may still be open there. You can fix it from your own phone without going back.

How do I check if my WhatsApp is open on another computer?

Every browser, computer, tablet or second phone that can read your chats shows up in one list on your primary phone, called Linked devices. WhatsApp lets you link up to four devices at a time, including WhatsApp Web, the Windows and Mac apps, Android tablets, iPads, watches and companion phones.

  • Android: open WhatsApp, tap the three-dot menu (⋮) at the top right, then Linked devices.
  • iPhone: open WhatsApp, tap Settings or your profile picture, then Linked devices.

That is the path in WhatsApp's own instructions as of October 2026. If the list is empty, nothing else is connected. If you see a browser or computer you don't remember linking, log it out.

WhatsApp says it can't tell you who accessed your account, or when and from where, so this list is the main record you have. Check it regularly, and keep WhatsApp notifications on: WhatsApp says you can tap a linked-device notification to review and remove a device you don't recognise.

How to log out of WhatsApp Web from your phone

On Android

  1. Open WhatsApp and tap ⋮ > Linked devices.
  2. Tap the computer or browser you want to remove.
  3. Tap Log out.

On iPhone

  1. Open WhatsApp and tap Settings or your profile picture.
  2. Tap Linked devices, then tap the device.
  3. Tap Log out, then Log out again to confirm.

WhatsApp's account-check guide says to repeat these steps for each linked device, so remove every session you don't recognise. If you are still at the computer, its unlink guide says you can log out there instead: in WhatsApp Web or Desktop, open Settings > Log out and confirm.

Logging out a linked device does not touch the chats on your phone. WhatsApp notes that your chat history stays visible on your primary device.

How WhatsApp linked devices work now

WhatsApp Web no longer needs your phone online, which is exactly why a forgotten session matters.

QuestionWhat WhatsApp says (as of October 2026)
Does the computer need my phone online?No. Each linked device connects to WhatsApp independently and works while your phone is off.
When does a forgotten session end?WhatsApp disconnects a linked device after 30 days of inactivity, and all linked devices log out if your phone goes unused for more than 14 days.
What does a new device get?Your phone sends an end-to-end encrypted copy of your recent message history, stored on that device.
Web or Desktop app?WhatsApp Desktop syncs more message history than WhatsApp Web.
Is a QR code the only way to link?No. WhatsApp Web and Windows can also link with your phone number and an eight-character code that you enter on your phone.

In practice: if you used WhatsApp Web at a shop last week and never logged out, that browser already holds a copy of your recent chats. A notice on WhatsApp's linking help page refers to linked devices showing up to one year of chat history, so that copy can go back a long way. Don't wait for the 30-day timer.

Linking has some guard rails: per the same page, your phone may ask for your fingerprint, face or screen-lock PIN before scanning, and WhatsApp asks you to confirm if it detects an unrecognised device. Those checks only help if the phone, and the decision, are yours.

Signs someone else is using your WhatsApp

  • An unknown entry in Linked devices. This is the clearest sign.
  • A link request you didn't start. Phone-number linking works by sending an "enter code to link new device" notification to your phone. If one arrives while you are not linking anything, someone (or a fake website) entered your number on a WhatsApp login screen. Don't act on it.
  • A registration code you didn't ask for. WhatsApp sends a push notification when someone tries to register your number. Often it is someone mistyping their own number; it can also be a takeover attempt. Never share the code.
  • "You have been logged out" on your phone when you haven't changed phones or numbers. Registering your number on a new phone logs out the old one.
  • Friends asking about messages you never sent, especially requests for money or links.

The pairing-code scam to watch for

In December 2025, security company Gen described a campaign it calls GhostPairing. A message from a contact says something like "I found your photo" with a link. The page looks like a Facebook photo viewer and asks for your phone number to "verify". It feeds that number into WhatsApp's real phone-number linking, then shows you the code and tells you to enter it in WhatsApp. If you do, the attacker's browser becomes one of your linked devices. Your phone keeps working normally, so the takeover often goes unnoticed while the attacker reads synced chats and messages your contacts.

The rule that stops this: only link a device when you are sitting at that computer and started the linking yourself. Never type a code into WhatsApp because a website, a caller or a "friend" asked you to. With phone-number linking, WhatsApp also shows the name and location of the device asking to link; if it isn't the computer in front of you, don't confirm.

My WhatsApp was taken over: what to do

If a stranger's computer is linked but WhatsApp still works on your phone, log that device out (steps above), then turn on two-step verification and app lock.

If your number was registered on someone else's phone, follow WhatsApp's recovery steps:

  1. Open WhatsApp on your phone, tap Log back in, and re-register with the 6-digit code sent to your number by SMS or call.
  2. Re-registering automatically logs every other device out of your account, including the intruder's.
  3. If WhatsApp asks for a two-step verification PIN or password you never set, the intruder may have turned it on. Without it, WhatsApp says to wait 7 days and try again.
  4. Tell family and friends, because the intruder can impersonate you in chats and groups.
  5. Try to recover the account before reporting it. WhatsApp warns that reporting it may get the account banned.

Recovery needs a working SIM with your number, so if your SIM was lost or stolen, get a replacement from your operator first.

Turn on two-step verification (now a password)

WhatsApp's help centre says it is replacing the old six-digit PIN with a password of at least 8 characters, with at least one letter and one number. If your app still shows a PIN, it may not have the change yet; turn on the PIN anyway.

  1. Android: tap ⋮ > Settings > Account > Two-step verification. iPhone: tap You > Account > Two-step verification.
  2. Turn it on and create your password.
  3. Add an email address you can access. If you forget the password, WhatsApp can verify you another way, including by email.
  4. Optionally tap Trust this device for your own phone.

Those are the current steps as of October 2026. Two-step verification protects against someone registering your number on a new phone. It does not undo a linked session that was approved from your own phone, which is why the Linked devices check still matters.

Add app lock and chat lock

App lock means someone who picks up your unlocked phone can't open WhatsApp and link a computer to it in a few seconds. Per WhatsApp's app lock guide:

  • Android: ⋮ > Settings > Privacy > App lock, then turn on Unlock with biometric. Your phone needs a fingerprint or face unlock set up first.
  • iPhone: Settings or your profile picture > Privacy > App Lock, then turn on Require Face ID (or Touch ID).
  • WhatsApp Web: Settings > Privacy > App lock, then set a password of 6 to 128 characters. In a Windows browser, Ctrl+Alt+L locks it on the spot.

Chat lock goes one step further for specific chats. Locked chats move into a separate folder that opens only with your phone passcode, fingerprint, face or a secret code you set, and notifications stop showing the sender's name. On a phone, tap and hold the chat, then ⋮ > Lock chat. On WhatsApp Web and Windows, right-click the chat and choose Lock chat.

Used WhatsApp Web at a print shop? Do this

If you must use WhatsApp Web at a print shop to get a PDF onto its computer, keep the session short.

  1. On the QR screen, untick Stay logged in on this browser before you scan. WhatsApp says the session then logs out automatically when you close the tab or browser.
  2. Open only the chat you need, download the one file, and print it.
  3. In WhatsApp Web, open Settings > Log out, then close the browser.
  4. Delete the downloaded file from the computer's Downloads folder, then remove it from the Recycle Bin too. Logging out ends the WhatsApp session; it doesn't remove files the browser already saved.
  5. On your phone, open Linked devices and confirm the shop computer is gone.

Already left the shop? Log it out from your phone now using the steps above. For the wider risks of shared PCs, read is it safe to log in on a cyber cafe computer and our guide to print shop document privacy.

Questions people ask

Can someone read my WhatsApp on a computer if my phone is off?

Yes. Linked devices connect to WhatsApp on their own and keep working while your phone is offline. A session ends when it is logged out, when that device is inactive for 30 days, or when your phone goes unused for more than 14 days.

Does logging out of WhatsApp Web from my phone delete my chats?

No. Logging out removes that computer's access, and your chat history stays on your primary phone. It also does not delete files that were already downloaded to that computer, so clean those up separately.

Can WhatsApp tell me who used my account or where they logged in?

No. WhatsApp says it cannot provide information about who accessed your account or the time and location of access. Your Linked devices list is the main record you have, so check it regularly.

Will changing my two-step verification password remove a hacker?

No. WhatsApp says the only way to remove someone who has taken over your account is to re-register your number with the 6-digit code sent by SMS or call, which logs out every other device. Turn on two-step verification after that.

How many devices can be linked to one WhatsApp account?

As of October 2026, up to four linked devices at a time alongside your primary phone. These can be browsers, the Windows or Mac app, tablets, watches or a companion phone.
