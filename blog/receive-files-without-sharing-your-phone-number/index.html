The short answer

Give strangers something other than your WhatsApp number: a masked email address for small files, or a Dropbox file request link that takes files up to 2 GB from people without an account. Telegram and Signal usernames work when the sender already uses those apps, while WhatsApp usernames are on hold in India as of October 2026.

The tutor wants to send notes. A recruiter wants your signed offer letter back. An OLX buyer says they will send a payment screenshot. Each asks for your WhatsApp number first, and once they have it, they keep it. Here is how to get the file without that trade, and where each method falls short.

Why your phone number is worth guarding in India

In India a mobile number works more like an ID than a contact detail.

  • It can reveal your name and face. On Google Pay, if "Allow people to find you" is switched on, people who have your number or email can find your name and photo across Google services (Google Pay Help). Caller-ID apps like Truecaller build their directory from the contact lists their users upload (Wikipedia), so your number may already carry your name even if you never installed the app.
  • It is a permanent line to you. You can't take a number back. The buyer or the coaching centre can keep messaging long after the deal is done, or pass the number on.
  • It is a key to your accounts. SIM-swap fraud works because one-time passwords sent by SMS go to whoever controls the number. A fraudster gets your number moved to a SIM they hold, receives your codes and resets your accounts (Wikipedia). The fewer strangers who can pair your number with your name, the smaller that target.

The government treats the number as an identifier too, from the other side. When WhatsApp announced usernames, the IT ministry's stated worry was that first contact without a visible phone number could make fraud and impersonation easier (The Register).

Option 1: Use a masked email address for small files

Email is normal for recruiters, colleges and offices, and you don't have to give out your main inbox.

  1. Create a forwarding address. DuckDuckGo Email Protection is free and lets you create unlimited private addresses that forward to Gmail, Outlook, Proton or another inbox you already use (DuckDuckGo). Firefox Relay gives everyone five masks for free (Firefox Relay).
  2. Use one address per context. One for job applications, one for the flat hunt, one for marketplace deals. If one starts attracting spam, you know who leaked it.
  3. Mind the size. Personal Gmail accounts can send attachments up to 25 MB, and Gmail turns anything bigger into a Google Drive link (Gmail Help), so large files from Gmail senders arrive as links. Firefox Relay does not forward emails larger than 10 MB, and replying from a mask needs Relay Premium, which is not sold in India as of October 2026 (Firefox Relay FAQ).

If a file arrives as a cloud link that asks you to request access, check which Google account you are requesting from before you click.

Option 2: Send a file request link

A file request is an upload-only link. The sender drops files in; they can't browse your folder. It suits a print shop scanning a stack of pages, a class collecting assignments, or anyone gathering documents from several people (see how to collect documents securely).

Dropbox (works on the free plan)

  1. Sign in at dropbox.com and click File requests in the left sidebar.
  2. Click New request, add a title, and add a password if you want one.
  3. Click Create and copy the link.

The sender doesn't need a Dropbox account, though they may be asked for a name and email so you know who uploaded (Dropbox Help). On Basic, Plus and Family plans each file can be up to 2 GB. Uploads use your own storage, so if your Dropbox is full the sender gets an error. Deadlines need a Professional, Essentials or team plan. When you have what you need, close the request so the link stops accepting uploads (Dropbox Help).

OneDrive and Google Forms

OneDrive's Request files lets anyone with the link upload without seeing the folder or who else uploaded. Microsoft says it isn't available for OneDrive for home, so it only helps if your college or employer account has it switched on. Names typed by senders who aren't signed in are not verified (Microsoft Support).

Google's nearest option is a Google Form with a file upload question. Files land in a new folder in your Drive and you can cap the file type, count and size, but every sender must sign in to a Google Account (Google Docs Editors Help). Fine for classmates, awkward for a shopkeeper.

Anyone holding a request link can upload to it, so send it only to the person you expect files from. Treat a surprise .apk or .exe "document" as a red flag and delete it.

Option 3: Use a Telegram or Signal username (and where WhatsApp stands)

WhatsApp usernames are on hold in India

WhatsApp announced usernames on 29 June 2026. They show in place of your number when you message someone new, there is no directory to search, and an optional username key adds a second secret a stranger must know (WhatsApp Blog). You would still need a phone number to register (WhatsApp).

In India, the IT ministry wrote to WhatsApp on 1 July (The Register) telling it not to roll the feature out until consultations conclude to the government's satisfaction (MediaNama). Meta said it would hold off (Republic World), and as of 6 October neither side had announced clearance (MediaNama). Don't count on it for now.

Telegram

Set a username in Settings and share your t.me/username link. By default only people you've saved as contacts see your number; tighten this under Settings > Privacy and Security > Phone Number. Anyone who already has your number saved still sees it, and a public username appears in Telegram's global search, so strangers can message you through it. Files can be up to 2 GB each (4 GB with Premium). Regular cloud chats use server-client encryption; only secret chats are end-to-end encrypted (Telegram FAQ).

Signal

Signal hides your number from anyone who doesn't already have it saved. Create a username under Settings > Profile (it must end in two or more digits) and share it as a link or QR code. There is no searchable username directory. To stop people reaching you by number at all, set Settings > Privacy > Phone Number > Who can find me by my number to Nobody (Signal).

MediaNama reports that the IT ministry sent Telegram and Signal similar notices on 3 July, and it reported no outcome (MediaNama). Both apps also need the sender to install them, which rules them out for most one-off deals.

What to do with tutors, buyers, recruiters and classmates

  • Tutor or coaching class: a masked email for PDFs, or a Dropbox request if a whole batch is coming.
  • OLX or Marketplace buyer: keep the conversation inside the marketplace chat. You rarely need a file from a buyer at all. Confirm payment in your own bank or UPI app, never from a screenshot.
  • Recruiter: put a job-hunt email mask on your CV and share your number once an interview is actually scheduled.
  • Print shop or cyber cafe: ask them to email scans to a mask instead of WhatsApping them. See the print shop privacy guide, and how to share an Aadhaar copy safely if ID is involved.
  • Classmates and event teams: a Google Form or Dropbox request for collecting from many people; Telegram or Signal for ongoing chat.

What if my number is already out there?

  1. Hide your name on Google Pay. Tap your profile picture, then Settings > Privacy and Security > How people find you on Google Pay, and switch off Allow people to find you. Google notes that with it off, people not already connected to you can't find you, and receiving money takes extra steps (Google Pay Help).
  2. Use a UPI number that isn't your phone number. A UPI number is a bank-verified 8 to 10 digit ID that can be your phone number or a number you choose, so you can give buyers a chosen one instead (Google Pay Help).
  3. Check SIMs in your name. Sanchar Saathi's "Know Mobile Connections in Your Name" lists numbers registered to you and lets you report ones you don't recognise (Sanchar Saathi).
  4. Report abuse. Use Chakshu on Sanchar Saathi for suspected fraud calls, SMS and WhatsApp messages. If money is already gone, call 1930 or file at the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal.
  5. Make SMS codes less important. Work through a Google account security checkup so your main account doesn't depend on your number alone.

Questions people ask

Are WhatsApp usernames available in India?

Not as of October 2026. WhatsApp announced usernames on 29 June 2026, but India's IT ministry wrote to WhatsApp on 1 July telling it not to launch the feature in India until consultations conclude. As of early October, neither side had publicly announced clearance.

Can I receive files on Telegram without showing my phone number?

Yes. Set a username, then limit who sees your number under Settings > Privacy and Security > Phone Number. People who already have your number saved will still see it, and a public username can be found through Telegram's global search.

Does the sender need a Dropbox account to upload to a file request?

No. Dropbox says you don't need an account to respond to a file request, though the sender may be asked for a name and email. On free Basic accounts each file can be up to 2 GB, and uploads use your own Dropbox storage.

How do I stop people finding my name from my number on Google Pay?

In Google Pay, tap your profile picture, go to Settings > Privacy and Security > How people find you on Google Pay, and switch off Allow people to find you. Google says that with this on, people who have your number or email can find your name and photo.

Where do I report a stranger who keeps messaging me after I shared my number?

Block them in the app first. Suspected fraud calls, SMS and WhatsApp messages can be reported on Chakshu in the government's Sanchar Saathi portal; if you have already lost money, call the 1930 cybercrime helpline.
