How to Share an Aadhaar Copy Safely (and Your PAN) in 2026

By the NO SUS team · 12 October 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer

Don't hand over a plain, full-number photocopy. As of October 2026 the safer options are UIDAI's new Aadhaar app, which shares only the fields a QR request asks for, a masked e-Aadhaar that shows just the last four digits, an offline e-KYC file, or DigiLocker. Mark any copy you do give with its purpose and date, then lock your biometrics and check your authentication history.

Your Aadhaar doesn't need to be hacked to leak. A stack of photocopies in a hostel office, a PDF forwarded around a WhatsApp group, or a scan left on a print-shop computer is enough. UIDAI says your number alone can't hurt you, but it still advises people not to post it on public platforms (UIDAI). The Aadhaar Act goes further: an Aadhaar number, demographic details or photo collected under the Act must not be published, displayed or posted publicly except where regulations allow (section 29(4)).

Here's how to share as little as possible with the official tools available in October 2026.

How do I download a masked Aadhaar in 2026?

A masked Aadhaar replaces the first eight digits with "xxxx-xxxx", so only the last four show (UIDAI). It is still a digitally signed e-Aadhaar, and UIDAI says e-Aadhaar is as valid as the physical letter for all purposes. The download is free but needs an OTP on your Aadhaar-linked mobile (UIDAI My Aadhaar).

  1. Open UIDAI's My Aadhaar page. Its links open a newer beta version of the myAadhaar portal, so labels may differ slightly.
  2. Under Keep Aadhaar Secure, choose Download Masked Aadhaar.
  3. Enter your Aadhaar number, Virtual ID or Enrolment ID, plus the security code if one is shown.
  4. Enter the OTP sent to your registered mobile and download the PDF.
  5. Open it with the password: the first four letters of your name in capitals plus your birth year. UIDAI's example is SURE1990 for SURESH KUMAR, born 1990.
  6. Check that only the last four digits are visible before you send it anywhere.

Masking hides digits, nothing else. Your name, photo, date of birth and address stay on the page, and the secure QR code carries the same details plus the last four digits (UIDAI). A masked copy is safer, not harmless.

No linked mobile? You'll have to register one at an Aadhaar Seva Kendra or enrolment centre first. The app can change an existing number but can't add one for the first time (UIDAI Aadhaar App FAQ).

How do I share Aadhaar with the new Aadhaar app?

UIDAI launched its new Aadhaar app on 28 January 2026. The organisation generates a QR code, you scan it in the app, and you share only the identity fields that use case needs. UIDAI's CEO said this means verifiers get a digitally signed credential instead of storing your Aadhaar number (PIB). The release lists hotel check-ins, age checks for cinema tickets, hospital visitors and gig-worker verification as uses.

To set it up:

  1. Install it from the links on UIDAI's FAQ page (Google Play package in.gov.uidai.pehchaan, or the App Store). It needs Android 9 or iOS 16 and above.
  2. Choose a language, enter your Aadhaar number, pick the SIM, complete face authentication and set a 6-digit PIN (UIDAI Aadhaar App FAQ).
  3. At the counter, scan the organisation's QR code and read which fields it asks for. If the request goes beyond the purpose, decline and ask why.

Some limits: the app doesn't work outside India or while Developer options are switched on, and one phone can hold up to five family profiles (UIDAI). UIDAI has said the older mAadhaar app will be discontinued (Biometric Update).

App-based sharing only works with a registered Offline Verification Seeking Entity, and UIDAI publishes the list (UIDAI OVSE). Under a December 2025 amendment, places that only check a physical or electronic copy or scan the printed QR don't have to register (Ikigai Law), so photocopy requests won't vanish overnight.

What is Aadhaar offline e-KYC, and when should I use it?

Offline e-KYC is a password-protected ZIP holding an XML file signed by UIDAI. Instead of your Aadhaar number, it carries a reference ID made of the last four digits and a timestamp, along with your name, address, photo, gender, date of birth, and hashed mobile and email (UIDAI). The receiver can check the signature to confirm nothing was edited, without calling UIDAI.

UIDAI's steps (Aadhaar Online Services):

  1. Go to myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in/offline-ekyc, or download it from the Aadhaar app.
  2. Enter your Aadhaar number or VID and the security code, then request an OTP.
  3. Enter the OTP, choose the details to include if asked, set a share code (the ZIP's password), and download.
  4. Send the ZIP and the share code separately: the file by email, the code by phone call, for example.

On the same page, UIDAI says service providers must not share, publish or display the XML, the share code or its contents, and can face action under the Aadhaar Act if they do.

Can I share Aadhaar through DigiLocker instead?

Yes, when the organisation requests documents through DigiLocker. Once you link Aadhaar to DigiLocker, the digital Aadhaar in your Issued Documents is the same as UIDAI's e-Aadhaar (DigiLocker FAQ). Organisations must register with DigiLocker and ask for your explicit consent, and DigiLocker says all sharing and access is logged and reported to you, with a record in your Activity section. That log is the real benefit over a photocopy, which leaves no trail at all.

If you have to give a photocopy, mark it

Some offices still want paper. Print the masked version, then write on it in pen:

  • who it is for and why ("For PG rental agreement with R. Sharma only")
  • the date
  • your signature

Let the writing run into the printed area so it can't be cropped out, without covering details the office needs. Hand over one copy and take back extras. If you printed at a shop, read our guide on print shop document privacy. For a soft copy, add the same line as a watermark using these PDF watermark steps.

The note also records what you agreed to. The Aadhaar Act bars an organisation that verifies you from using your identity information beyond the purposes it told you in writing (section 29(3)), so asking "what is this for?" is fair.

What about PAN card copies?

Use the same habits. Mark every PAN copy with purpose and date, never post it in a group, and don't attach PAN and Aadhaar together unless both are genuinely required. Section 139AA of the Income Tax Act requires you to quote Aadhaar when applying for PAN and filing returns, and the Supreme Court upheld it in 2018 (Puttaswamy judgment), so treat the two as one identity set.

Lock your biometrics and check who used your Aadhaar

Biometric lock. Locking blocks fingerprint, iris and face authentication; any attempt returns error code 330 (UIDAI). It does not cover OTP or demographic authentication. You can lift it temporarily, say before a fingerprint check at a ration shop, through UIDAI's website, the Aadhaar app, an enrolment centre or an Aadhaar Seva Kendra. A registered mobile is required.

Full Aadhaar lock. This stops biometric, demographic and OTP authentication with your Aadhaar number. You need a 16-digit Virtual ID before you can lock, and your latest VID to unlock (UIDAI). Most people only need the biometric lock; the full lock is for suspected misuse.

Authentication history. The Aadhaar app lets you view your authentication history (PIB). UIDAI also emails you every time a biometric or OTP authentication request arrives, if your email is registered (UIDAI Authentication FAQ). If you see one you didn't make, contact UIDAI on 1947 or help@uidai.gov.in, using the details in that email.

Who can legally ask for your Aadhaar?

  • Government schemes. Aadhaar can be required for subsidies, benefits and services paid from the Consolidated Fund, but if you don't have one you must be offered another way to identify yourself (section 7).
  • Income tax. You must quote Aadhaar in PAN applications and tax returns under section 139AA.
  • Banks and telecom. They may verify you with Aadhaar, but you can choose a passport or other notified document, and no one can be denied service for not having Aadhaar (PRS on the 2019 amendment).
  • Schools. The Supreme Court said Aadhaar can't be compulsory for school admission (Puttaswamy).
  • Private companies. In 2018 the Court struck down the part of section 57 that let companies and individuals seek Aadhaar authentication (Puttaswamy). Since then, rule changes have let some public and private organisations, such as hotels, housing societies and workplaces, verify Aadhaar offline. Privacy groups such as Rethink Aadhaar argue this risks bringing back private-sector use the Court barred (TechCrunch).
  • Hotels and other offline verifiers. They must take your consent and tell you what they collect, how they'll use it and what the alternatives are. They must not collect, use or store your Aadhaar number or biometrics (section 8A). Verifying without consent, or using your information beyond the verification, can mean up to three years in jail, a fine of up to ₹10,000 (₹1 lakh for a company), or both (section 40).

At a counter, "Will a masked copy or the app's QR do?" usually works better than quoting section numbers.

Questions people ask

Is a masked Aadhaar valid as ID proof?

A masked Aadhaar is a digitally signed e-Aadhaar downloaded from UIDAI, and UIDAI says e-Aadhaar is as valid as the physical Aadhaar letter. Some organisations may still ask for the full number; ask them why they need all 12 digits before you agree.

Can someone misuse my Aadhaar with just a photocopy?

UIDAI says knowing your Aadhaar number alone is not enough to harm you. A copy still exposes your name, photo, date of birth and address, so lock your biometrics, mark every copy with its purpose and avoid leaving spare copies around.

Is it legal for a hotel to keep a copy of my Aadhaar?

Under the Aadhaar Act, an entity doing offline verification must take your consent, tell you the purpose and the alternatives, and must not collect, use or store your Aadhaar number or biometrics. Offer the Aadhaar app's QR sharing or a masked copy so no full number is kept.

Does locking my biometrics stop OTP-based Aadhaar use?

No. The biometric lock covers fingerprint, iris and face authentication only. Blocking OTP and demographic authentication as well needs the full Aadhaar lock, so keep your Aadhaar-linked SIM secure and never share an Aadhaar OTP.

What is an Aadhaar Virtual ID and where can I use it?

A Virtual ID (VID) is a revocable 16-digit number mapped to your Aadhaar that can be used instead of the Aadhaar number for authentication and e-KYC. Agencies should not store it. You can generate it on UIDAI's website, in the Aadhaar app, or by texting GVID plus the last 4 digits of your Aadhaar to 1947 from your registered mobile.
