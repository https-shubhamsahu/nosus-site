The short answer

For a conversation, Signal has the most honest disappearing messages and Telegram secret chats are a close second. For a single photo, WhatsApp view once is the easiest. For a password or file that should never sit in a chat, use a one-time link tool: Yopass for open code, Privnote for the simplest note, NO SUS Burn for files up to 50 MB with no account. None of them can stop a camera pointed at the screen.

The best self-destructing option depends on what you are sending. For a conversation, use Signal or a Telegram secret chat. For one photo, WhatsApp view once is the easiest. For a password or file that should not sit in a chat at all, use a one-time link tool such as Yopass, Privnote or NO SUS Burn. None of them can stop a camera.

We read each product's own help pages, docs or code on 16 October 2026. Where a page was unclear, we left the claim out.

Chat apps versus link tools

A chat app deletes messages inside a conversation. A link tool deletes a single secret behind a link, and the chat only ever holds the link. If you are sending a password, an OTP or an Aadhaar scan, the second kind leaves less behind. If you are just talking, the first kind is the right tool. Our guide on how to send a password securely covers the second case in detail.

Signal: the most honest timer

Signal's help page lets anyone in the chat set a timer, with a custom time of up to 4 weeks. For a message you send, the timer starts when you send it. For one you receive, it starts after you read it. The message is then deleted from disk.

Signal also says plainly that this is for tidiness, not for a contact who is your adversary, because they can photograph the screen with another camera. Its Screen Security setting blocks screenshots on your own device only.

Better than the others at: honesty and read-based timers. Weaker: both people need Signal.

WhatsApp: disappearing messages and view once

WhatsApp's disappearing messages run for 24 hours, 7 days or 90 days. A message can be kept, forwarded to a chat without a timer, or quoted in a reply, and a backup made earlier still holds it. A notification preview can also outlast it.

View once is stricter for photos, videos and voice messages: they cannot be forwarded, saved or replayed, and WhatsApp says its screenshot blocking covers photos and videos. WhatsApp also admits that someone can film the screen with another device, and that a phone's own screen recorder may capture the audio of a view once voice note. We cover this in can WhatsApp view once be screenshotted. A PDF cannot be sent as view once at all; see view once PDF.

Better than the others at: reach. Everyone already has it. Weaker: no way to see whether anyone captured anything.

Telegram: secret chats only

In secret chats, messages are end-to-end encrypted, cannot be forwarded, and live only on the devices that started the chat. The self-destruct timer starts when the recipient sees the message. Telegram tries to warn you about screenshots but says it cannot reliably detect them on some devices. Ordinary Telegram chats are stored encrypted in its cloud and use client-server encryption, not end-to-end.

Since March 2026, Premium users can switch on Disable Sharing in a one-to-one chat, which blocks forwarding, screenshots and media saving. Better at: secret chats are strong. Weaker: you must remember to start one.

Snapchat: only if your friends are already there

Snapchat deletes Chats 24 hours after everyone has viewed them, or 31 days after sending. Saving, replying or reacting keeps a message longer, and Snapchat itself says recipients can always capture content with a screenshot or another camera. It is a social app first. We would not use it for anything sensitive.

Privnote: the simplest note

Privnote's privacy policy says the link is generated in your browser and is never sent to Privnote, and notes are deleted after the first read by default. Its FAQ says unread notes are removed after 30 days and that it cannot stop a recipient copying or capturing a note. Text only. See our NO SUS vs Privnote comparison for more.

Onetime Secret: open code, different encryption

Onetime Secret encrypts secrets on its servers. With a passphrase it stores only a bcrypt hash, so it cannot decrypt the secret afterwards. Without an account, a secret lasts up to 7 days and 100 KB. A free account raises that to 14 days and 1,000 KB. The code is public. More in the Onetime Secret alternatives page.

Yopass: best if you want to inspect or self-host

Yopass encrypts in the browser with OpenPGP, puts the key after the # in the link, needs no sign-up and is Apache 2.0 licensed. Its README warns that the public demo is for trying it out and that you should host your own for sensitive information. See Yopass alternatives.

NO SUS Burn: one entry, with honest limits

NO SUS Burn works on nosus.foo with no account for sender or reader. A note can hold up to 50,000 characters, or you can send up to 10 files, 50 MB combined, as one link. It is encrypted in your browser with AES-256 and opens once. Unread notes expire after 7 days, and files after 1 hour, 24 hours or 7 days.

Where it is weaker than the others on this list:

  • If you tap More, then Use a code instead, you get a 6-digit code that needs no link and opens once. A copy of the key then sits on our server until up to 10 minutes after the code is used or expires. The plain link keeps the key after the #, which browsers don't send to our server.
  • It is new and closed source, so it has no long track record and nobody outside can audit the code.
  • A browser cannot block screenshots, so a reader can still capture it.
  • It has no custom passwords or read alerts.

Where it fits: files and PDFs without sign-up. Try it on the homepage or read about one-time file links.

Which should you use?

  • Daily chat with someone privacy-minded: Signal.
  • A photo to someone on WhatsApp: view once.
  • A sensitive conversation on Telegram: a secret chat, not a normal one.
  • A password or OTP: Yopass or Privnote, link in one app and any extra detail in another.
  • A scanned document or PDF: NO SUS Burn, or Yopass for small files on an instance you trust. Check each tool's file limit first.
  • Anything you cannot afford to have copied: do not send it. The tools in the disappearing messages comparison agree on that.

A self-destructing tool deletes your copy and the link, but it cannot delete what the other person already saw.

Questions people ask

What is the best app for self-destructing messages?

Signal, if both people can install it: its timer runs up to 4 weeks, and on the recipient's phone it starts only after they read the message. If your contact is on WhatsApp, its disappearing messages and view once are good enough for tidiness. Pick by who you are talking to, not by features.

Is there an app where messages delete after being read?

Yes. Signal starts a received message's timer after it is read, Telegram secret chats start the timer when the message appears on the recipient's screen, and link tools such as Privnote, Yopass and NO SUS Burn delete the note after its first open.

Can you send a self-destructing message without an app?

Yes. Privnote, Yopass and NO SUS Burn work in a browser with no sign-up. You paste the text, get a link, and send that link through any chat or email. The link stops working after it is opened once.

Do self-destructing messages stop screenshots?

Not reliably. WhatsApp blocks screenshots of view once photos and videos, and Telegram's Disable Sharing blocks them for Premium users, but every app admits a second phone can photograph the screen. Signal, Snapchat and the link tools only reduce what is left behind.

Which self-destructing tool is the most private?

For link tools, Yopass encrypts in the browser and never receives the key, Privnote says it never receives the key, and Yopass publishes its code. For chat, Signal and Telegram secret chats are end-to-end encrypted, while ordinary Telegram chats use client-server encryption and are stored encrypted in Telegram's cloud, so they are not end-to-end encrypted.
