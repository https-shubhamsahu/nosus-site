<h2 id="chat-apps-versus-link-tools">Chat apps versus link tools</h2>
<p>A chat app deletes messages inside a conversation. A link tool deletes a single secret behind a link, and the chat only ever holds the link. If you are sending a password, an OTP or an Aadhaar scan, the second kind leaves less behind. If you are just talking, the first kind is the right tool. Our guide on <a href="/blog/how-to-send-a-password-securely/">how to send a password securely</a> covers the second case in detail.</p>
<h2 id="signal-the-most-honest-timer">Signal: the most honest timer</h2>
<p>Signal&#39;s <a href="https://support.signal.org/hc/en-us/articles/360007320771-Set-and-manage-disappearing-messages" rel="noopener" target="_blank">help page</a> lets anyone in the chat set a timer, with a custom time of up to 4 weeks. For a message you send, the timer starts when you send it. For one you receive, it starts after you read it. The message is then deleted from disk.</p>
<p>Signal also says plainly that this is for tidiness, not for a contact who is your adversary, because they can photograph the screen with another camera. Its <a href="https://support.signal.org/hc/en-us/articles/360043469312-Screen-Security" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Screen Security</a> setting blocks screenshots on your own device only.</p>
<p>Better than the others at: honesty and read-based timers. Weaker: both people need Signal.</p>
<h2 id="whatsapp-disappearing-messages-and-view-once">WhatsApp: disappearing messages and view once</h2>
<p>WhatsApp&#39;s <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/673193694148537/?locale=en_US" rel="noopener" target="_blank">disappearing messages</a> run for 24 hours, 7 days or 90 days. A message can be kept, forwarded to a chat without a timer, or quoted in a reply, and a backup made earlier still holds it. A notification preview can also outlast it.</p>
<p><a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1077018839582332/?locale=en_US" rel="noopener" target="_blank">View once</a> is stricter for photos, videos and voice messages: they cannot be forwarded, saved or replayed, and WhatsApp says its screenshot blocking covers photos and videos. WhatsApp also admits that someone can film the screen with another device, and that a phone&#39;s own screen recorder may capture the audio of a view once voice note. We cover this in <a href="/blog/can-whatsapp-view-once-be-screenshotted-or-saved/">can WhatsApp view once be screenshotted</a>. A PDF cannot be sent as view once at all; see <a href="/whatsapp-view-once-pdf/">view once PDF</a>.</p>
<p>Better than the others at: reach. Everyone already has it. Weaker: no way to see whether anyone captured anything.</p>
<h2 id="telegram-secret-chats-only">Telegram: secret chats only</h2>
<p>In <a href="https://telegram.org/faq" rel="noopener" target="_blank">secret chats</a>, messages are end-to-end encrypted, cannot be forwarded, and live only on the devices that started the chat. The self-destruct timer starts when the recipient sees the message. Telegram tries to warn you about screenshots but says it cannot reliably detect them on some devices. Ordinary Telegram chats are stored encrypted in its cloud and use client-server encryption, not end-to-end.</p>
<p>Since March 2026, Premium users can switch on <a href="https://telegram.org/blog/member-tags-disable-sharing-and-more" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Disable Sharing</a> in a one-to-one chat, which blocks forwarding, screenshots and media saving. Better at: secret chats are strong. Weaker: you must remember to start one.</p>
<h2 id="snapchat-only-if-your-friends-are-already-there">Snapchat: only if your friends are already there</h2>
<p><a href="https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-us/articles/7012334940948-When-does-Snapchat-delete-Snaps-and-Chats" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Snapchat</a> deletes Chats 24 hours after everyone has viewed them, or 31 days after sending. Saving, replying or reacting keeps a message longer, and Snapchat itself says recipients can always capture content with a screenshot or another camera. It is a social app first. We would not use it for anything sensitive.</p>
<h2 id="privnote-the-simplest-note">Privnote: the simplest note</h2>
<p>Privnote&#39;s <a href="https://privnote.com/info/privacy" rel="noopener" target="_blank">privacy policy</a> says the link is generated in your browser and is never sent to Privnote, and notes are deleted after the first read by default. Its <a href="https://privnote.com/info/faq" rel="noopener" target="_blank">FAQ</a> says unread notes are removed after 30 days and that it cannot stop a recipient copying or capturing a note. Text only. See our <a href="/compare/privnote/">NO SUS vs Privnote comparison</a> for more.</p>
<h2 id="onetime-secret-open-code-different-encryption">Onetime Secret: open code, different encryption</h2>
<p><a href="https://onetimesecret.com/en/about" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Onetime Secret</a> encrypts secrets on its servers. With a passphrase it stores only a bcrypt hash, so it cannot decrypt the secret afterwards. Without an account, a secret lasts up to 7 days and 100 KB. A free account raises that to 14 days and 1,000 KB. The code is public. More in the <a href="/alternatives/onetimesecret/">Onetime Secret alternatives page</a>.</p>
<h2 id="yopass-best-if-you-want-to-inspect-or-self-host">Yopass: best if you want to inspect or self-host</h2>
<p><a href="https://github.com/jhaals/yopass" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Yopass</a> encrypts in the browser with OpenPGP, puts the key after the <code>#</code> in the link, needs no sign-up and is Apache 2.0 licensed. Its README warns that the public demo is for trying it out and that you should host your own for sensitive information. See <a href="/alternatives/yopass/">Yopass alternatives</a>.</p>
<h2 id="no-sus-burn-one-entry-with-honest-limits">NO SUS Burn: one entry, with honest limits</h2>
<p>NO SUS Burn works on nosus.foo with no account for sender or reader. A note can hold up to 50,000 characters, or you can send up to 10 files, 50 MB combined, as one link. It is encrypted in your browser with AES-256 and opens once. Unread notes expire after 7 days, and files after 1 hour, 24 hours or 7 days.</p>
<p>Where it is weaker than the others on this list:</p>
<ul>
<li>A single note or file gets a 2-digit code that works only with its link. If you tap Use a code instead, you get a 6-digit code that needs no link and opens once, for up to 10 minutes. While either code is valid (20 or 10 minutes by default), the key is also held on our server.</li>
<li>It is new and closed source, so it has no long track record and nobody outside can audit the code.</li>
<li>A browser cannot block screenshots, so a reader can still capture it.</li>
<li>It has no custom passwords or read alerts.</li>
</ul>
<p>Where it fits: files and PDFs without sign-up. Try it on the <a href="/#try">homepage</a> or read about <a href="/one-time-file-link/">one-time file links</a>.</p>
<h2 id="which-should-you-use">Which should you use?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Daily chat with someone privacy-minded:</strong> Signal.</li>
<li><strong>A photo to someone on WhatsApp:</strong> view once.</li>
<li><strong>A sensitive conversation on Telegram:</strong> a secret chat, not a normal one.</li>
<li><strong>A password or OTP:</strong> Yopass or Privnote, link in one app and any extra detail in another.</li>
<li><strong>A scanned document or PDF:</strong> NO SUS Burn, or Yopass for small files on an instance you trust. Check each tool&#39;s file limit first.</li>
<li><strong>Anything you cannot afford to have copied:</strong> do not send it. The tools in the <a href="/blog/do-disappearing-messages-really-disappear/">disappearing messages comparison</a> agree on that.</li>
</ul>
<p>A self-destructing tool deletes your copy and the link, but it cannot delete what the other person already saw.</p>
