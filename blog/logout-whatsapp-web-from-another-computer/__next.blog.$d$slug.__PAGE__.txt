36:T38ed,<p>If you have ever scanned a QR code on a college lab PC, a cyber cafe computer or the Xerox shop near campus, your WhatsApp may still be open there. You can fix it from your own phone without going back.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-i-check-if-my-whatsapp-is-open-on-another-computer">How do I check if my WhatsApp is open on another computer?</h2>
<p>Every browser, computer, tablet or second phone that can read your chats shows up in one list on your primary phone, called <strong>Linked devices</strong>. WhatsApp lets you <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/378279804439436/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">link up to four devices at a time</a>, including WhatsApp Web, the Windows and Mac apps, Android tablets, iPads, watches and companion phones.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Android:</strong> open WhatsApp, tap the three-dot menu (⋮) at the top right, then <strong>Linked devices</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>iPhone:</strong> open WhatsApp, tap <strong>Settings</strong> or your profile picture, then <strong>Linked devices</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<p>That is the path in <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1428782138011916/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">WhatsApp&#39;s own instructions</a> as of October 2026. If the list is empty, nothing else is connected. If you see a browser or computer you don&#39;t remember linking, log it out.</p>
<p>WhatsApp says it <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1428782138011916/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">can&#39;t tell you who accessed your account, or when and from where</a>, so this list is the main record you have. Check it regularly, and keep WhatsApp notifications on: WhatsApp says you can <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/378279804439436/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">tap a linked-device notification to review and remove</a> a device you don&#39;t recognise.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-log-out-of-whatsapp-web-from-your-phone">How to log out of WhatsApp Web from your phone</h2>
<h3 id="on-android">On Android</h3>
<ol data-checklist>
<li>Open WhatsApp and tap ⋮ &gt; <strong>Linked devices</strong>.</li>
<li>Tap the computer or browser you want to remove.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Log out</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<h3 id="on-iphone">On iPhone</h3>
<ol data-checklist>
<li>Open WhatsApp and tap <strong>Settings</strong> or your profile picture.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Linked devices</strong>, then tap the device.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Log out</strong>, then <strong>Log out</strong> again to confirm.</li>
</ol>
<p>WhatsApp&#39;s <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1428782138011916/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">account-check guide</a> says to repeat these steps for each linked device, so remove every session you don&#39;t recognise. If you are still at the computer, its <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/834124628020911/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">unlink guide</a> says you can log out there instead: in WhatsApp Web or Desktop, open <strong>Settings &gt; Log out</strong> and confirm.</p>
<p>Logging out a linked device does not touch the chats on your phone. WhatsApp notes that your <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/653480766448040/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">chat history stays visible on your primary device</a>.</p>
<h2 id="how-whatsapp-linked-devices-work-now">How WhatsApp linked devices work now</h2>
<p>WhatsApp Web no longer needs your phone online, which is exactly why a forgotten session matters.</p>
<div class="table-scroll" role="region" aria-label="Table" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Question</th><th scope="col">What WhatsApp says (as of October 2026)</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><th scope="row">Does the computer need my phone online?</th><td>No. Each linked device <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/378279804439436/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">connects to WhatsApp independently</a> and works while your phone is off.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">When does a forgotten session end?</th><td>WhatsApp <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/378279804439436/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">disconnects a linked device after 30 days of inactivity</a>, and all linked devices log out if your phone goes unused for more than 14 days.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">What does a new device get?</th><td>Your phone sends an <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/653480766448040/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">end-to-end encrypted copy of your recent message history</a>, stored on that device.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Web or Desktop app?</th><td>WhatsApp Desktop <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/653480766448040/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">syncs more message history</a> than WhatsApp Web.</td></tr><tr><th scope="row">Is a QR code the only way to link?</th><td>No. WhatsApp Web and Windows can also <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1324084875126592/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">link with your phone number and an eight-character code</a> that you enter on your phone.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<p>In practice: if you used WhatsApp Web at a shop last week and never logged out, that browser already holds a copy of your recent chats. A notice on WhatsApp&#39;s <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1317564962315842/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">linking help page</a> refers to linked devices showing up to one year of chat history, so that copy can go back a long way. Don&#39;t wait for the 30-day timer.</p>
<p>Linking has some guard rails: per the same page, your phone may ask for your fingerprint, face or screen-lock PIN before scanning, and WhatsApp asks you to confirm if it detects an unrecognised device. Those checks only help if the phone, and the decision, are yours.</p>
<h2 id="signs-someone-else-is-using-your-whatsapp">Signs someone else is using your WhatsApp</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>An unknown entry in Linked devices.</strong> This is the clearest sign.</li>
<li><strong>A link request you didn&#39;t start.</strong> <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1324084875126592/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Phone-number linking</a> works by sending an &quot;enter code to link new device&quot; notification to your phone. If one arrives while you are not linking anything, someone (or a fake website) entered your number on a WhatsApp login screen. Don&#39;t act on it.</li>
<li><strong>A registration code you didn&#39;t ask for.</strong> WhatsApp <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/479314433984258/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">sends a push notification when someone tries to register your number</a>. Often it is someone mistyping their own number; it can also be a takeover attempt. Never share the code.</li>
<li><strong>&quot;You have been logged out&quot; on your phone</strong> when you haven&#39;t changed phones or numbers. Registering your number on a new phone <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/120604060995491/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">logs out the old one</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Friends asking about messages you never sent</strong>, especially requests for money or links.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="the-pairing-code-scam-to-watch-for">The pairing-code scam to watch for</h3>
<p>In December 2025, security company Gen described a campaign it calls <a href="https://www.gendigital.com/blog/insights/research/ghostpairing-whatsapp-attack" rel="noopener" target="_blank">GhostPairing</a>. A message from a contact says something like &quot;I found your photo&quot; with a link. The page looks like a Facebook photo viewer and asks for your phone number to &quot;verify&quot;. It feeds that number into WhatsApp&#39;s real phone-number linking, then shows you the code and tells you to enter it in WhatsApp. If you do, the attacker&#39;s browser becomes one of your linked devices. Your phone keeps working normally, so the takeover often goes unnoticed while the attacker reads synced chats and messages your contacts.</p>
<p>The rule that stops this: only link a device when you are sitting at that computer and started the linking yourself. Never type a code into WhatsApp because a website, a caller or a &quot;friend&quot; asked you to. With phone-number linking, WhatsApp also <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1324084875126592/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">shows the name and location of the device</a> asking to link; if it isn&#39;t the computer in front of you, don&#39;t confirm.</p>
<h2 id="my-whatsapp-was-taken-over-what-to-do">My WhatsApp was taken over: what to do</h2>
<p><strong>If a stranger&#39;s computer is linked but WhatsApp still works on your phone,</strong> log that device out (steps above), then turn on two-step verification and app lock.</p>
<p><strong>If your number was registered on someone else&#39;s phone,</strong> follow <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1131652977717250/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">WhatsApp&#39;s recovery steps</a>:</p>
<ol data-checklist>
<li>Open WhatsApp on your phone, tap <strong>Log back in</strong>, and re-register with the 6-digit code sent to your number by SMS or call.</li>
<li>Re-registering automatically logs every other device out of your account, including the intruder&#39;s.</li>
<li>If WhatsApp asks for a two-step verification PIN or password you never set, the intruder may have turned it on. Without it, WhatsApp says to wait 7 days and try again.</li>
<li>Tell family and friends, because the intruder can impersonate you in chats and groups.</li>
<li>Try to recover the account before reporting it. WhatsApp warns that reporting it may get the account banned.</li>
</ol>
<p>Recovery needs a working SIM with your number, so if your SIM was lost or stolen, get a replacement from your operator first.</p>
<h2 id="turn-on-two-step-verification-now-a-password">Turn on two-step verification (now a password)</h2>
<p>WhatsApp&#39;s help centre says it is <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/798764543324146/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">replacing the old six-digit PIN with a password</a> of at least 8 characters, with at least one letter and one number. If your app still shows a PIN, it may not have the change yet; turn on the PIN anyway.</p>
<ol data-checklist>
<li><strong>Android:</strong> tap ⋮ &gt; <strong>Settings &gt; Account &gt; Two-step verification</strong>. <strong>iPhone:</strong> tap <strong>You &gt; Account &gt; Two-step verification</strong>.</li>
<li>Turn it on and create your password.</li>
<li>Add an email address you can access. If you forget the password, WhatsApp can <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/2183055648554771/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">verify you another way</a>, including by email.</li>
<li>Optionally tap <strong>Trust this device</strong> for your own phone.</li>
</ol>
<p>Those are the <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1920866721452534/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">current steps</a> as of October 2026. Two-step verification protects against someone registering your number on a new phone. It does not undo a linked session that was approved from your own phone, which is why the Linked devices check still matters.</p>
<h2 id="add-app-lock-and-chat-lock">Add app lock and chat lock</h2>
<p>App lock means someone who picks up your unlocked phone can&#39;t open WhatsApp and link a computer to it in a few seconds. Per WhatsApp&#39;s <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1726618467788240/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">app lock guide</a>:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Android:</strong> ⋮ &gt; <strong>Settings &gt; Privacy &gt; App lock</strong>, then turn on <strong>Unlock with biometric</strong>. Your phone needs a fingerprint or face unlock set up first.</li>
<li><strong>iPhone:</strong> <strong>Settings</strong> or your profile picture &gt; <strong>Privacy &gt; App Lock</strong>, then turn on <strong>Require Face ID</strong> (or Touch ID).</li>
<li><strong>WhatsApp Web:</strong> <strong>Settings &gt; Privacy &gt; App lock</strong>, then set a password of 6 to 128 characters. In a Windows browser, Ctrl+Alt+L locks it on the spot.</li>
</ul>
<p>Chat lock goes one step further for specific chats. Locked chats move into a separate folder that opens only with your phone passcode, fingerprint, face or a <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/764072925284841/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">secret code you set</a>, and notifications stop showing the sender&#39;s name. On a phone, tap and hold the chat, then ⋮ &gt; <strong>Lock chat</strong>. On WhatsApp Web and Windows, <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/2892312017616916/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">right-click the chat and choose Lock chat</a>.</p>
<h2 id="used-whatsapp-web-at-a-print-shop-do-this">Used WhatsApp Web at a print shop? Do this</h2>
<p>If you must use WhatsApp Web at a print shop to get a PDF onto its computer, keep the session short.</p>
<ol data-checklist>
<li>On the QR screen, untick <strong>Stay logged in on this browser</strong> before you scan. WhatsApp says the session then <a href="https://faq.whatsapp.com/1428782138011916/?lang=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">logs out automatically when you close the tab or browser</a>.</li>
<li>Open only the chat you need, download the one file, and print it.</li>
<li>In WhatsApp Web, open <strong>Settings &gt; Log out</strong>, then close the browser.</li>
<li>Delete the downloaded file from the computer&#39;s Downloads folder, then remove it from the Recycle Bin too. Logging out ends the WhatsApp session; it doesn&#39;t remove files the browser already saved.</li>
<li>On your phone, open Linked devices and confirm the shop computer is gone.</li>
</ol>
<p>Already left the shop? Log it out from your phone now using the steps above. For the wider risks of shared PCs, read <a href="/blog/is-it-safe-to-log-in-on-a-cyber-cafe-computer/">is it safe to log in on a cyber cafe computer</a> and our guide to <a href="/blog/print-shop-document-privacy/">print shop document privacy</a>.</p>

The computer never receives your Google password or token, and shows only your NO SUS Chat, for the time you pick: 15 minutes, 1 hour or 8 hours. Downloads and prints can still stay on that computer, so clean up the same way.</p>
