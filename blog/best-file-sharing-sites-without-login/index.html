Best File Sharing Sites Without Login: 9 Compared (2026)

By Shubham Sahu, founder of NO SUS8 November 2026, 10 min read

The short answer

Eight of the nine file sharing sites here work with no account for sender or recipient; WeTransfer now creates one when you send. For the biggest files, transfer.it states no size limit and Send Anywhere moves 50 GB by key. Wormhole and Tresorit Send encrypt on your device. NO SUS caps at 50 MB but opens once, which suits an ID scan or signed PDF, not a video.

Eight of these nine sites let you send a file with no account for you or the person receiving it. WeTransfer is the exception: since November 2024 it won't let anyone send without an account, which it creates after an email code. For very big files, transfer.it states no size limit and Send Anywhere moves up to 50 GB with a 6-digit key. If you care who can read the file, Wormhole and Tresorit Send encrypt it on your device and keep the key in the link.

We read each service's own pricing, help and FAQ pages on 8 November 2026. Where a page didn't say something, the table says "not stated". Limits change, so check the site before you send anything big.

Quick answers:

  • Biggest file with no account: transfer.it, whose FAQ says there are no size limits. Send Anywhere's 6-digit key takes up to 50 GB.
  • Encrypted before it leaves your phone or PC: Wormhole, Tresorit Send and NO SUS.
  • No ads, in their own words: Wormhole and Filemail.
  • A link that opens once: NO SUS. Tresorit Send allows 10 downloads per file and file.io 10 downloads a day.

Each site in short

Wormhole

Wormhole's FAQ says files up to 5 GB stay on its servers for 24 hours. Between 5 GB and the 10 GB shown on its home page, it sends the file peer-to-peer, so you keep the page open until the other person finishes downloading. Its security page says files are encrypted with 128-bit AES-GCM before they leave the browser, the key sits after the # in the link, and there are no ads. No page we read mentions an account, and the home page opens straight on a file picker.

Best for: a private file up to 5 GB that the other person will download within a day. After that it's gone.

transfer.it

Transfer.it is run by MEGA. Its FAQ says you don't need an account to send or receive, there are no size limits, and a transfer expires 90 days after it is created unless you change that before uploading. The same FAQ says it uses managed encryption keys for faster downloads, and that MEGA's own cloud is the one where only you hold the keys. If you want to delete a transfer before it expires, you'll need a MEGA account.

Best for: a folder of hundreds of gigabytes, when you don't mind the service holding the keys.

Tresorit Send

Tresorit's help page lists 5 GB per upload, up to 100 files, and 10 downloads per file, with links available for 7 days. Files are end-to-end encrypted on your device, and its FAQ says the decryption key is inside the link and never stored on Tresorit's servers. You type your email address, and an admin link for revoking the share is mailed to you.

Best for: a contract or scan under 5 GB that you may want to revoke later.

Smash

Smash's pricing page says the free plan needs no sign-up, and its help center says recipients never need an account. Before a send, Smash checks the sender's email with a code. Free transfers up to 2 GB go straight through; above 2 GB they join a queue behind paying users. Transfers last 7 days by default. Smash gives the loading and download screens of the free version to advertisers, and it encrypts stored files with AES-256 with TLS in transit.

Best for: a 1 or 2 GB video when ads on the download page don't bother you.

Filemail

Filemail's help center says the free service needs no registration and allows 5 GB per transfer, 2 transfers in 24 hours, and storage for up to 7 days. It also says the free service isn't available in certain regions. Its home page says recipients don't register or install anything and lists end-to-end encryption on the Business plan, but it doesn't say how free transfers are encrypted. Another help article says it shows no ads.

Best for: an occasional file between 2 and 5 GB, with no ads.

file.io

file.io's home page says you can upload up to 4 GB with no account, and nobody needs an account to download. A link works for 3 days, each file can be downloaded up to 10 times a day, and the file is deleted after 3 days even if no one downloaded it. Files are encrypted on its servers and sent over HTTPS. It doesn't say whether it shows ads.

Best for: a quick handoff under 4 GB to a few people.

Send Anywhere

Send Anywhere's help center says you don't need to sign up. A 6-digit key sends up to 50 GB straight to the other device without storing it on the server, and the key works once within 10 minutes. Share links are listed at 10 GB for a free member and last 48 hours. On a computer the receiver can type the key on the website; on a phone, receiving by key needs the app, while a link opens in the browser. It shows ads unless you pay, and its safety page says files are encrypted in transit and when stored.

Best for: moving a big file between your own phone and PC in the next 10 minutes.

WeTransfer

WeTransfer's help center says that since November 2024 everyone sending needs an account: you enter your email, type the code it sends, and a Free account is created. Free allows 10 transfers or 3 GB in any 30 days, and uploads stay online for up to 3 days. The person receiving needs no account. Its advertising page sells full-screen ads to brands. If you're here for WeTransfer without the account step, see our WeTransfer alternative page.

Best for: people who already have a WeTransfer account and a file under 3 GB.

NO SUS

NO SUS is ours, so weigh this entry with that in mind. On nosus.foo you can send up to 10 files, 50 MB combined, as one link, with no account for you or the reader. Files are encrypted on your device, and the key stays after the # in the link, which browsers don't send to the server. The link opens once. The file is fetched and deleted only when the reader taps download, so a chat app's link preview doesn't use it up. An unopened link expires after 1 hour, 24 hours or 7 days, as you pick.

The limits: 50 MB is far smaller than anything else in this list, and that's the main reason you'd pick another site. If you tap "Use a code instead" for a 6-digit code, the key sits on our server until up to 10 minutes after the code is used or expires. NO SUS is closed source, and a browser cannot stop the reader from taking a screenshot.

Best for: a document that should open once, such as an Aadhaar scan, a bank statement or a signed PDF. If you're sending a big video, use one of the sites above. More on one-time download links.

End-to-end or encrypted on the server: why it matters

The difference that matters is who holds the key once the file is stored. Wormhole, Tresorit Send and NO SUS encrypt the file before upload and put the key in the link, so the server stores data it cannot open by itself (for NO SUS, unless you ask for a 6-digit code). Smash, file.io, Send Anywhere and WeTransfer describe encryption on their own servers, and transfer.it says it manages the keys. For a holiday video, it rarely matters. For your PAN card or a medical report, it's worth picking one of the first group. Our guide on how to send a PDF securely covers the rest.

Send a file without login on Android

You don't need an app to send from a phone. Wormhole's FAQ says it has no app yet and its website works on Android and iOS browsers, and file.io says you can send from a computer or a phone. The steps are the same on any site that uploads from the browser:

  1. Open the site in Chrome and tap the upload button.
  2. Pick the file from your phone storage or gallery.
  3. Copy the link and send it on WhatsApp, SMS or email.

If the other person will receive by Send Anywhere key on their phone, they'll need its app. A Send Anywhere link opens in their browser instead.

Send a file without login on PC

On a laptop or desktop, all nine have a web upload page. For files above 10 GB, transfer.it states no limit, and Send Anywhere's key takes 50 GB without storing the file on its server. Wormhole sends up to 10 GB, but above 5 GB your browser tab has to stay open until the download ends.

To move a file from your phone to a PC, upload it on the phone, then open the link on the PC. With Send Anywhere you can send by key from the phone and type that key on its website, with no app on the computer. Our page on how to send a file without login walks through the same steps on nosus.foo.

Send a file online without an app

Every site in the table has a web upload page. The only app requirement is receiving a Send Anywhere key on a phone. Blip, which also comes up in these searches, works the other way: its FAQ says it has no web links and you send directly to other Blip users, so you'd both need its app.

Free file sharing without login: what the free tier limits

The free tiers differ in their limits:

  • Filemail: 2 transfers in 24 hours, and not offered in every region.
  • WeTransfer: 10 transfers or 3 GB per 30 days, and you get an account whether you want one or not.
  • file.io: 10 downloads per file per day.
  • Smash: files above 2 GB wait in a queue, and the download page shows ads.
  • Tresorit Send: 10 downloads per file, for 7 days.
  • Wormhole: gone after 24 hours.
  • NO SUS: 50 MB, and it opens once.

Which one should you use?

  • A video or folder over 10 GB: transfer.it, or a Send Anywhere key if you can both act within 10 minutes.
  • A private file up to 5 GB: Wormhole if it will be downloaded within 24 hours, Tresorit Send if you'd like 7 days and the option to revoke.
  • A 2 to 5 GB file with no ads: Filemail.
  • An ID scan, payslip or signed PDF that should open once: NO SUS. Our guide on sharing an Aadhaar copy safely explains what to mask first.

Questions people ask

Which file sharing sites work without login?

Wormhole, transfer.it, Tresorit Send, Smash, Filemail, file.io, Send Anywhere and NO SUS all let you send a file with no account. Their help or FAQ pages say so, except Wormhole, which simply has no sign-in step. Smash checks the sender's email with a code and Tresorit Send asks for an email address, but neither creates an account. None of the pages we read asks the person receiving to sign up.

Does WeTransfer need an account now?

Yes, to send. WeTransfer's help center says every sender has needed an account since November 2024: you enter your email, type the code it sends, and a Free account is created. The person downloading still needs no account. Free allows 10 transfers or 3 GB in any 30 days, and uploads stay online for up to 3 days.

How can I send a large file without login?

transfer.it says there is no size limit and no account is needed to send or receive. Send Anywhere sends up to 50 GB with a 6-digit key, and Wormhole takes up to 10 GB, though above 5 GB you keep the page open until the download ends. NO SUS takes only 50 MB, so it's the wrong tool for a big video.

Which no-login file sharing site encrypts files before upload?

Wormhole and Tresorit Send say they encrypt files on your device and put the key in the link, so their servers never get it. NO SUS also encrypts on the device and keeps the key after the # in the link, unless you ask for a 6-digit code, which keeps the key on the server while the code is valid and for up to 10 minutes after. The others describe encryption in transit and on their servers.

Does file.io delete a file after one download?

No. file.io's home page says a link works for 3 days, each file can be downloaded up to 10 times a day, and the file is deleted after 3 days even if nobody downloaded it. If you need a link that works exactly once, use a tool built for that, such as a NO SUS one-time link.

How do I send a file from Android to PC without login?

Open a browser-based site such as Wormhole, file.io or nosus.foo in Chrome on the phone, pick the file and copy the link. Then open the link on the PC. With Send Anywhere you can also send by 6-digit key from the phone and type the key on its website on the PC, with no app on the computer.
