25:T2748,<h2 id="what-did-the-imdea-chatbot-study-find">What did the IMDEA chatbot study find?</h2>
<p>Researchers at IMDEA Networks Institute in Madrid tested the websites of nine consumer AI chatbots (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, Mistral&#39;s Le Chat and Meta AI) and eight of their Android apps. According to the <a href="https://jorgegarciaherrero.com/wp-content/interactivos/20260916-Prompt-like-a-butterfly-sting-like-a-tracker-%28clean%29.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">full preprint</a>, tests ran from Spain in May 2026 with health-related prompts, across guest, free and paid accounts and different cookie choices.</p>
<p>The main numbers:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Every service contacted at least one ad or tracking company</strong>: 124 third-party domains belonging to 44 organisations, 34 of them advertising or tracking firms.</li>
<li><strong>6 of 9 websites and 3 of 8 Android apps passed chat details to outside services</strong>: chat links, chat IDs, auto-generated titles, prompts or screenshots. These often travelled with identifiers that can point to a person, such as Meta&#39;s <code>_fbp</code> and TikTok&#39;s <code>_ttp</code> cookies, account IDs, or plain or hashed emails.</li>
<li><strong>Privacy settings helped less than you&#39;d hope.</strong> After non-essential cookies were rejected, 80.8% of third-party trackers stayed active. Free and paid accounts talked to largely the same third parties.</li>
</ul>
<p>The paper, &quot;Prompt like a Butterfly, Sting like a Tracker&quot;, is listed for the 2027 Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies in its <a href="https://github.com/guinucool/pbst2027" rel="noopener" target="_blank">artifact repository</a>. <a href="https://ppc.land/6-of-9-ai-chatbots-pass-chat-titles-or-links-to-trackers-imdea-finds/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">PPC Land</a> reported it on 5 October and <a href="https://www.basicthinking.de/blog/2026/10/08/datenschutz-ki-anbieter-geben-sogar-chatdaten-weiter/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">BASIC thinking</a> on 8 October. It expands the team&#39;s four-service <a href="https://leakylm.github.io/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">LeakyLM</a> release of 4 May 2026, which <a href="https://tech.yahoo.com/cybersecurity/articles/ai-chatbot-may-leaking-chats-190646856.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Decrypt covered</a>, so this is the full study, not a new leak.</p>
<h2 id="which-chatbots-sent-what-and-to-whom">Which chatbots sent what, and to whom?</h2>
<p><strong>Grok was the worst case.</strong> In the <a href="https://jorgegarciaherrero.com/wp-content/interactivos/20260916-Prompt-like-a-butterfly-sting-like-a-tracker-%28clean%29.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">study</a>, ordinary Grok chat links for free and paid users were readable by anyone by default unless you opted out, and LeakyLM found guest chats were always public. Once cookies were accepted, one Grok conversation reached seven ad and analytics services, each getting the chat&#39;s ID and title, and Meta&#39;s pixel tied it to the user&#39;s Meta identity through the <code>_fbp</code> cookie. Grok also relayed links and titles to Meta and TikTok through a server-side setup that ad blockers can&#39;t touch. When someone opened a shared Grok chat, TikTok received a screenshot of it, and Meta and TikTok both got the latest prompt.</p>
<p>The others:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Gemini</strong> sent conversation titles to Google Analytics (Google&#39;s own product, which the paper still counts as a tracker).</li>
<li><strong>ChatGPT and Claude</strong> websites sent each chat&#39;s unique ID to Datadog, a monitoring service. The ID is enough to rebuild the chat&#39;s link, though chats on both stay owner-only unless shared.</li>
<li><strong>Claude</strong>, if you accept non-essential cookies, forwards events server-to-server to eleven ad and tracking services, including Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok and Reddit. Ad blockers can&#39;t see this, but rejecting cookies stopped it in the tests.</li>
<li><strong>Perplexity</strong> sent hashed emails to the marketing firm Singular. It stopped sending chat links to Meta on 3 April 2026.</li>
<li><strong>Mistral&#39;s Le Chat</strong> sent chat titles, links and the account email to Intercom, a customer-support tool.</li>
</ul>
<p>Titles are revealing because the service writes them to summarise what you asked. In the paper&#39;s examples, a question about early Parkinson&#39;s symptoms got a title naming the condition, and a salary question got one naming the salary, the city and a mortgage range. Gemini, Grok and Mistral sent titles like these to outside firms.</p>
<h2 id="did-anyone-actually-read-the-chats">Did anyone actually read the chats?</h2>
<p>The team planted unique trap links (canary tokens) in chats and uploaded files. Only Grok showed outside access: 70 activations from 70 IP addresses in 14 countries, hours to days later, about two-thirds from the US although the tests ran in the EU. Perplexity&#39;s own crawler also fetched the links repeatedly, even when told not to. The Grok result shows links inside Grok chats were picked up by outside systems, but not by whom or why.</p>
<p>The caveats:</p>
<ul>
<li>It is a preprint, not yet formally published.</li>
<li>It doesn&#39;t show any ad company reading a conversation; the authors argue the exposure itself is the problem.</li>
<li>It was measured from Spain under EU cookie rules; the authors say other countries may differ. Nothing was tested from India.</li>
<li>Meta AI was tested on its own website and app, not inside WhatsApp or Instagram.</li>
</ul>
<p>The team alerted EU and UK data protection authorities and xAI in April 2026. The paper says that as of 10 September 2026, Grok links were still public and xAI had not replied.</p>
<h2 id="why-this-matters-if-you-use-ai-for-sops-resumes-and-loan-papers">Why this matters if you use AI for SOPs, resumes and loan papers</h2>
<p>Polishing a resume or SOP, drafting a visa or loan letter, or asking about health and money can mean pasting an Aadhaar or PAN number, phone number, address, marksheet, offer letter or salary slip into a chatbot.</p>
<p><a href="https://leakylm.github.io/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">LeakyLM</a> warns that leaking a chat&#39;s link &quot;can be equivalent to leaking the conversation itself&quot;. <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7925741-sharing-conversations-and-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt" rel="noopener" target="_blank">OpenAI&#39;s help page</a> says personal-account share links open for anyone with the link, can&#39;t be limited to specific people and have no expiry setting. It also asks users to keep health information, financial details, passwords and account numbers out of shared chats.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-do-now">What to do now</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Don&#39;t paste ID numbers, phone numbers, addresses, bank details or whole documents.</strong> Swap them for placeholders like [NAME], [PAN] and [PHONE]. If someone genuinely needs your Aadhaar, see <a href="/blog/how-to-share-aadhaar-copy-safely">how to share an Aadhaar copy safely</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Keep your opening message generic.</strong> In the paper&#39;s examples, titles were built from the question itself. Start with &quot;Help me edit a letter&quot;, not the diagnosis, bank or visa type.</li>
<li><strong>Stop using share links for anything personal, and clear old ones.</strong> In ChatGPT: Settings &gt; Data controls &gt; Shared links &gt; Manage, then delete. Deleting a conversation also removes its link, but anyone who already continued a shared chat keeps their own copy. <a href="https://leakylm.github.io/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">LeakyLM advises</a> restricting conversation access in Grok&#39;s settings, revoking Grok shared links chat by chat, and setting Perplexity threads to Private.</li>
<li><strong>Reject non-essential cookies and use a tracker-blocking browser.</strong> Firefox&#39;s <a href="https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/enhanced-tracking-protection-firefox-desktop" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Strict mode</a> blocks tracking content in every window, and the paper notes Brave blocks trackers by default. Neither stops server-to-server forwarding.</li>
<li><strong>Use temporary chats and delete old ones, but don&#39;t treat them as hidden from trackers.</strong> ChatGPT&#39;s <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8914046-temporary-chat-in-chatgpt" rel="noopener" target="_blank">temporary chats</a> stay out of history and training but may be kept for up to 30 days, and the study saw tracker disclosures in incognito modes too.</li>
<li><strong>Log out of AI accounts on lab and cyber cafe computers.</strong> An open session shows your chat history to the next user. See <a href="/blog/is-it-safe-to-log-in-on-a-cyber-cafe-computer">is it safe to log in on a cyber cafe computer</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Consider a separate email for AI sign-ups.</strong> Hashed emails were among the identifiers sent, and the <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2024/07/no-hashing-still-doesnt-make-your-data-anonymous" rel="noopener" target="_blank">US FTC says</a> hashing doesn&#39;t make data anonymous.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="where-no-sus-fits">Where NO SUS fits</h2>
<p>NO SUS can&#39;t change what chatbot websites send to trackers. It targets the habit this study punishes: long-lived links anyone holding them can open. To send someone a password, ID number or document, a <a href="/#try">Burn Note</a> is encrypted in your browser, opens once, then is gone, and expires when you choose; its direct link keeps the key in the part of the URL browsers don&#39;t send to the server. Whoever opens it first gets it. For documents that must stay viewable longer, SecureSend in the Android app adds a viewer-email watermark, expiry and revoke, but it isn&#39;t end-to-end encrypted and can&#39;t recall screenshots or downloads. See also <a href="/blog/how-to-send-a-password-securely">how to send a password securely</a>.</p>
