3b:T2303,<p>Partly. Gmail&#39;s confidential mode can cut an email off on a date you pick, Proton Mail can delete one up to 28 days after you send it, and classic Outlook&#39;s Expires after option only strikes the message through. None of them can promise to stop a screenshot, and none can stop someone photographing the screen. If the content should be gone after one read, send it as a one-time link inside the email instead.</p>
<h2 id="self-destructing-email-in-gmail-confidential-mode">Self-destructing email in Gmail (confidential mode)</h2>
<p>Gmail calls it confidential mode, and it works on the web, Android and iPhone. In a new email, turn on confidential mode, choose a date under <strong>Set expiration</strong>, and pick <strong>No SMS passcode</strong> or <strong>SMS passcode</strong> (<a href="https://support.google.com/mail/answer/7674059?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Gmail Help</a>). SMS codes work for phone numbers in India.</p>
<p>What it does:</p>
<ul>
<li>The recipient can&#39;t forward, copy, print or download the email or its attachments.</li>
<li>You can open the email in <strong>Sent</strong> and choose <strong>Remove access</strong> before it expires.</li>
<li>Gmail doesn&#39;t send the content itself. Per <a href="https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/gmail/advanced/protect-gmail-messages-with-confidential-mode" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Google&#39;s admin guide</a>, it swaps the body and attachments in the recipient&#39;s copy for a link to the content. Only the subject and that link travel to the recipient.</li>
</ul>
<p>What it doesn&#39;t do: Google&#39;s own page says recipients can still take screenshots or photos. The page never calls confidential mode end-to-end encryption. On a work or school account it may also be switched off by your admin. For PDFs specifically, see <a href="/blog/how-to-send-a-pdf-securely/">how to send a PDF securely</a>.</p>
<h2 id="self-destructing-email-in-outlook">Self-destructing email in Outlook</h2>
<p>Outlook has three settings that sound like self-destruct, and only one of them is.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Expires after</strong> (the classic Outlook desktop app: Options, Message Options, Delivery options). After the date the message <a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/set-a-message-expiration-date" rel="noopener" target="_blank">stays in the list with a strikethrough</a> and can still be opened. It is a label, not a deletion.</li>
<li><strong>Do Not Forward</strong> (needs a qualifying Microsoft 365 subscription). Recipients <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/rights-management-usage-rights" rel="noopener" target="_blank">must sign in and can&#39;t forward, print or copy</a>. Office attachments stay protected after download, but <a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/send-encrypted-messages-with-a-microsoft-365-personal-or-family-subscription" rel="noopener" target="_blank">PDF and image attachments can be downloaded without that protection</a>. People on Gmail or Yahoo get a link and sign in, or use a one-time passcode that <a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/mail/open-encrypted-and-protected-messages" rel="noopener" target="_blank">expires after 15 minutes</a>. Microsoft 365 Personal and Family subscribers have it in <a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/send-encrypted-messages-with-a-microsoft-365-personal-or-family-subscription" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Outlook.com</a> too. It doesn&#39;t expire the email.</li>
<li><strong>Advanced Message Encryption.</strong> This is the version that can <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/ome-advanced-message-encryption" rel="noopener" target="_blank">expire or revoke</a> an email, but it comes with <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/office365/servicedescriptions/microsoft-365-service-descriptions/microsoft-365-tenantlevel-services-licensing-guidance/microsoft-purview-service-description" rel="noopener" target="_blank">top business plans</a> such as Microsoft 365 E5, and works only for outside recipients who read the email in Microsoft&#39;s web portal.</li>
</ol>
<p>Microsoft is clear about the same gap as Google: its rights management <a href="https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/introduction-to-irm-for-email-messages" rel="noopener" target="_blank">can&#39;t stop a photograph or a screen capture</a>.</p>
<h2 id="self-destructing-email-in-proton-mail">Self-destructing email in Proton Mail</h2>
<p>Proton Mail has the closest thing to a real self-destructing email. In the composer, open the ⋯ menu, choose <strong>Expiration time</strong> and pick a date and time, up to <a href="https://proton.me/support/expiration" rel="noopener" target="_blank">four weeks (28 days)</a>. The clock starts when you send, not when it is read.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>To another Proton user,</strong> the email is deleted from your Sent folder and from their inbox.</li>
<li><strong>To Gmail or anyone else,</strong> it works only as a <a href="https://proton.me/support/password-protected-emails" rel="noopener" target="_blank">password-protected email</a>: tick that you are sending to a non-Proton Mail user and set a password. At expiry the content is deleted, but the notification email with the link stays in their inbox. Password-protected emails are on every plan, Proton Free included, and expire after 28 days by default.</li>
</ul>
<p>Proton&#39;s pages don&#39;t address screenshots. Like any screen, it can be photographed.</p>
