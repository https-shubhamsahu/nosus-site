39:T24c7,<p>No small business holds more sensitive personal data per person than an HR, payroll or staffing firm. One joining kit has an Aadhaar, a PAN, a bank account, a UAN, a salary, an address, often a medical form and a family nominee. Multiply that by every associate on your rolls, and a single salary sheet in the wrong WhatsApp group becomes a reportable breach under India&#39;s DPDP Act.</p>
<p>The general duties are in our <a href="/blog/dpdp-act-checklist-for-small-business/">DPDP Act checklist for small businesses</a>. This page covers what is different for employee and candidate data. It is practical guidance, not legal advice.</p>
<h2 id="when-the-duties-start">When the duties start</h2>
<p>The <a href="https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">DPDP Rules</a> were published on 13 November 2025. The duties below, and the Data Protection Board&#39;s power to fine, start 18 months later: mid-May 2027, or 13 May 2027 on a plain count (<a href="https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/267647.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">G.S.R. 843(E)</a>). There is no size threshold and no exemption for small firms has been notified.</p>
<h2 id="employees-consent-is-not-the-main-ground">Employees: consent is not the main ground</h2>
<p>Section 7(i) of the <a href="https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Act</a> lets an employer process personal data &quot;for the purposes of employment&quot; or to protect itself from loss or liability, such as preventing corporate espionage or protecting trade secrets, and to provide a benefit the employee asked for. Payroll, attendance, PF and ESI filings, appraisals and exit formalities sit comfortably here.</p>
<p>What that does not remove:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Security safeguards</strong> (section 8(5), Rule 6): encryption or masking, access control, logs kept for a year, backups.</li>
<li><strong>Breach reporting</strong> (section 8(6), Rule 7): tell the affected people and the Board without delay, with a detailed report to the Board within 72 hours.</li>
<li><strong>Erasure</strong> (section 8(7)): delete when the purpose ends, unless a law requires you to keep the record.</li>
<li><strong>A published contact person and a grievance process</strong> (Rule 9, Rule 14(3)).</li>
</ul>
<p>Some law firms read the Act as saying that no consent notice is needed for data processed on the employment ground, because section 5 ties the notice to consent requests. Neither MeitY nor the Board has confirmed that. A short privacy note in the offer letter or HR policy costs little and covers you either way.</p>
<h2 id="candidates-are-not-employees-yet">Candidates are not employees yet</h2>
<p>The Act is silent on whether shortlisting, interviews and background checks count as &quot;purposes of employment&quot;. Law firms are split (<a href="https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/employee-background-verification-and-pre-employment-screening-under-the-dpdp-act/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">King Stubb &amp; Kasiva</a>). The cautious route:</p>
<ul>
<li>Collect only what the hiring step needs. A CV and contact details for shortlisting; ID and bank details only at offer stage.</li>
<li>Before a background check, give the candidate a notice that says what will be checked, from which sources and why, and get their consent.</li>
<li>When a candidate isn&#39;t hired, the purpose is over. Delete their documents after the one-year minimum in Rule 8(3), unless a law needs them kept.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="fiduciary-or-processor-it-depends-on-the-data-set">Fiduciary or processor? It depends on the data set</h2>
<p>The Act puts the main duties on the Data Fiduciary, the party that decides why and how data is used, and lets it use a Data Processor only under a valid contract (section 8(2)). The fiduciary stays liable for what the processor does (section 8(1)).</p>

<p>The staffing case is the least settled. Commentators disagree on how the Act applies when two employers share one worker (<a href="https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/the-staffing-industry-compliance-roadmap-implementing-dpdp-act-and-labour-codes-as-a-single-system" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Bar &amp; Bench</a>). The practical answer is the same everywhere: write each party&#39;s role, for each data set, into the contract.</p>
<h2 id="the-hr-data-checklist">The HR data checklist</h2>
<ol data-checklist>
<li><strong>Map every HR data set.</strong> Candidates, employees, ex-employees, associates at clients, nominees and dependants. For each, note where copies sit: HRMS, payroll software, Excel, email, recruiters&#39; phones, printed joining kits.</li>
<li><strong>Write down the ground for each.</strong> Employment use (section 7(i)) for employees; consent with a notice for candidates and background checks; legal duty for statutory filings.</li>
<li><strong>Stop collecting what payroll doesn&#39;t need.</strong> Full bank statements, full Aadhaar numbers and medical history are rarely required. A <a href="/blog/masked-aadhaar-vs-photocopy/">masked Aadhaar</a> and a cancelled cheque usually do.</li>
<li><strong>Move documents off personal phones.</strong> Recruiters&#39; WhatsApp is the biggest leak in most firms. Give candidates one upload route instead (<a href="/blog/how-to-collect-documents-from-clients-securely/">how to collect documents securely</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Lock down access by role.</strong> Salary data visible to payroll only, documents to HR only, and access removed the day someone leaves. Turn on two-step verification and check that your systems keep access logs for a year (Rule 6).</li>
<li><strong>Put roles into every contract.</strong> Client agreements, vendor agreements and background check agency terms should say who is fiduciary and who is processor for which data, require security safeguards, and require deletion at the end.</li>
<li><strong>Set retention periods.</strong> Labour, tax and PF laws require some records to be kept; keep those for the period the law sets and no longer. Everything else goes when its purpose ends, after Rule 8(3)&#39;s one-year minimum.</li>
<li><strong>Plan for exits.</strong> When someone leaves, keep what the law and final settlement need, revoke their system access, and schedule the rest for deletion.</li>
<li><strong>Write the breach plan for HR&#39;s usual mistakes.</strong> A payslip to the wrong person, a salary sheet in a group, a lost laptop. Each one triggers Rule 7&#39;s notices. Name who calls the employee, who emails the Board and who writes the 72-hour report.</li>
<li><strong>Publish the contact person.</strong> Name the role and email that answers employees&#39; and candidates&#39; data questions, on the website and in the HR policy (Rule 9).</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="aadhaar-copies-in-joining-kits">Aadhaar copies in joining kits</h2>
<p>Collecting employee Aadhaar copies is not banned, but the <a href="https://www.uidai.gov.in/images/The_Aadhaar_Sharing_of_information_regulation_2016.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">UIDAI regulations</a> set conditions that already apply, on paper and on screen: tell the person the purpose and whether giving Aadhaar is mandatory, get consent, never display it, keep it secure and don&#39;t keep it longer than needed (regulations 5 and 6).
