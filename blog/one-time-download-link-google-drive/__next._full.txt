48:T16fe,<p>No. Google Drive has no setting that makes a link stop working after one open or one download: none of its sharing help pages, checked on 23 October 2026, describe one. Drive can limit who opens a file, end a person&#39;s access on a date (on work and school accounts) and hide the download button. If the file should be gone after one download, put it in a one-time link and send that instead.</p>
<h2 id="can-google-drive-make-a-one-time-link">Can Google Drive make a one-time link?</h2>
<p>Not built in. A Drive link keeps working for as long as its sharing settings allow. With General access set to <strong>Anyone with the link</strong>, <a href="https://support.google.com/drive/answer/7166529?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Google says</a> anyone who has the link can open the file without signing in to a Google account. Nothing in the help pages counts opens or downloads.</p>
<p>The workarounds people try are all manual:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Switch it back to Restricted after they download it.</strong> Only people you added can open it then. You have to notice the download first, and it can&#39;t reach the copy they saved.</li>
<li><strong>Delete the file afterwards.</strong> <a href="https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2494893?hl=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Google notes</a> that if you delete a shared file you own, people it was shared with can still make a copy until you delete it permanently.</li>
<li><strong>&quot;Direct download&quot; links passed around on forums.</strong> We found no Google help page that documents them, so don&#39;t count on how they behave.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="how-to-make-a-google-drive-link-expire">How to make a Google Drive link expire</h2>
<p>On eligible work or school accounts, a person&#39;s access can end on a date (<a href="https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2494893?hl=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Google Drive Help</a>):</p>
<ol>
<li>Open <strong>Share</strong> and add the person.</li>
<li>Click the arrow next to their name and choose <strong>Add expiration</strong>.</li>
<li>Pick a date within one year.</li>
</ol>
<p>The limits: personal Google accounts don&#39;t have it, folders allow it only for viewers, and Google describes it per person. Its pages describe no expiry for an Anyone with the link link. When access to a file expires, <a href="https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/13005112?hl=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Google says</a> the person falls back to whatever access they have on the folder it sits in.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-stop-people-downloading-a-drive-file">How to stop people downloading a Drive file</h2>
<p>In <strong>Share</strong>, open <strong>Settings</strong> and untick viewers and commenters under the option for who can download, copy and print (<a href="https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2494893?hl=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Google Drive Help</a>). Google adds its own caveat: the limits work only inside Drive, Docs, Sheets and Slides, and you can&#39;t stop people sharing the content in other ways. A screenshot or a phone camera still works, as it does on any website.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-send-a-one-time-download-link-instead">How to send a one-time download link instead</h2>
<p>Use a tool with a one-open limit. NO SUS deletes the files on the first download; Bitwarden Send switches the link off after the number of views you set and deletes the file on its deletion date.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bitwarden Send.</strong> Turn on <strong>Limit views</strong> and set it to 1 (<a href="https://bitwarden.com/help/create-send/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Bitwarden</a>). File Sends need Premium or a paid organization, files can be up to 500 MB (100 MB on mobile), and a Send is deleted at its deletion date, 7 days by default. Better than NO SUS for big files.</li>
<li><strong>NO SUS Burn Files.</strong> Up to 10 files, 50 MB combined, as one link, with no account for you or the person receiving it. Your browser encrypts the files before upload, and the key stays in the link after the #, which browsers do not send to our server. The page the link opens shows a button first, so a chat preview doesn&#39;t use it up. The first download does, and the files are deleted from our server. Steps: <a href="/one-time-file-link/">send a file that deletes after it is opened</a>.</li>
</ul>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>What a one-time link does not do</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>It can&#39;t take back the copy they downloaded. One-time means the link, not their copy.</li>
<li>It can&#39;t stop a screenshot or a photo of the screen.</li>
<li>Whoever opens it first uses it up. If your recipient finds it already used, assume someone else got the file and tell them.</li>
</ul>
</blockquote>
