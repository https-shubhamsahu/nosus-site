Lost money to a scam? Do this in the next 5 days.

  • Money left your account after a call, an OTP, a fake job, a trading app or a wrong-number UPI request.
  • The first days decide two things: whether the receiving account gets frozen, and whether the bank's zero-liability rules apply to you.
  • Both depend on reporting in the right places, in writing, with the right IDs. The order is below.

Free steps, in this order

  1. 1

    Step 1: Call 1930 now, then file on cybercrime.gov.in

    1930 is the national cybercrime helpline. The earlier the call, the better the chance that the money is still sitting in the receiving account and can be put on hold. Then file the same case on cybercrime.gov.in with every transaction ID (UTR), the time, the number or UPI ID you paid, and screenshots. Write down the acknowledgement number. From 1 January 2027 this report, together with the one to your bank, is what makes the zero-liability rule apply.1: National Cybercrime Reporting Portal and the 1930 helpline (opens in a new tab)2: RBI: framework for customer liability and compensation in digital payment frauds, in force from 1 January 2027 (opens in a new tab)

    cybercrime.gov.in (opens in a new tab)

  2. 2

    Step 2: Tell your bank in writing today

    Call the bank's fraud line to block the card or UPI, then send the written complaint the same day: email, the app's complaint form, or a letter at the branch with a stamped copy for you. Ask for the complaint number. RBI's 2017 circular gives you zero liability for an unauthorised transaction reported within 3 working days, and capped liability within 4 to 7 days. After that the bank's own policy decides. Banks count from the day you were told, so the date on your complaint matters.3: RBI circular, 6 July 2017: customer protection and limiting liability in unauthorised electronic banking transactions (opens in a new tab)

  3. 3

    Step 3: Raise the UPI or card dispute where you paid

    In the UPI app (PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm, BHIM or your bank's app), open the payment and choose Raise a dispute or Report a problem. Keep the ticket number. If the app does not resolve it, the same dispute goes to your bank, then to NPCI's portal. For a card payment, ask the bank for a chargeback in writing. Every ID from these steps goes into your evidence log.4: NPCI: UPI dispute redressal mechanism (opens in a new tab)

    npci.org.in dispute redressal (opens in a new tab)

  4. 4

    Step 4: Cut off what the scammer can still use

    Change your UPI PIN and the passwords on email and banking. Report the fraud number or message on Sanchar Saathi's Chakshu. Check Know Your Mobile Connections (TAFCOP) for SIMs on your Aadhaar that are not yours. If you shared an Aadhaar or PAN photo, lock your Aadhaar biometrics and pull your free credit reports in a month: the ID-misuse steps cover that.5: Sanchar Saathi: Chakshu (report a fraud call or message) and Know Your Mobile Connections (opens in a new tab)

    sancharsaathi.gov.in (opens in a new tab)

  5. 5

    Step 5: Police complaint, then escalate on day 30

    Take the cybercrime acknowledgement and the bank complaint to your police station or cyber cell and ask for an FIR; a Zero FIR can be registered at any station. Keep the copy. If the bank has not resolved your complaint in 30 days, or you disagree with its reply, complain to the RBI Integrated Ombudsman on cms.rbi.org.in. Under the 2026 scheme you have 90 days from the end of that wait or from the bank's last reply, whichever is later.6: RBI: Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 FAQs (in force from 1 July 2026; time limits in Q17) (opens in a new tab)

    cms.rbi.org.in (opens in a new tab)

What this page does not promise

  • No letter gets your money back. Between April 2021 and November 2025 about 0.3% of reported losses were returned to victims (₹167 crore of ₹52,969 crore, MHA SOP of 2 January 2026). Reporting fast raises the odds of a hold; it does not promise one.

Questions people ask

I lost money to a scam. What should I do first?

Call 1930 and file on cybercrime.gov.in within the hour, then tell your bank in writing the same day. These two reports are what the bank's liability rules turn on, and the early call is the only chance of a hold on the receiving account. The steps above are in that order.

Can I get my money back after a UPI scam?

Sometimes, if the money is still in the receiving account when it is put on hold, or if the bank accepts the transaction as unauthorised under RBI's liability rules. Most victims do not get it back: about 0.3% of reported losses were returned between 2021 and 2025. Report fast and keep every ID; that is what you can control.

What is the 5-day rule from 1 January 2027?

RBI's framework in force from 1 January 2027 ties zero liability and compensation to reporting within 5 days to both your bank and the 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in system. Until then the 2017 circular applies: 3 working days for zero liability on unauthorised transactions.

Someone offers to recover my money for a fee. Is that real?

No. Recovery agents, unfreeze fees and anyone asking for an OTP or a second payment to release funds are a second scam aimed at the same victims. Only your bank, the Ombudsman and the courts can move money back, and none of them charge you to do it.

The bank is not replying. Where do I complain?

Wait 30 days from your written complaint. Then file with the RBI Integrated Ombudsman on cms.rbi.org.in. You have 90 days from the end of the 30-day wait or from the bank's last reply, whichever is later. Keep your first complaint and every reply.
